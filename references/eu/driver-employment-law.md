## Overview

Professional drivers in the EU are subject to a layered framework of employment legislation at both the EU and national level. The **EU Mobility Package** (adopted 2020) significantly reshaped the social dimension of road transport, introducing specific posting rules for drivers and amending working time enforcement. This document covers the principal EU-level employment directives affecting professional drivers and the contractual obligations of transport operators as employers.

---

## Directive 2019/1152 — Transparent and Predictable Working Conditions

This Directive, which member states were required to implement by 1 August 2022, establishes minimum rights for workers in all sectors including road transport.

### Key Rights for Drivers

| Right | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| Written statement of employment | Employer must provide written terms within **7 days** of start date for core information (identity of parties, place of work, job title, start date, remuneration, working hours) |
| Maximum trial period | **6 months** maximum probationary/trial period (member states may allow longer for management roles or where it is in the worker's interest) |
| Parallel employment | Employer **cannot prohibit** a driver from taking employment with other employers outside scheduled working hours; non-compete clauses for parallel employment are void unless justified by a legitimate business reason |
| Predictability of work | For workers with fully or mostly unpredictable schedules: employer must inform the driver of work assignments within a **reasonable advance notice period** set by national law; driver has right to refuse assignments outside guaranteed hours/days without adverse consequences |
| Transition to more stable employment | Workers may request transition to a more predictable form of employment after 6 months service; employer must respond in writing within 1 month |
| Mandatory training | Where employer requires training by law or collective agreement, training must be provided free of charge and counted as working time |

### Practical Implications for Transport Operators

- Drivers engaged on zero-hours or call-in contracts must receive advance notice of shifts within the period set by national law.
- Standard employment contracts must be issued promptly — not merely at or after the end of the probation period.
- Many transport operators use collective agreements that exceed the Directive's minimums; the Directive sets a floor only.

## Directive 2020/1057 — Posting of Workers in Road Transport

This Directive creates a **lex specialis** (sector-specific rule) for posting in road transport, derogating from the general posting framework of Directive 96/71/EC and its 2018 revision.

### When Posting Rules Apply

| Transport type | Posting applies? |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Transit through a member state (no loading/unloading) | **No** — not posting |
| Bilateral transport between establishment state and one other member state | **No** — not posting (exemption introduced by 2020/1057) |
| Bilateral transport with one additional loading/unloading in a transit country | **No** — still not posting (one cross-trade allowed per bilateral journey) |
| Cabotage operations | **Yes** — posting rules apply from day 1 |
| Cross-trade (third-country) operations | **Yes** — posting rules apply from day 1 |

### Posting Obligations Where Applicable

When a driver is posted (cabotage or cross-trade):
- Host member state's **mandatory remuneration rules** apply (including minimum wage, overtime rates, and any sector-specific wage supplements)
- Host member state's **working conditions rules** apply
- **IMI Declaration** must be submitted before the start of the posting via the EU Internal Market Information System
- Driver must carry proof of posting (IMI confirmation) at roadside

### Interaction with Working Time

The working time rules of Directive 2002/15/EC continue to apply regardless of whether posting applies. Posting determines *which country's wage and employment rules* apply — working time rules follow the driver's habitual working country (typically country of establishment).

## Directive 2002/15/EC — Working Time for Mobile Road Transport Workers

This Directive governs working time for drivers subject to EU Regulation 561/2006 (tachograph-regulated transport). It operates alongside — but is separate from — the general Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC.

**Key limits:**
- Maximum **48 hours** average working time per week (reference period: 4 months)
- Maximum **60 hours** in any single week
- Minimum rest, breaks, and night work rules

**Cross-reference:** `references/eu/working-time.md` — full details on working time calculation, night work, opt-outs.

## Directive 2003/59/EC — Driver CPC (Certificate of Professional Competence)

Drivers of vehicles requiring a C or D category licence for commercial transport must hold a **Driver CPC**. This creates a mandatory training obligation that operators must factor into employment planning.

- **Initial CPC:** Required before driving professionally (qualification test or grandfather rights)
- **Periodic CPC:** 35 hours of approved training every 5 years (7 hours/year or in blocks)
- Operator obligation: Cannot lawfully deploy a driver who has allowed their CPC to lapse; operators should track CPC renewal dates and budget for training time and cost

**Cross-reference:** `references/eu/professional-driver.md` — full CPC requirements.

## Contractual Requirements

EU law does not harmonise the full content of employment contracts for drivers, but national law in all member states implements Directive 2019/1152. A compliant driver employment contract should include:

| Element | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Identity of parties | Employer (registered operator name) and driver |
| Place of work | Usually the operator's establishment; for drivers also the nature of mobile work |
| Job title / description | e.g., "HGV Driver — international long-distance" |
| Start date | And end date if fixed-term |
| Remuneration | Basic salary, any variable components, payment frequency |
| Working hours | Normal hours; on-call arrangements; reference to collective agreement if applicable |
| Notice periods | Both employer and employee |
| Applicable collective agreement | Reference to specific agreement by name |
| Right to parallel employment | Statement confirming or lawfully restricting parallel work |
| Probation period | If applicable, not exceeding 6 months |

## Social Security Coverage — EU Regulation 883/2004

### General Principle for Road Transport

EU Regulation 883/2004 (coordination of social security systems) determines which **single** member state's social security system applies to a worker at any given time. For most workers, this is the country of habitual residence where they also work. For mobile road transport workers the rules are more complex.

### Applicable Legislation for International Drivers

Under the **Mobility Package amendments** and the guidance developed by the Administrative Commission on Social Security Coordination:

- **Employed road transport workers** working in two or more member states: the applicable legislation is **the member state where the employer's registered office or place of business is situated** (country of establishment), provided the driver does not spend a substantial part (25% or more) of their working time in their state of residence.
- If the driver **works 25% or more of their time in their state of residence**, then the state of residence becomes the competent state.
- **Self-employed international drivers:** Similar rules apply — competent state is state of establishment unless substantial activity test triggers the state of residence.

### Practical Application

For most international truck drivers employed by a transport company:
- Social security is paid in the **country where the transport company is established** (e.g., a Polish carrier's drivers pay contributions in Poland even when driving regularly through Germany and France)
- This applies regardless of how many countries the driver passes through or works in

### A1 Certificate (Portable Document A1)

The **A1 certificate** (formerly E101) is issued by the social security authority of the competent member state and proves that the driver is covered by that state's social security system.

**Drivers performing international operations should carry the A1 certificate at all times during cross-border work.** Enforcement authorities in host countries may request it to verify that social security obligations are met.

| Detail | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| Issued by | Social security authority of competent state (e.g., ZUS in Poland, INPS in Italy, Försäkringskassan in Sweden) |
| Validity | Usually 12–24 months; renewable |
| Format | Paper or electronic (digital A1 accepted in some states) |
| Required at roadside | Yes — enforcement authorities in host states may request it during labour inspections |
| Consequence of absence | Host state may claim that local social security applies and seek contributions; possible labour inspection penalty |

## Driver as Self-Employed ("False Self-Employment")

EU and national authorities increasingly scrutinise arrangements where drivers are engaged as self-employed ("own-account" operators) when the economic reality is that of employment. Indicators of false self-employment include:
- Driver works exclusively or predominantly for one principal
- Principal controls driver's working hours, routes, and methods
- Driver uses the principal's vehicle, fuel card, and equipment
- Driver bears no real entrepreneurial risk

Where false self-employment is found, authorities may reclassify the relationship as employment, triggering back-payment of social security, tax, and entitlements.

## Penalties

Employment law violations for transport operators may result in:

| Violation | Possible consequence |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Failure to issue written employment terms (Dir. 2019/1152) | National labour authority fine; varies by member state (e.g., in DE: up to €2,000 per case; in SE: up to SEK 10,000–20,000) |
| Deploying driver without valid CPC | Penalty under national transport law; in SE: sanktionsavgift; in DE: fine up to €20,000 |
| Failure to submit IMI posting declaration before cabotage/cross-trade | Fine per journey; in DE: up to €30,000; in FR: up to €8,000 per worker; in SE: SEK 40,000 |
| No A1 certificate at roadside during inspection | Host state labour authority may assess social security contributions; possible administrative penalty |
| Unlawful prohibition of parallel employment | Worker can claim breach of contract; labour authority fine under national law |
| False self-employment | Back-payment of social contributions; tax liability; employment rights claim |
