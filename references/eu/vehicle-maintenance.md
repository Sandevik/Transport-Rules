## Overview

EU law establishes minimum standards for **periodic roadworthiness testing** (Directive 2014/45/EU) and **roadside technical inspection** (Directive 2014/47/EU), but the **obligation to maintain vehicles in a roadworthy condition between tests** rests on operators through national law and the general duty of care under EU Regulation 561/2006 Article 10.

A sound maintenance programme is both a legal obligation and a commercial necessity: vehicles that fail roadside inspections may be immobilised, drivers issued prohibition notices, and operators face sanctions including the loss of their Community Licence.

## EU Legal Framework

### EU Regulation 561/2006 — Article 10: Transport Undertaking Obligations

Article 10 imposes a direct obligation on **transport undertakings** (operators), not just drivers:

> "A transport undertaking shall not give drivers it employs or who are put at its disposal any payment, even in the form of a bonus or wage supplement, related to distances travelled and/or the amount of goods carried if that payment is of such a kind as to endanger road safety or encourage infringement of this Regulation."

More broadly, Article 10(1) requires that the transport undertaking **organise drivers' work in such a way that drivers can comply** with the Regulation. The European Court of Justice and national courts have interpreted this to mean operators must not create scheduling or commercial pressure that leads drivers to operate defective vehicles.

### Directive 2014/45/EU — Periodic Roadworthiness Tests

This Directive harmonises the minimum frequency and scope of periodic roadworthiness tests (MOT equivalent) across the EU:

| Vehicle category | Test frequency |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Goods vehicles >3.5t GVW (N2, N3) | First test: 4 years after registration; then every **2 years** |
| Trailers >3.5t (O3, O4) | First test: 4 years after registration; then every **2 years** |
| Passenger vehicles >8 seats (M2, M3 — buses) | Every **1 year** |
| Light goods vehicles ≤3.5t (N1) | 4-year/2-year/annual cycle (same as passenger cars M1) |
| Vehicles used for dangerous goods transport | More frequent — see national rules |

**Important:** These are **minimum** standards. Member states may apply **more frequent** testing schedules. Sweden requires annual testing (kontrollbesiktning) for HGVs from the second year onwards.

### Directive 2014/47/EU — Roadside Technical Inspections

This Directive establishes minimum requirements for roadside technical inspections of commercial vehicles in the EU:

- Member states must inspect a sufficient number of vehicles annually (risk-based targeting using ERRU — European Register of Road Transport Undertakings)
- Inspection items cover: brakes, steering, tyres, wheels, chassis, lights, emissions, tachograph, speed limiter, cargo securing
- **Risk classification:** Operators with poor inspection histories are targeted for more frequent checks
- **Immobilisation:** Vehicles with dangerous defects may be immobilised at roadside until repaired

## Operator's Maintenance Obligation

### General Principle

The operator (transport undertaking) is responsible for ensuring that every vehicle in its fleet is **maintained in a roadworthy condition at all times**. This duty exists continuously — not only around periodic inspection dates.

A properly implemented maintenance programme must demonstrate:
1. A systematic approach to identifying and repairing defects
2. Adequate inspection intervals based on vehicle use and conditions
3. A complete paper or electronic audit trail

### DVSA Guide to Maintaining Roadworthiness (UK Best Practice — Widely Referenced)

The UK DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency) publishes a 106-page guide that is widely regarded as the most detailed practical guidance available for HGV maintenance programmes. Although UK-specific, it reflects best practice applicable across the EU and is explicitly referenced by transport consultants and enforcement bodies in multiple EU countries.

Key principles from the guide applicable throughout the EU:

**1. Maintenance Planning**
- Each vehicle/trailer should have a written maintenance schedule setting out inspection frequencies
- Safety inspections should be more frequent than the periodic statutory test interval
- High-use vehicles (daily long-distance), vehicles operating in harsh conditions (off-road, temperature extremes, heavy loads), or older vehicles should have more frequent inspections
- A specimen maintenance planner (Annex 6 in the guide) provides a template

**2. Qualified Workshop**
- Safety inspections must be carried out by a competent person / qualified workshop
- The operator must satisfy themselves that the workshop is capable of identifying and repairing defects
- Workshop must hold relevant equipment (brake testing, tachograph calibration)

**3. Defect Reporting System**
- A written defect reporting system must be in place
- Drivers complete defect reports after every duty (or at minimum daily)
- The operator must have a clear procedure for reviewing reports and authorising repairs
- Records of defect reports and repairs must be retained

## Maintenance Programme Structure

### Three-Tier Inspection System

| Level | Who performs | Frequency | Items covered |
|-------|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| **1. Daily walkaround check** | Driver | Before every shift | Safety-critical items visible to driver (see below) |
| **2. Safety inspection** | Qualified technician/workshop | Every 6–8 weeks (or by mileage — e.g., every 30,000–40,000 km for HGV) | Full system check (see below) |
| **3. Periodic statutory test** | Approved testing station | Every 1–2 years (per Directive 2014/45/EU and national law) | Comprehensive statutory inspection |

---

### Daily Walkaround Check

The driver must complete a **daily walkaround check** before operating the vehicle. This is a legal obligation in practice enforced through operator licence conditions and roadside inspection findings.

**Items to check:**

| System | What to check |
|--------|-------------|
| Tyres | Pressure (visual or gauge); tread depth (legal minimum 1mm for HGVs in EU; 1.6mm in some states); no visible damage, cuts, or bulges; wheel nuts/studs present and secure |
| Brakes | Air pressure warning light (park brake engaged until pressure adequate); no air leaks audible; brake drum/disc not visibly damaged; brake hose connections on trailer coupling |
| Lights and signals | All external lights operational (headlights main/dipped, sidelights, indicators, brake lights, reverse light, hazard lights, marker lights); reflectors clean and unbroken |
| Mirrors and visibility | All mirrors present and correctly adjusted; windscreen clean and free of damage in driver's field of vision; wipers and washers functional |
| Fuel, oil, coolant | Fuel level adequate; engine oil level within range; coolant level within range; no visible leaks under vehicle |
| Bodywork | No damage creating a hazard to other road users (sharp edges, trailing metal, unsecured body panels); load area secured |
| Load | Load properly secured; no overhanging items without appropriate markings; curtainsider straps and buckles intact |
| Tachograph | Correct card inserted; time set correctly; no fault warnings displayed; seals intact |
| Coupling (if trailer) | Fifth wheel / drawbar coupling properly engaged and locked; safety chains/cables connected; electrical connections (7-pin or 15-pin) plugged in and functional |
| Emergency equipment | Fire extinguisher present (where required by national law); warning triangles; any other mandatory equipment |

The driver must **record and report any defects** found during the walkaround check before departure. A pre-existing defect must be reported to the operator — the driver should not depart if the defect renders the vehicle unsafe or illegal.

### Safety Inspection (Workshop Check — 6–8 Weekly)

Performed by a qualified technician. This covers:

**Braking system:**
- Brake performance test (roller brake tester or decelerometer)
- Brake pad/shoe wear and disc/drum condition
- Air lines, valves, and connections
- Trailer brake performance
- Electronic Braking Systems (EBS/ABS) — fault code check

**Steering and suspension:**
- Steering play and condition (steering box, track rod ends, ball joints)
- Suspension (leaf springs, air suspension bags, shock absorbers)
- Wheel bearings (free play check)
- Axle condition

**Tyres and wheels:**
- Full tread depth measurement all positions
- Tyre condition — sidewall, casing
- Wheel rim condition; no cracking
- Wheel nut torque check

**Lights:**
- Full operational check all circuits
- Headlight aim
- Wiring condition — no chafe, secure connections

**Engine and drivetrain:**
- Engine oil, coolant, power steering fluid
- Belts and hoses condition
- Exhaust system — leaks, condition
- Fuel system — no leaks

**Tachograph:**
- Check calibration certificate validity (must be within 2-year calibration cycle — see `references/eu/tachograph.md`)
- Check seals are intact
- Operational function test

**Safety equipment:**
- Fire extinguisher charge
- First aid kit (where required)
- Speed limiter — function check

## Record-Keeping Requirements

### Duration

Operators must retain maintenance records. Standard practice across EU:

| Record type | Minimum retention period |
|-------------|-------------------------|
| Safety inspection reports | **12–15 months** (UK: 15 months; SE/EU: minimum 12 months) |
| Driver defect reports | **12–15 months** |
| Repair records (invoices, workshop reports) | **12–15 months** |
| Tachograph calibration certificates | Life of vehicle or until recalibrated |
| Periodic test certificates | Keep current plus previous |

### Content of Records

Each safety inspection record must contain:
- Vehicle registration and fleet number
- Date of inspection
- **Mileage/odometer** reading at inspection
- Name and signature of technician performing the inspection
- Every item inspected (using a standard checklist)
- Any defects found — description and severity
- Action taken — repair carried out, part replaced, deferred for repair
- Date of any repair and who carried it out
- Inspector sign-off after repair confirmed

### Defect Report System

The operator must maintain a **driver defect reporting system**:
- Drivers complete a defect report form at the end of each shift (or when a defect is discovered)
- The transport office must acknowledge receipt of defect reports
- The operator must demonstrate that reports were acted upon (signed off by workshop or manager)
- Defect reports with no action record are a serious finding at operator licence review

## Tachograph Calibration in Maintenance Context

Tachographs must be **calibrated every 2 years** by an approved calibration centre. The calibration status is part of the vehicle's maintenance record:

- The calibration certificate/plaque must be attached to the tachograph or vehicle
- Workshops performing safety inspections should check calibration status
- A tachograph more than 2 years past its last calibration is a criminal offence in most EU states as well as a vehicle defect

## Swedish National Rules (Fordonslagen)

In Sweden, the **Fordonslagen (2002:574)** and **Fordonsförordningen (2009:211)** impose explicit maintenance obligations on vehicle owners and operators:

- Vehicles must be maintained so as to not be **a danger to traffic safety** (Fordonslagen Chapter 2)
- **Transportstyrelsen** is the competent authority for vehicle roadworthiness
- **Kontrollbesiktning** (statutory test) for HGVs: annually from the second year of registration (more frequent than the EU minimum)
- Operators must also comply with requirements for workshop standards if operating their own workshop (verkstad)

**Swedish testing authority:** AB Svensk Bilprovning and approved private testing stations.

## Defect Categories at Roadside Inspection

Under Directive 2014/47/EU, defects found at roadside inspections are classified:

| Category | Definition | Action |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| **Minor (MM)** | Defect with no significant effect on safety or environment | Note recorded; driver continues |
| **Major (MJ)** | Defect adversely affecting road safety; risk to other road users | Rectification required within specified time; possible restriction on use |
| **Dangerous (D)** | Direct and immediate risk to road safety | **Immediate immobilisation** — vehicle prohibited from further use until repaired |

Operators whose vehicles regularly attract Major or Dangerous defects are flagged in ERRU and targeted for more frequent inspections, and may be referred to the operator licence authority.

---

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence |
|-----------|------------|
| Operating a vehicle with a dangerous defect (immediate prohibition category) | Immobilisation at roadside; in SE: sanktionsavgift; prosecution possible for operator |
| Failure to maintain adequate maintenance records | Adverse finding at operator licence review; can lead to revocation of Community Licence |
| Operating with expired tachograph calibration | Criminal offence / fine in most EU states; in SE: sanktionsavgift |
| Failure to act on driver defect reports | Operator licence sanction; evidence of systemic failure can lead to revocation |
| Allowing driver to depart with defective vehicle known to operator | Operator criminal liability under national road traffic law |
| Periodic test overdue | Vehicle prohibition; fine; in SE: vehicle tax surcharge |
