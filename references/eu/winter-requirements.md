# EU — Winter Tyre and Equipment Requirements

**EU Baseline:** There is NO EU-wide winter tyre mandate. Requirements are national. This file explains the EU framework and refers to country files.

## Overview

The European Union has **no harmonised mandate for winter tyres** on commercial or private vehicles. EU Regulation 661/2009 (General Safety Regulation) establishes type-approval requirements for tyres — including wet-grip performance, rolling noise, and rolling resistance — but does not mandate seasonal tyre changes or winter-specific tyre fitment.

Winter tyre requirements are set entirely at the **national level** by each EU member state, as well as by EEA/AETR countries (Norway, Switzerland). The result is a patchwork of national laws that differ significantly in:

- Whether a mandate exists at all
- Whether the mandate is **fixed-date** (applies on set calendar dates regardless of weather) or **situational** (applies only when winter road conditions are present)
- The date ranges of the mandatory period
- Minimum tread depth requirements
- Whether studded tyres are permitted, and if so, during what periods
- Whether chains must be carried or used on certain roads
- The type of symbol required (M+S marking or the **Three-Peak Mountain Snowflake (3PMSF)** symbol)
- Fine levels

### EU Tyre Symbols

Two symbols are relevant for winter tyres across all EU and EEA member states:

| Symbol | Meaning | Test standard |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **M+S** (Mud and Snow) | Tyre has a tread pattern suitable for mud and light snow | Marketing label only; no independent performance test required |
| **3PMSF** (Three-Peak Mountain Snowflake, ❄) | Tyre has been tested and meets minimum snow traction performance | UN-ECE Regulation 117; EU Regulation 661/2009 Annex II |

The M+S marking has historically been accepted as "winter tyre" in most national laws, but **Germany and several other countries** now require the 3PMSF symbol for full compliance. Many national regulators accept M+S as a transitional arrangement, but the trend is towards requiring 3PMSF.

### EU Tyre Labelling

Since 2012 (updated 2021 with EU Regulation 2020/740), all tyres sold in the EU must carry an EU tyre label showing:
- Fuel efficiency rating (A–G)
- Wet grip rating (A–G)
- External rolling noise (dB and class)
- Severe snow grip (snowflake symbol, if tested)
- Ice grip (if tested)

The EU tyre label does not impose seasonal requirements; it informs purchaser choice.

|---------|------|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Finland** | Fixed-date | 1 Dec – 28/29 Feb (mandatory) | Yes | 3 mm |

## Key Regulatory Notes for Cross-Border Transport

1. **Operators crossing multiple countries** must comply with the strictest requirement applicable in each country. Carrying winter tyres from November through March is the practical baseline for all European cross-border HGV operations.

2. **Chain carry requirements**: Norway, Austria, and Switzerland require heavy vehicles to carry chains during certain periods or on certain roads, even if winter tyres are fitted.

3. **Enforcement**: Winter tyre violations at roadside checks can result in fines, prohibition from continuing the journey, or both. In Norway and Austria, enforcement is frequent at mountain pass checkpoints.

4. **Insurance liability**: Drivers/operators involved in accidents while operating without mandated winter tyres may face reverse burden of proof on fault in civil liability proceedings.
