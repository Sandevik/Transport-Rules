## What is ADR?

**ADR** (Accord relatif au transport international des marchandises Dangereuses par Route) is the international agreement governing road transport of dangerous goods. It is updated every two years (ADR 2025 applies from 1 January 2025, with a 6-month transition for the 2023 version until 30 June 2025).

**Directive 2008/68/EC** makes ADR mandatory within the EU for both international and domestic transport. Each member state designates a national competent authority for ADR implementation and enforcement.

---

## Scope

ADR applies to transport of:
- **Explosives** (Class 1)
- **Gases** (Class 2) — compressed, liquefied, dissolved
- **Flammable liquids** (Class 3) — including petrol, diesel above thresholds
- **Flammable solids** (Class 4.1, 4.2, 4.3)
- **Oxidising substances and organic peroxides** (Class 5.1, 5.2)
- **Toxic and infectious substances** (Class 6.1, 6.2)
- **Radioactive materials** (Class 7)
- **Corrosive substances** (Class 8)
- **Miscellaneous dangerous substances** (Class 9) — incl. lithium batteries, dry ice

**Exemptions** apply for small quantities (LQ — limited quantities), quantities below certain thresholds, and transport by private individuals.

---

## UN Numbers and Proper Shipping Names

Every dangerous good has a **UN number** (e.g. UN 1203 = petrol/gasoline, UN 1978 = propane) and a **Proper Shipping Name** (PSN). These determine:
- Hazard class and packing group
- Packaging requirements
- Labelling/placarding
- Documentation requirements
- Transport restrictions

## Classification and Packing Groups

| Packing Group | Degree of danger |
|---------------|----------------|
| **I** (PG I) | High danger |
| **II** (PG II) | Medium danger |
| **III** (PG III) | Low danger |

## Key ADR Requirements

### 1. Driver training and ADR certificate

Drivers transporting dangerous goods (above LQ and small load thresholds) must hold a valid **ADR certificate** (ADR-intyg/ADR-bevis):
- **Basic certificate** — all road transport of dangerous goods
- **Specialisation for tanks** — required for tank vehicle transport
- **Specialisation for explosives (Class 1)**
- **Specialisation for radioactive (Class 7)**

**Training:** Approved ADR training course + theoretical exam
**Certificate validity:** 5 years (renewable with refresher training)
**Issuing authority:** The national transport authority in the driver's country of residence (e.g. Transportstyrelsen in Sweden, Bundesamt fur Güterverkehr in Germany). ADR certificates are mutually recognised across all AETR/ADR contracting parties.

### 2. Vehicle and equipment requirements

- Correct **placards** (orange panels, hazard diamond labels) on all four sides of vehicle/tank
- **Fire extinguishers** (sizes per ADR 8.1.4)
- **Warning triangle** and appropriate PPE (gloves, eye protection)
- **Written instructions** (Schriftliche Weisungen / skriftliga instruktioner) — one set per language of the countries being traversed
- Appropriate **ADR marking** on vehicle

### 3. Packaging

- Approved packaging (UN-marked) required for most goods
- Packaging tested and certified according to ADR Chapter 6
- Segregation requirements between incompatible goods (Table 7.2.4)

### 4. Transport documents

Required documents in the cab:
- **Transport document** (fraktsedel/transportdokument) — must include UN number, PSN, hazard class, packing group, quantity
- **ADR certificate** (driver's)
- **Vehicle approval certificate** (for tank vehicles)
- **Written instructions** (emergency action document)

### 5. Tunnels

Tunnel restrictions per ADR Chapter 8.6:
- Tunnels classified A (no restriction) through E (prohibition)
- Dangerous goods vehicles may be restricted from certain tunnels
- Alternative routes must be planned

## Transport Categories and Threshold Quantities

ADR defines **transport categories 0–4** for substances. When a vehicle carries mixed goods, the total transport index determines whether full ADR requirements apply:

| Transport category | Threshold for full ADR compliance |
|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| 0 | Always full ADR |
| 1 | > 20 (in mixed load calculation) |
| 2 | > 333 |
| 3 | > 1000 |
| 4 | Unlimited quantity — not restricted |

Below thresholds: **exemptions apply** (still requires some markings and documentation).

## Safety Adviser (Säkerhetsrådgivare / DGSA)

Companies that transport, pack, load, or unload dangerous goods must appoint a **Dangerous Goods Safety Adviser (DGSA/säkerhetsrådgivare)** unless fully exempt:
- Holds a DGSA certificate (written exam)
- Responsible for monitoring compliance
- Submits annual report on company's dangerous goods activities
- Must report accidents involving dangerous goods to MSB

**Legal basis:** ADR 1.8.3, implemented via national regulations in each member state.

---

## Enforcement

ADR enforcement is carried out by national authorities. Under Directive 2008/68/EC, member states must conduct roadside checks covering:
- Driver ADR certificate (validity, correct specialisation class)
- Transport documents (UN number, PSN, hazard class, quantity)
- Vehicle placards and labels
- Written instructions present in cab
- Equipment (fire extinguishers, PPE, warning triangle)
- Vehicle approval certificate (tank vehicles)

Violations are classified by national law. Most EU member states treat driving without a valid ADR certificate as a criminal or administrative offence with fines and possible driving prohibition. Systematic failures by the company are reported to the operator's home country authority via EU enforcement cooperation channels.

For country-specific enforcement bodies and fine amounts, see the relevant country file (e.g. `references/countries/se/dangerous-goods.md`, `references/countries/de/dangerous-goods.md`).

## What to Do if Goods Leak or Spill

**Written Instructions (Schriftliche Weisungen / instructions ecrites) are the primary emergency reference.** ADR 5.4.3 requires the driver to carry written instructions for every hazard class loaded. These instructions specify the exact emergency steps for that substance.

### General emergency procedure (ADR Chapter 1.3 + 8.5)

1. **Stop safely** — pull off the road as far as possible; do not obstruct traffic unnecessarily
2. **Engine off, handbrake on** — reduce ignition risk; do not use mobile phone near flammable goods
3. **Warn other road users** — warning triangle/lights; keep people well clear of the vehicle
4. **Consult written instructions** — the hazard-specific emergency action sheet in the cab tells you exactly what to do for the substance involved
5. **Call emergency services** — provide: UN number, proper shipping name, hazard class, approximate quantity spilled, vehicle location
6. **Do not attempt to stop a spill** unless the instructions specifically say it is safe to do so and you have the correct PPE
7. **Move upwind** — stay upwind and uphill of any spill or vapour cloud
8. **Do not eat, drink, or smoke** near the spill area
9. **Prevent spread** — if safe, contain the spill from reaching drains, watercourses, or soil
10. **Inform the consignor / DGSA** — as soon as practicable after the emergency services are notified

### What emergency services need from you

| Information | Why |
|---|---|
| UN number | Identifies the exact substance |
| Proper Shipping Name | Confirms the hazard |
| Hazard class and subsidiary hazards | Determines response method |
| Estimated quantity leaked | Determines evacuation radius |
| Vehicle location (GPS or road name) | Routes emergency response |
| Whether anyone is injured | Prioritises ambulance dispatch |

### DGSA accident reporting obligation

If dangerous goods are involved in an accident or incident that results in:
- Injury to persons
- Property damage beyond the vehicle
- Any release of dangerous goods from their packaging

The **DGSA (Dangerous Goods Safety Adviser)** must report the incident to the national competent authority (ADR 1.8.5). In practice this means notifying the relevant national authority within the reporting period prescribed by national law (often 1 month).

## ADR 2025 Key Changes (vs ADR 2023)

- Updated provisions for **lithium batteries** (Class 9) — tightened rules for damaged/defective batteries
- New requirements for **electric vehicles** during transport
- Updated **special provisions** for certain Class 3 substances
- Revised **packaging instructions** for several UN numbers
