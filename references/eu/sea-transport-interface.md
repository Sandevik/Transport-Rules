## Overview

Road transport intersects with maritime operations primarily through **Ro-Ro (Roll-on/Roll-off) ferries**, where trucks drive directly onto vessel car/freight decks. This is a critical interface for Scandinavian operators (Sweden, Finland, Norway, Denmark) routing freight to continental Europe, and for operators crossing the English Channel (France–UK). The transition between road and sea involves a change of governing law: **ADR** (road dangerous goods) gives way to **IMDG Code** (maritime DG) once cargo is onboard a sea-going vessel, while **CMR** continues to govern the cargo liability contract throughout.

## Ro-Ro Ferry Operations — Key Facts

### Truck Compatibility
Most major Ro-Ro freight ferries accommodate:
- Length: up to 18.75 m (standard semi + tractor); some routes allow longer combinations — verify per operator
- Height: typically 4.5 m–5.0 m; some routes with low decks: 4.0–4.2 m — **always confirm with operator when booking**
- Width: standard 2.6 m road width + mirrors generally OK; oversized loads need advance notification
- GVW: ferry deck load limits; typical 120–160 tonnes per lane per metre; declare GVW at booking

### Driver Requirements on Ferry
- Driver typically remains with vehicle on freight deck OR disembarks to passenger areas
- On long overnight crossings (>1 hour): EU 561/2006 Art. 9 — ferry crossing ≥ 1 hour allows driver to interrupt daily or weekly rest (may take two interruptions during the ferry leg)
- On short crossings (<1 hour): rest time does not count

## Dangerous Goods on Ro-Ro Ferries — IMDG Code

**When IMDG applies:** The **IMDG Code** (International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code, published by IMO) governs dangerous goods transported on sea-going vessels, including in Ro-Ro trucks. ADR documentation remains valid on the road legs, but at-sea the master (captain) has authority over DG under IMDG/SOLAS.

**Key DG obligations for Ro-Ro truck operators:**
- **Declare DG at booking** — operators must declare dangerous goods when booking ferry passage
- **Minimum 24 hours advance notice** — many ferry operators require DG declaration ≥24h before departure (some: 48h)
- **DG manifest** — ferry must have ship's manifest listing all DG onboard
- **Segregation on deck** — DG vehicles parked in designated areas; passengers and crew may not approach
- **ADR vs IMDG class compatibility** — ADR and IMDG both use UN classification system but have different stowage/segregation requirements for sea; verify with operator
- **Prohibited goods by class on ferries:**
  - Class 1 (explosives): strictly controlled; often prohibited on passenger Ro-Ro
  - Class 7 (radioactive): requires special certification; usually prohibited on passenger vessels
  - Large quantities Class 2 gases: restricted on some routes
- Verify per-route DG permissions with the ferry operator before booking

## Channel Tunnel — Le Shuttle Freight

The Channel Tunnel (Eurotunnel) operates a separate Ro-Ro train service for trucks. See `references/topics/major-tunnel-restrictions.md` for full Channel Tunnel details including DG restrictions.

Key points:
- Mandatory advance booking (eurotunnel.com/uk/freight)
- DG must be declared ≥24 hours in advance
- ADR Category B tunnel classification
- Some DG classes prohibited

## CMR During Sea Leg

The **CMR Convention** continues to apply to the road transport contract even when the truck is aboard a ferry. The carrier remains responsible under CMR for goods onboard. However:
- If goods are **damaged by a maritime peril** (storm, vessel stranding) — the carrier may invoke Art. 17(4) CMR exonerating circumstances
- The carrier should have their CMR liability insurance confirm that sea-leg coverage is included
- Some CMR policies exclude damage occurring during sea transit — **verify with insurer**

## Customs on Ferry Routes

| Route type | Customs procedure |
|---|---|
| Within EU/EEA (e.g., SE ↔ DE, FI ↔ EE) | No customs stop; border guard checks identity documents |
| EU ↔ non-EU (e.g., FI ↔ RU, SE ↔ Ukraine via RO ferry) | Full customs clearance at port; T1/T2 transit documents required |
| EU ↔ UK (post-Brexit) | GVMS (Goods Vehicle Movement Service) — truck must be pre-notified; customs at port |

## Enforcement

| Authority | Role |
|---|---|
| Port State Control (European Maritime Safety Agency — EMSA) | Vessel and cargo safety checks at EU ports |
| National maritime authorities | DG compliance at port; document checks |
| DVSA / Transportstyrelsen / etc. | Road transport compliance begins/ends at port gate |

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Undeclared dangerous goods on ferry | Removal of vehicle from vessel; criminal prosecution; fines | IMDG Code; national maritime law |
| False DG declaration | Prosecution; unlimited fines | SOLAS; national maritime law |
| Overloading ferry deck | Refusal to load; carrier liability for delay | Shipping conditions |
