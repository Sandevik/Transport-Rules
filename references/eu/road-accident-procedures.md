## Overview

When a commercial vehicle is involved in a road accident abroad, the driver faces simultaneous obligations: safety, legal reporting, insurance notification, tachograph data preservation, and communications with the employer. Procedures vary significantly by country — particularly regarding mandatory police involvement. Failure to follow correct procedures can result in criminal liability, invalid insurance claims, and loss of tachograph evidence in serious accident investigations.

This guide covers practical steps for professional drivers and transport operators managing road accidents in EU and AETR countries.

## Immediate Steps at the Scene

### Priority Order

| Step | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Safety** | Switch on hazard lights; apply handbrake; move to safety if possible | Do not move vehicle if injury accident (preserve scene for police) |
| **2. Warning triangle** | Place triangle at safe distance behind vehicle (50m in good conditions; 100m+ on motorways) | EU Directive mandates carrying one triangle for commercial vehicles; many countries require two |
| **3. High-visibility vest** | All occupants exiting vehicle must wear hi-vis vest before exiting | Mandatory in ES, FR, IT, AT, ES, PT, BE, RO, BG, HU, HR; recommended everywhere |
| **4. First aid** | Attend to injured persons if safe and trained; do not move seriously injured unless immediate life risk | |
| **5. Call emergency services** | Dial **112** (EU-wide) or national number | See emergency numbers table below |
| **6. Secure the scene** | Prevent further collisions; use flares or second triangle if available | |

## Police Reporting — When Required

Police attendance obligations vary significantly by country and accident severity:

### Always Call Police (Injury or Death)

**In every country**: police must be called if any person has been injured or killed. This is a universal requirement. Failure to call police when injury has occurred is a criminal offence.

### Country-Specific Requirements for Property Damage Only

| Country | Police required for property damage only? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PL (Poland)** | Yes — mandatory | Police must attend all accidents; failing to call police is an offence |
| **RO (Romania)** | Yes — mandatory | Police must issue accident report for insurance purposes |
| **BG (Bulgaria)** | Yes — mandatory | Insurance claim invalid without police report in many cases |
| **HU (Hungary)** | Yes — mandatory if driver disagrees on fault or damage exceeds threshold | EAS used when both agree |
| **SK (Slovakia)** | Yes — mandatory | Police report required for insurance |
| **TR (Turkey)** | Yes — strongly recommended | Official kaza tutanağı (accident report) required by Turkish insurers |
| **GR (Greece)** | Recommended; required if unresolved dispute | Insurance company may require police report |
| **DE (Germany)** | No — not required if both parties agree | EAS form sufficient; police only if injury or dispute |
| **AT (Austria)** | No — EAS sufficient if agreement | Police if injury or dispute |
| **NL (Netherlands)** | No — EAS sufficient | |
| **BE (Belgium)** | No — EAS sufficient | |
| **FR (France)** | No — EAS sufficient | Constat Amiable is the standard; police only for injury |
| **SE (Sweden)** | No — EAS sufficient | Driver must report to own insurer; police called for injury |
| **IT (Italy)** | Recommended | CID (Convenzione Indennizzo Diretto) — direct compensation; works without police |
| **ES (Spain)** | No — EAS sufficient for property damage | |
| **CH (Switzerland)** | If damage exceeds CHF 1,000 or injury | |

**Practical advice:** In eastern EU countries and non-EU AETR states, **always call police** — the insurance system often requires an official protocol (protokol/proces-verbal) to process claims.

## European Accident Statement (EAS)

### What It Is

The **European Accident Statement** (EAS — also known as Constat Amiable, Gemeinsame Unfallmitteilung, or Europäischer Unfallbericht) is a standardised two-part self-copying form used to record accident details by mutual agreement of the parties involved.

### Availability

- **Free** from any motor insurer and national insurance bureau
- Available in all EU languages
- Can be downloaded and pre-printed from national insurer websites
- Commercial drivers should carry at least two blank forms in the cab

### How to Complete the EAS

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Date, time, location | Exact location (road number, km marker, municipality) |
| Vehicle details (A and B) | Registration, make, model, insurance policy, insurer name and address |
| Driver details (A and B) | Name, address, driving licence number |
| Sketch | Simple diagram of road layout, position of vehicles at moment of impact, direction of travel, road markings |
| Circumstances boxes | Tick-box list of circumstances (e.g., "was overtaking," "was changing lanes," "was emerging from parking"); each driver marks what applies to their vehicle |
| Damage | Mark area of damage on vehicle diagram |
| Signature | **Both drivers sign** — this is the key step; retain your copy before signing |

### After Completing EAS

1. **Retain your copy** — make a photograph of the signed form immediately
2. Send to your insurer within the time limit specified in your policy (typically 24–72 hours)
3. EAS does NOT constitute an admission of liability by either party — the tick-boxes describe circumstances, not fault
4. Where police have attended, request the **police report number** (PVN/Protokol number)

### When EAS Is Not Possible

- Other driver refuses to complete EAS: photograph their vehicle, licence plate, document their identity, call police
- Language barrier: the tick-box format works without a common language; use the sketch
- Other driver flees: note plate, colour, direction; call police immediately; this is a hit-and-run offence

## Tachograph Data Preservation

In a **serious accident** (injury, fatality, significant vehicle damage), the tachograph record is critical evidence:

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Do NOT attempt to erase or modify** tachograph data | Tampering is a criminal offence and destroys evidence |
| **Do NOT remove the driver card** unless instructed by police | If police are downloading data on-site, follow their instructions |
| **Inform employer/dispatcher** immediately | Operator must preserve digital records; download VU data remotely or on-site |
| **Police may download** VU (Vehicle Unit) data at scene | Police in DE, FR, SE, NL have equipment and authority to do this |
| **Printout before leaving** | Where possible, take a printout of the current activity from the tachograph |
| **Keep 28-day record intact** | Do not allow the rolling 28-day card data to be overwritten; download promptly if card is near memory limit |

Under EU Regulation 165/2014, tachograph data is admissible evidence in criminal and civil proceedings.

## Driver Rights

### Rights Within the EU

| Right | Details |
|---|---|
| **Right to interpreter** | EU law and ECHR guarantee interpretation in criminal proceedings; request formally at police station |
| **Right to remain silent** | In criminal investigations; applicable in all EU states |
| **Right to consult a lawyer** | Before and during police questioning |
| **Right to medical examination** | Especially if alcohol/drug testing is being conducted; driver may request independent examination |
| **Right to contact employer** | Police must facilitate this for commercial drivers |

### Rights for Non-EU Citizens (Consular Access)

Non-EU drivers (UA, BY, TR, GE, etc.) have the right under the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations to:
- Be informed of the right to contact their consulate
- Have the consulate notified of their detention
- Receive consular assistance

### Vehicle Immobilisation

Police in several countries (DE, CH, AT, IT) may **prohibit further driving** if:
- The vehicle is deemed unsafe after the accident
- The driver is suspected of driving under the influence (pending test results)
- The driver has no local address and poses a flight risk (financial guarantee may be demanded)

In DE and CH: **financial deposit or guarantee** (Sicherheitsleistung) may be demanded on-the-spot to cover potential fines. Amount varies; transport companies should carry a credit card with adequate limit and have roadside assistance numbers readily available.

## Insurance Claim Procedure

### Step-by-Step Claims Process

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Notify insurer **immediately** (same day; most policies require 24–48 hours) |
| 2 | Provide: EAS form, police report number (if applicable), photographs of damage, location details, witness contacts |
| 3 | Submit claim to the **insurer in the country of accident** or through the at-fault vehicle's insurer's claims representative in your home country |
| 4 | If at-fault vehicle is uninsured: claim to the national **Motor Insurers Bureau** (MIB) of the country where the accident occurred |
| 5 | If at-fault insurer disputes or delays: escalate through national insurance mediator (Ombudsman) or courts |
| 6 | For cargo damage: notify CMR cargo insurer separately; preserve damaged goods as evidence |
| 7 | Time limit for claims: **3 years** from date of accident in most EU countries |

### Claims Representatives

Under Directive 2009/103/EC Art. 21, all EU motor insurers must appoint a **claims representative** in every other member state. Drivers can file claims in their home country through this representative rather than dealing with foreign insurers directly. The insurer's claims representative contact details should be in your insurance certificate.

## Towing and Recovery

EU law does not specify towing procedures, but practical guidance:

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| **Vehicle immobilised** | Contact roadside assistance (Europ Assistance, ADAC, AA, RAC, Falck, etc.) — have membership/contract number in cab |
| **Police requiring tow** | Police in some countries can compulsorily assign a towing contractor; costs may be high; get a receipt |
| **Vehicle recovery across borders** | Check insurance for repatriation cover; commercial vehicles may require special recovery transport (low-loader) |
| **Load transfer** | If cargo must be transferred to another vehicle, document carefully; take photos; get signatures; CMR implications apply |

**Recommended:** Every commercial vehicle operating internationally should carry the employer's 24-hour emergency contact number and the insurer's 24-hour claims line prominently in the cab.

## Checklist: Documents to Gather at Scene

Before leaving the accident scene, ensure you have:

- [ ] Both drivers' names, addresses, licence numbers, nationality
- [ ] Both vehicles' registration plates, makes, and models
- [ ] Both insurers' names, policy numbers, and contact details
- [ ] EAS form completed and signed by both parties (if possible)
- [ ] Police attendance confirmed (if required) — note officer's name and report number
- [ ] Photographs: damage to both vehicles, road layout, skid marks, road signs, weather conditions
- [ ] Witness details (name, phone, car registration)
- [ ] GPS coordinates or exact road location (km marker, road name)
- [ ] Tachograph printout taken

## Penalties

Failure to comply with accident reporting obligations is a criminal offence in most countries:

| Violation | Country | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Hit and run** (failing to stop after accident with injury) | All EU countries | Criminal prosecution; imprisonment; licence revocation |
| **Failing to call police** (where mandatory) | PL, RO, BG, SK, TR | Fine + criminal investigation; insurance claim may be invalidated |
| **Tampering with tachograph after accident** | All EU | Criminal offence under Regulation 165/2014; up to 2 years imprisonment in some countries |
| **Failing to render aid** | All EU | Criminal offence; imprisonment in DE (up to 3 years), FR, IT |
| **Providing false information to police** | All countries | Criminal fraud; licence suspension |
| **Not wearing hi-vis vest before exiting vehicle** | ES, FR, IT, RO, AT, BE (mandatory) | Fine; typically €50–300 |
