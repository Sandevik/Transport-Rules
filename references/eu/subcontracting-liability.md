## Overview

Subcontracting is standard practice in road freight transport — a carrier (the **contracting carrier**) accepts a transport order from a shipper and then engages a subcarrier (the **actual carrier**) to perform all or part of the transport. This creates a chain of liability that has significant legal and commercial implications for all parties.

Under the **CMR Convention**, the contracting carrier does not shed liability by subcontracting. The shipper retains a direct claim against the contracting carrier regardless of who actually performed the transport.

---

## CMR Article 3 — Contractor Responsibility for Subcontractors

### Text of Article 3

> *"For the purposes of this Convention, a carrier shall be responsible for the acts and omissions of his agents and servants and of any other persons of whose services he makes use for the performance of the carriage, when such agents, servants or persons are acting within the scope of their employment, as if such acts or omissions were his own."*

### Practical Meaning

This is one of the most commercially significant provisions in the CMR Convention. It means:

1. The **contracting carrier is fully responsible to the shipper** for any loss, damage, or delay caused by the subcarrier
2. The shipper does **not** need to identify which carrier in the chain actually caused the problem
3. The shipper can claim directly against the contracting carrier regardless of subcontractor involvement
4. The contracting carrier's **CMR liability insurance** must cover this exposure

**Example:** Swedish carrier (A) accepts an order from a German shipper. A subcontracts to a Polish carrier (B). B's driver causes an accident and cargo worth €200,000 is destroyed. The German shipper claims against A. A must pay (up to CMR limits) and then exercise its right of recourse against B.

---

## Right of Recourse Against Subcontractor

The contracting carrier who pays a CMR claim has a **right of recourse** against the subcarrier that actually caused the damage. However, this right of recourse must be established through a **separate contract** with the subcarrier — CMR does not itself create a contractual relationship between the shipper and the subcarrier.

### Back-to-Back Contract Structure

Best practice is to include in the subcontract agreement a clause making the subcarrier:
1. Subject to the **same CMR liability** as the contracting carrier's obligation to the shipper
2. Obligated to **maintain valid CMR insurance** with adequate limits
3. Required to **cooperate in any claims investigation**
4. Responsible for **providing documentation** (CMR consignment note, tachograph data, etc.) in the event of a claim

This "back-to-back" structure ensures the subcarrier cannot benefit from more favourable contractual terms than those the contracting carrier owes the shipper.

## CMR Articles 34–40 — Successive Carriers

Where a transport is performed by **successive carriers** (each carrier taking over the goods and becoming a party to the contract for their leg):

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Art. 34 — Multiple parties | Each successive carrier becomes a party to the contract by accepting the goods and the CMR consignment note |
| Art. 35 — Duty to check | Each carrier must check apparent condition of goods on taking over; enter reservations if defects found |
| Art. 36 — Claim route | Claimant may sue first carrier, last carrier, or the carrier on whose leg damage occurred |
| Art. 38 — Indemnity | Where the carrier paying the claim cannot identify which leg damage occurred on, liability is shared proportionally by all carriers in proportion to their share of the freight |
| Art. 39 — Recourse | Carrier paying the claim has right of recourse against the carrier(s) responsible |
| Art. 40 — Security | Carriers may require security from each other before carrying onwards |

**Important distinction:** Articles 34–40 apply to **successive carriers** (each carries a leg under one CMR). Simple subcontracting (one carrier engages another to perform the whole transport under one CMR) is governed by Article 3.

## Freight Forwarder vs. Carrier Distinction

### Why It Matters

A **freight forwarder (speditor)** acting as an **agent** for the shipper is not a carrier and CMR does not apply to the forwarder directly — the forwarder arranges transport on behalf of the shipper and the actual carrier has CMR liability.

A freight forwarder acting as a **principal** (undertaking to carry rather than merely arranging) becomes a carrier for CMR purposes, even if they do not own a single vehicle.

### Key Indicators: Agent vs. Principal

| Factor | Agent (forwarder only) | Principal (forwarder as carrier) |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Issuing their own CMR waybill | No — endorses carrier's CMR | Yes — issues CMR in their own name |
| Issuing FIATA FBL | No | Yes |
| Specifying the price to shipper as a transport price | No — shows actual freight + commission | Yes — quotes all-in transport price |
| Taking responsibility for cargo loss | No — refers shipper to actual carrier | Yes — assumes direct liability |
| Controlling routing/carrier selection | Advisory | Yes — full discretion |

### Forwarder Liability

When a forwarder **acts as a principal carrier**:
- CMR applies in full — including the 8.33 SDR/kg liability limit
- The forwarder needs **freight forwarder's liability (FFL) insurance** or **CMR insurance** in their own name
- The forwarder has a right of recourse against the actual carrier under CMR Art. 3/34–40

## Subcontractor Selection Obligations

Contracting carriers should conduct **due diligence** on subcontractors before engagement. At minimum:

### Legal Compliance Checks

| Requirement | How to verify |
|-------------|--------------|
| EU Community Licence (or national operator licence) | Request certified copy; verify via ERRU or national authority |
| Valid CMR insurance | Request current certificate of insurance showing adequate limits and coverage period |
| Driver holds valid CPC | Verify Driver CPC card or digital record; check expiry date |
| Driver holds valid tachograph card (digital driver card) | Request card copy or confirmation |
| Vehicle has valid periodic test certificate (contrôle technique / HU / kontrollbesiktning) | Check certificate |
| Vehicle has valid tachograph calibration | Check calibration plaque on tachograph |
| No transport authority prohibition orders | Check ERRU where available |

### Posting Obligations in Subcontracted Transport

When a contracting carrier engages a **subcarrier from another EU member state** to perform:
- **Cabotage** in the contracting carrier's home state
- **Cross-trade** (third-country) operations

...the **posting rules of Directive 2020/1057** may apply to the **subcarrier's drivers**. The contracting carrier should ensure the subcarrier has:
1. Submitted the required **IMI posting declaration** for the host member state
2. Applied the **host state's mandatory remuneration rules** (minimum wage etc.) to the posted driver
3. The driver carries **proof of posting** (IMI confirmation reference) at roadside

**Cross-reference:** `references/eu/posting-workers.md` and `references/eu/driver-employment-law.md`.

## Supply Chain Due Diligence — Emerging Legal Framework

### German Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz (LkSG) — Supply Chain Due Diligence Act

Germany's LkSG came into force on 1 January 2023:
- **Applies to:** Companies with ≥3,000 employees (from 2023); ≥1,000 employees (from 2024) with their registered office in Germany
- **Obligation:** Companies must identify, prevent, and mitigate **human rights and environmental risks** in their supply chains — including from subcontractors and suppliers
- **For transport operators:** Large German operators (and German companies that are major shippers) must verify that their transport subcontractors comply with:
  - Prohibition of forced and child labour
  - Fair working conditions and wages
  - Safety at work
  - Environmental protection requirements
- **Enforcement:** Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA); fines up to 2% of annual global turnover for large companies; exclusion from public procurement for up to 3 years

### EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) — Directive 2024/1760

The EU CSDDD was adopted in May 2024 and extends supply chain due diligence obligations across the EU:
- **Applies to:** Large EU companies (≥1,000 employees and >€450m turnover) phased in from 2027; third-country companies of equivalent size with significant EU turnover
- **Obligations similar to LkSG** but EU-wide in scope
- **Transport sector impact:** Large transport operators and logistics groups will need to document due diligence procedures covering their subcontractor networks
- **Civil liability:** Companies can be held civilly liable for damages resulting from due diligence failures in their supply chains

### Practical Implications for Transport Operators

Even where LkSG or CSDDD do not directly apply to a carrier (below the size threshold), **large shipper customers** may require:
- Completion of due diligence questionnaires covering employment practices
- Evidence of social compliance audits (e.g., SQAS, SMETA)
- Contractual representations about compliance with labour law
- Right to audit subcontractors

## Contractual Best Practices for Subcontracting

A well-drafted subcontract agreement for road transport should include:

1. **CMR back-to-back clause** — subcarrier assumes same CMR liability as contracting carrier owes shipper
2. **Insurance warranty** — subcarrier warrants maintaining CMR insurance with specified minimum limits; evidence to be provided on request
3. **Driver compliance warranty** — subcarrier warrants drivers hold valid CPC, tachograph card, and that driving time/rest rules are complied with
4. **Posting compliance warranty** — subcarrier warrants compliance with all applicable posting requirements
5. **No further subcontracting** — without written consent of contracting carrier (to prevent multi-tier chains without visibility)
6. **Indemnity clause** — subcarrier indemnifies contracting carrier for claims arising from subcarrier's breach
7. **Right of recourse** — explicit statement that contracting carrier may recover from subcarrier amounts paid to shipper
8. **Data and documentation** — subcarrier must provide CMR copy, tachograph data, and incident reports promptly

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence |
|-----------|------------|
| Contracting carrier fails to pay CMR claim (due to uninsured exposure) | Shipper enforces judgment; operator's financial standing undermined; risk to Community Licence |
| Using unlicensed subcarrier | EU Regulation 1071/2009 violation; fine under national law; reputational and regulatory risk |
| Subcarrier's drivers without valid CPC | National penalty applies; contracting carrier may share liability for deploying |
| Failure to verify posting compliance for cross-border subcontracting | Host state labour authority fine (varies by country — see posting-workers.md) |
| LkSG violation (DE companies above threshold) | BAFA fine up to 2% annual global turnover; public procurement exclusion |
| CSDDD violation (post-2027 for applicable companies) | Civil liability for damages; national regulatory fine |
