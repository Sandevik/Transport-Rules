## Overview

There is **no harmonised EU directive on HGV weekend or holiday driving bans** — these are entirely national matters governed by each member state's road traffic law. However, most Western and Central European countries impose some form of restriction on heavy goods vehicles (typically >7.5t or >12t GVW) during weekends, public holidays, and sometimes nights. These bans aim to reduce congestion, improve road safety, and give roads back to leisure traffic during peak holiday periods.

Understanding driving bans is essential for route planning and schedule compliance. Violations result in fines and vehicle immobilisation, and permits can sometimes be obtained for specific exempt operations.

## Common Exemptions

Most countries with driving bans allow exemptions for:

| Exemption category | Notes |
|---|---|
| Perishable goods | Fresh food, chilled/frozen, live animals, cut flowers |
| Press and postal | Newspapers, time-sensitive mail |
| Fuel and energy | Heating oil, petrol, electricity sector vehicles |
| Agricultural harvest | Seasonal harvest transport (limited periods) |
| Emergency/breakdown | Vehicles responding to or recovering from breakdowns |
| Military and emergency services | Police, fire, ambulance, civil defence |
| Combined transport | Road legs of combined transport (rail/sea connections) |
| Specific industries | Oil industry, dangerous goods (some classes) |
| Humanitarian/disaster relief | With special permission |

**Exemption permits** must typically be obtained in advance from the national road/transport authority. Emergency exemptions may be issued at short notice.

---

## Public Holidays

Driving bans typically extend to **public holidays** (national, regional, and sometimes also regional/local holidays). Holiday calendars vary by country. In federal countries (e.g., Germany, Austria), **state-level (Länder) public holidays** also trigger driving bans in that state.

See individual country driving-bans.md files for precise fine amounts and current holiday schedules.

---

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Operating HGV in violation of driving ban | Fixed fine (country-specific amounts); vehicle may be immobilised at roadside until ban period ends |
| Operating without valid exemption permit | Fine + seizure of permit fee |
| Repeat offences | Escalated fines; possible prosecution |
