## Overview

There is **no EU-harmonised blood alcohol content (BAC) limit specifically for professional or HGV drivers**. EU Council Recommendation 2001/115/EC suggested a general limit of **0.5‰ BAC** for all drivers across EU member states, but this is non-binding and professional driver limits are entirely a matter of national law.

In practice, most EU member states apply **stricter BAC limits** for professional drivers (categories C, CE, D, DE and holders of professional licences) compared to general drivers. Several countries apply a **zero-tolerance** or near-zero limit (0.0‰ or 0.2‰) for drivers operating commercially.

This file covers:
1. Country-by-country BAC limits for professional/HGV drivers
2. Drug testing practices and substances tested
3. Post-accident mandatory testing requirements
4. Prescribed medication and impairment
5. Penalties

## Drug Testing — Roadside Practice

### Testing Equipment

EU roadside drug testing is conducted using **saliva-based screening devices**. Commonly used devices include:

- **DrugWipe** (Securetec) — rapid saliva swab test
- **Dräger DrugTest 5000** — saliva test with on-site readout (also used as evidential level in some jurisdictions)
- **Alere DDS2** — portable saliva-based immunoassay

### Substances Routinely Tested

| Substance | Detection window (saliva) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **THC (cannabis)** | 4–8 hours after use | Frequent cannabis users may test positive longer |
| **Cocaine / benzoylecgonine** | 1–2 days | Rapid onset; short saliva window |
| **Amphetamines (including MDMA)** | 1–3 days | Includes speed, ecstasy |
| **Methamphetamine** | 1–3 days | |
| **Opiates (morphine, heroin, codeine)** | 1–2 days | Codeine from medication may trigger; disclose prescriptions |
| **Benzodiazepines** | 1–3 days | Includes diazepam, lorazepam — available on prescription |

Most EU jurisdictions treat **any positive saliva test** as grounds for a blood test. A confirmed blood test positive is the evidential standard for prosecution.

### Confirmatory Blood Testing

Roadside saliva test is a **screening test** only. A positive result leads to a blood draw (either at roadside by medic or at a police station/hospital). The blood test is the legally admissible evidence for prosecution.

## Post-Accident Mandatory Testing

Several EU member states require **mandatory blood testing** after road accidents involving injury or death:

| Country | Mandatory post-accident testing | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| **FR** France | Yes — blood test mandatory after injury accident | Code de la route Art. L234-9 |
| **DE** Germany | Yes — blood test mandatory after accidents with personal injury | §81a StPO |
| **PL** Poland | Yes — mandatory after injury accidents | Kodeks postępowania karnego Art. 74 |
| **SE** Sweden | Yes — police may order blood test at any stop; routinely done post-accident | Lagen om åtgärder mot rattfylleri |
| **GB** United Kingdom | Yes — blood/urine test required if impairment suspected after accident | Road Traffic Act 1988 §7 |

## Prescribed Medication and Impairment

Drivers taking prescribed medication that may cause **drowsiness, impaired reaction time, or altered judgement** face prosecution for impaired driving even if their BAC is below the legal limit. This includes:

| Medication class | Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioid analgesics** (morphine, tramadol, codeine) | High — drowsiness, impaired reaction | Professional drivers should consult prescribing doctor; some opioids are incompatible with commercial driving |
| **Benzodiazepines** (diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam) | High — sedation, impaired coordination | Even therapeutic doses may impair; night-time use may carry over |
| **Sleep aids** (zolpidem, zopiclone) | High — carry-over sedation | Must not drive within 8–12 hours of use |
| **Antihistamines with sedative effect** (diphenhydramine, chlorphenamine) | Moderate–high | Non-sedating antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine) generally acceptable |
| **Antiepileptics** | Moderate | Drowsiness side effect; if driver is on antiepileptics, also assess whether underlying condition allows Group 2 driving |
| **Antidepressants (SSRI/SNRI)** | Low–moderate | Modern SSRIs generally accepted; tricyclic antidepressants carry sedation risk |

**Key principle:** The obligation is on the driver to ensure they are fit to drive. "My doctor prescribed it" is not a defence to impaired driving. Drivers should carry documentation of prescribed medication and be aware of interactions.

## Penalties

### Penalty Overview by Country

| Country | BAC 0.2–0.5‰ | BAC 0.5–0.8‰ | BAC > 0.8‰ / criminal level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SE** | 0.2–1.0‰: fine + possible licence suspension; 1.0‰+: imprisonment up to 2 years, licence revoked | — | Grovt rattfylleri ≥ 1.0‰: imprisonment 6 months–2 years; licence revoked min. 1 year |
| **DE** | General 0.5–1.6‰: OWi fine €500, 1 month ban, 2 points; Professional 0.0‰+: OWi fine from €500; > 1.6‰ criminal | €500–€1,500 OWi | Criminal: Trunkenheitsfahrt (§316 StGB): up to 1 year imprisonment; licence withdrawn |
| **AT** | 0.1–0.5‰ (professional): fine €300+, licence suspension | Fine €800–€3,700 | Above 1.2‰: criminal; fine up to €5,900 |
| **FR** | 0.2–0.5‰: immediate licence suspension possible; fine €135 | €135 fixed fine + 6-point deduction | Above 0.8‰: criminal; up to 2 years imprisonment, €4,500 fine; licence suspension 3 years |
| **GB** | Below 80 mg/100ml: no offence (statutory) | 80 mg/100ml+: drink drive offence | Criminal: up to 6 months imprisonment, unlimited fine, 12-month disqualification minimum. HGV licence lost. |
| **PL** | 0.2–0.5‰: wykroczenie — up to 30 days arrest or fine PLN 2,500 | 0.5‰+: przestępstwo — criminal offence | Imprisonment up to 3 years; driving ban 1–15 years; licence forfeited |
| **NO** | 0.2–0.5‰: fine (1.5 × monthly salary) + 1 year licence suspension | Fine + suspension | Above 1.2‰: up to 30 days imprisonment; above 1.5‰: unconditional imprisonment |

### Additional Consequences for Professional Drivers

Beyond criminal/administrative penalties, professional drivers face:

- **Immediate loss of HGV/PSV driving entitlement** upon conviction for drink-drive
- **CPC disqualification** in many jurisdictions — a drink-drive conviction may trigger revocation of the Driver CPC certificate
- **Employer notification** — police and licensing authorities typically notify the driver's employer and transport undertaking
- **Transport operator licence risk** — repeated driver offences may trigger a review of the operator's good repute under Regulation 1071/2009
