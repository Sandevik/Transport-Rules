# EU — Enforcement of Road Transport Social Legislation

**Framework:** Directive 2006/22/EC (as amended by Regulation 2020/1054). Sets minimum conditions for enforcement of Regulations 561/2006 (driving/rest times) and 165/2014 (tachographs) in EU member states.

**Sources:**
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32006L0022 — Directive 2006/22/EC full text
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32020R1054 — Regulation 2020/1054 (Mobility Package enforcement amendments)

**Last verified: 2026-05-31**

---

## Purpose

Directive 2006/22/EC ensures a minimum level of enforcement across all EU member states and creates the framework for cross-border enforcement cooperation. Without it, uneven enforcement would distort competition between carriers registered in strictly and leniently enforcing countries.

---

## Minimum Check Requirements

Member states must perform a minimum number of roadside checks annually:

| Minimum threshold | Requirement |
|------------------|------------|
| Working days to be checked at roadside | At least **3% of all working days** driven by vehicles subject to 561/2006 |
| Premises inspections | At least **30%** of total working days checked must be at undertaking premises |

Checks must cover:
- Driving periods, breaks, and rest times
- Tachograph data (current day + preceding 28 days)
- Weekly rest
- Return-home obligations (Mobility Package)
- Undertaking scheduling practices

---

## ERRU — Enterprise Risk Rating

Directive 2006/22/EC established **ERRU** (European Register of Road Transport Undertakings) for cross-border risk rating:

- Each undertaking is assigned a risk classification (high/medium/low) based on infringement frequency and severity
- Risk classification is shared across EU member states via ERRU
- **High-risk operators** are subject to more frequent roadside checks
- An undertaking's risk rating in Sweden affects how their vehicles are treated at the roadside in Germany, France, etc.

---

## Infringement Classification

The Mobility Package (Regulation 2020/1054) standardised infringement categories across the EU:

| Category | Description | Typical examples |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Most Serious Infringement (MSI)** | Highest severity; direct impact on risk rating | Tachograph manipulation; driving time exceeded >50%; no tachograph |
| **Very Serious Infringement (VSI)** | Significant; logged in ERRU | Driving >2h over limit; rest <50% of required; no driver card |
| **Serious Infringement (SI)** | Logged; contributes to risk rating | Driving 1-2h over; missing printouts; incorrect mode entries |
| **Minor Infringement (MI)** | Administrative; handled locally | Incomplete records; minor administrative errors |

## Roadside Check Procedure

During a roadside inspection, enforcement officers examine:

1. **Driver card** — validity, correct usage, not expired or suspended
2. **Tachograph data** — current day + preceding 28 calendar days
3. **Driving periods** — verify against daily (9/10 h) and weekly (56 h) limits
4. **Breaks** — 45-minute break after ≤4h30min driving
5. **Daily rest** — 11-hour (or reduced 9-hour) daily rest compliance
6. **Weekly rest** — 45-hour regular or 24-hour reduced weekly rest
7. **Manual entries** — completeness and accuracy
8. **Return-home obligation** — driver returned home within 4 consecutive weeks (Mobility Package)
9. **Vehicle tachograph** — seals intact, calibration current, no signs of tampering

---

## Immobilisation

Enforcement officers may **immobilise the vehicle** if:
- The driver has insufficient rest to continue safely
- Tachograph tampering is suspected
- The driver poses an immediate road safety risk

The vehicle must be allowed to move once the issue is resolved (sufficient rest taken, inspection complete). Immobilisation solely for non-payment of fines is not permitted — fines are collected through administrative processes.

## What to Expect at a Roadside Check — Driver Perspective

### When you are signalled to stop

1. Pull over safely and completely — do not stop on the hard shoulder of a motorway unless directed to
2. Remain in the cab until the officer approaches — some countries require you to exit; follow the officer's instructions
3. Have the following ready before the officer reaches the window:

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Driver card | Must be in the tachograph slot; hand over for inspection |
| Tachograph printouts | For current day and any days the card was not used (up to 56 days lookback) |
| Driving licence | Including any categories (C, CE, D) |
| ADR certificate | If carrying dangerous goods |
| CPC qualification card | Proof of periodic training |
| Vehicle registration document | |
| Operator's licence / Community licence | Typically in the cab — a certified copy is sufficient |
| CMR waybill | For international freight |
| Cabotage documentation | If operating cabotage — dates, consignments, prior international journey |
| Attestation form | For periods not recorded by the tachograph (e.g. sick leave, annual leave) |

4. Answer questions honestly — providing false information is a separate and more serious offence
5. You have the right to request that the officer's findings and any fine notice be issued in writing before you sign anything

### If the officer finds a violation

- The officer will classify the infringement (MI / SI / VSI / MSI — see Infringement Classification above)
- For minor infringements: typically a warning or a locally collectible fine
- For serious or very serious infringements: a fine may be collectible at the roadside (advance payment / security deposit) in most EU member states
- For most serious infringements (tachograph manipulation, driving time exceeded >50%): the vehicle may be immobilised until the situation is resolved
- You are entitled to receive written documentation of any infringement found and any amount collected

### Advance payment / security deposits

Most EU member states permit or require payment of a security deposit at the roadside for serious violations by non-resident drivers. This is not the final fine — it is a guarantee that the fine will be paid. The actual decision is made later by the competent authority.

- **AETR exception:** On journeys governed by AETR (routes through non-EU AETR countries), advance roadside payment is **prohibited** under AETR Art. 22ter.
- If you cannot pay (no card, no cash), the officer may hold the vehicle as security in some countries — know the rules for each country on your route.

### After the check

- Keep all documentation issued by the officer
- Inform your dispatcher or employer immediately after a serious finding
- Your employer must log the infringement and review scheduling if the violation resulted from a planning error

## Appeals and Dispute Procedures

Each EU member state provides an administrative appeals process for contested roadside fines. The process differs by country, but the general framework is:

1. **Request written documentation** at the roadside — you cannot contest what is not documented
2. **Do not sign a document you do not understand** — request a translation or at minimum a verbal explanation; signing a payment receipt does not waive appeal rights in most countries
3. **File an appeal** with the competent authority of the country where the infringement was issued, within the statutory deadline (varies: typically 14–30 days from issue date)
4. **In your home country:** Infringements issued abroad are reported to your home country authority; the home country authority handles follow-up. Contact your national transport authority if you receive a cross-border enforcement notice.

For country-specific appeal procedures and fine amounts, see the relevant country `penalties.md` file.

---

## Cross-Border Cooperation

- **ERRU** enables real-time sharing of undertaking risk ratings
- Enforcement authorities can request data from the country of establishment
- Infringements detected in one EU country can be reported to the undertaking's home country authority for follow-up action against the operator
