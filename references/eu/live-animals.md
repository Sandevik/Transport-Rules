# EU Live Animal Transport — Regulation EC 1/2005

**Sources:**
- Regulation (EC) No 1/2005 — on the protection of animals during transport and related operations
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32005R0001
- Directive 91/628/EEC — original animal transport directive (repealed by 1/2005)

**Last verified: 2026-03-19**

---

## Overview

**Regulation (EC) No 1/2005** sets EU-wide rules for the transport of live vertebrate animals in connection with economic activity. It covers:
- All species of vertebrate animals transported commercially
- All stages of transport including loading, transit, and unloading
- Both domestic and international transport within/between member states

The regulation is directly applicable in all member states (no national transposition needed for the core rules).

---

## Scope

### Applies to:
- Transport of live vertebrate animals in connection with **economic activity**
- Transport operators, drivers, attendants, dealers, and markets
- Transport within and between EU member states

### Does NOT apply to:
- Transport by farmers of their own animals using their own vehicles (for farming purposes) for short journeys
- Transport of pets with owners (non-commercial)
- Registered equidae (horses) going to/from veterinary treatment

---

## Key Requirements

### 1. Authorisation of transporters

**Short journeys (≤8 hours):** Transporter must hold a **Type 1 authorisation** from the national competent authority.
**Long journeys (>8 hours):** Transporter must hold a **Type 2 authorisation** — more stringent requirements.

Both types valid for 5 years, issued by the national competent authority (e.g. Jordbruksverket in Sweden, BLE in Germany, APHA in the UK).

### 2. Driver and attendant certificate

Drivers and attendants of animals must hold a **certificate of competence** covering:
- Animal physiology, behaviour, and welfare needs
- Handling and loading/unloading techniques
- Emergency care
- Record keeping

Certificate issued by approved training bodies, recognised across EU.

### 3. Means of transport requirements

Vehicles must:
- Have adequate ventilation (adjustable for temperature and stocking density)
- Have non-slip floors
- Have drainage where appropriate
- Have adequate space (see stocking densities below)
- Be equipped to provide water and feed
- For long journeys: GPS tracking and automated climate control

### 4. Fitness for transport

Animals must be **fit for transport**. Animals that are:
- Sick or injured
- Heavily pregnant (within 10% of gestation for long journeys)
- Newborn (umbilical cord not yet healed)

...must NOT be transported (or only with veterinary certificate under specific conditions).

## Journey Time Limits

### Solipeds (horses, donkeys, mules), cattle, sheep, goats, pigs

#### Pigs
| Stage | Duration |
|-------|---------|
| Maximum journey | **24 hours** (with water available at all times) |
| After 24 hours | 24-hour rest + water + feed before continuing |

#### Cattle, sheep, goats (unweaned young)
| Stage | Duration |
|-------|---------|
| Travel | **9 hours** |
| Rest | **1 hour** (water + if necessary, feed) |
| Further travel | **9 hours** |

#### Cattle, sheep, goats (adults)
| Stage | Duration |
|-------|---------|
| Long journey maximum | **29 hours** travel time |
| After 29 hours | 24-hour rest required at approved station |

#### Horses (registered equidae for competition etc.)
- Up to **24 hours** without rest if watered every 8 hours and fed

#### Poultry, domestic birds, domestic rabbits
- Maximum **12 hours** without rest (then 24 hours rest, water, feed)

## Documentation for Long Journeys (>8 hours)

A **journey log** must be prepared and approved by the competent authority before departure. The journey log covers:

1. **Place of departure** and intended place of destination
2. **Estimated journey times** and rest points
3. **Veterinary certificates** (for cross-border transport)
4. **Vehicle** details and transporter authorisation number
5. **Animals:** species, number, age, weight (where relevant)

Journey log signed off at:
- Departure
- Each checking point / rest stop
- Destination

---

## Temperature Rules

During transport, temperature inside the vehicle must be maintained:
- **Between 5°C and 30°C** (±5°C tolerance)
- For long journeys: automatic climate control with alarm required

If temperature exceeds limits: journey must be interrupted and animals rested.

---

## Enforcement

Under Regulation (EC) 1/2005 Art. 22, the **competent authority** of each member state is responsible for enforcement. Checks are conducted at loading points, border posts, markets, and at roadside. Officers examine:
- Transporter authorisation (Type 1 or Type 2)
- Driver/attendant certificate of competence
- Journey log completeness and accuracy
- Vehicle fitness (space, ventilation, bedding, water/feed availability)
- Animal condition and fitness to continue transport

**Consequences of violations:**
- Revocation or suspension of transporter authorisation (Art. 26)
- Administrative fines under national law
- Criminal prosecution for serious animal welfare violations under national animal welfare legislation
- Vehicle may be stopped until animals are given adequate rest, food, water, and veterinary attention

For country-specific enforcement bodies, see the relevant country file.

## What to Do if an Animal Becomes Ill or Injured During Transport

**Legal basis:** Regulation (EC) 1/2005 Art. 22(2) — transporters must take appropriate measures for sick or injured animals.

### Immediate steps

1. **Stop at the nearest suitable place** — do not continue transporting an animal that is clearly unable to continue the journey without suffering
2. **Provide first aid** — basic measures: ensure the animal can access water, is not being trampled, is not in extreme temperature conditions
3. **Call a veterinarian** — mandatory if the animal's condition requires it; the TRACES system or national authority can provide emergency contacts
4. **Notify the national competent authority** — required for serious injury or death; the local authority for the region where the incident occurs is responsible
5. **Record in the journey log** — document: the condition observed, action taken, time, location, veterinarian contacted

### When to stop the journey entirely

The journey must be interrupted, and animals unloaded, rested, and attended to, if:
- Any animal cannot stand unaided
- An animal has an open wound that is not being treated
- Temperature conditions cannot be maintained within required limits
- Animals have been without water or feed for longer than permitted (see journey time limits)

### If an animal dies during transport

1. Isolate the carcass from other animals where possible
2. Record in the journey log: time, animal identity, cause if known
3. Notify the competent authority at the destination or next stopping point
4. Do not proceed to the original destination with the carcass unless authorised — contact the consignor and the receiving establishment

### Who to contact

Each EU member state has a competent authority for animal welfare in transport (typically the agricultural or food safety ministry). At roadside, local police or official veterinarians can assist in contacting the relevant authority.
