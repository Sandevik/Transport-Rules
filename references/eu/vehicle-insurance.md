## Overview

All motor vehicles normally based in the EU must be covered by **Motor Third-Party Liability (MTPL) insurance**. This is mandatory under Article 3 of Directive 2009/103/EC and must extend throughout all EU member states on a single premium basis. The 2021 recast (Directive 2021/2118/EU) significantly raised minimum coverage amounts and clarified Green Card obligations between EU and EEA states.

For commercial road transport operators, MTPL is distinct from and complementary to:
- **CMR cargo insurance** — covers the carrier's liability to the shipper for goods loss or damage
- **Own-damage insurance** (Kasko/comprehensive) — covers the vehicle itself

MTPL covers liability to **third parties** (other road users, pedestrians, property owners) arising from vehicle accidents. It is mandatory regardless of whether the vehicle is operated commercially or privately.

## Minimum Coverage Amounts

### After 2021 Recast (Directive 2021/2118/EU) — Applicable from 23 December 2023

| Coverage type | EU minimum |
|---|---|
| Personal injury | **€6,450,000 per accident** (or €1,300,000 per injured party if higher) |
| Property damage | **€1,300,000 per accident** |

The Commission reviews and adjusts these amounts every five years against the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP). The next review is due 2026.

### Original 2009/103/EC Minimums (superseded after 23 Dec 2023)

| Coverage type | EU minimum |
|---|---|
| Personal injury | €1,000,000 per victim or €5,000,000 per claim |
| Property damage | €1,000,000 per claim |

### National Minimums Exceeding EU Minimum

Several member states require or provide coverage well above the EU floor:

| Country | Personal injury | Property damage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| DE | Unlimited | €100M (practical limit) | PflVG § 4; in practice insurers cover unlimited; property capped at €100M by some policies |
| SE | Unlimited | Unlimited | Trafikskadelagen 1975:1410 — no statutory cap |
| FR | Unlimited | Unlimited | Code des assurances L421-1; no cap for bodily injury |
| GB | Unlimited for death/injury (in practice) | No minimum | Road Traffic Act 1988 s. 145; minimum £1.2M for emergency treatment |
| AT | €6M+ per accident | €1.2M+ | Exceeds EU floor |
| CH | CHF 100M per event | CHF 100M per event | Swiss law (not EU; own framework) |

## Green Card System

### What Is the Green Card?

The **International Motor Insurance Certificate (Green Card)** is a standardised document, originally printed on green paper, issued by national insurance bureaux to prove that a vehicle carries at least the minimum MTPL required in the visited country. It is part of the **IVR (International Organisation of Vehicle Insurers)** / **Council of Bureaux** multilateral system established under the Geneva-based UNECE framework (1949).

### Green Card Within EU/EEA: No Longer Required (from 2021)

Under **Directive 2021/2118/EU, Article 22**, EU member states **may not require** a Green Card as proof of insurance for vehicles normally based in another EU or EEA member state. A national insurance certificate or any other proof of valid MTPL insurance is sufficient.

**In practice within the EU/EEA:**
- No Green Card required at borders between EU/EEA countries
- Insurance must still be in force — the obligation is on the insurer to cover the full EU territory under a single premium (Art. 14 MID)
- Enforcement authorities (e.g., police, customs at some eastern border crossings) may request proof of valid insurance — a national insurance certificate or policy document is acceptable

### When the Green Card IS Still Required

The Green Card remains **mandatory** when entering or transiting:

| Region / Countries | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Turkey (TR)** | Full Green Card required; also requires Turkish compulsory insurance (trafik sigortası) at border if not covered |
| **Ukraine (UA)** | Green Card required |
| **Belarus (BY)** | Green Card required (subject to sanctions and border status) |
| **Serbia (RS)** | Green Card required |
| **Montenegro (ME)** | Green Card required |
| **North Macedonia (MK)** | Green Card required |
| **Morocco (MA)** | Green Card required |
| **Georgia (GE)** | Green Card required |
| **Moldova (MD)** | Green Card required |
| **Albania (AL)** | Green Card required |

### Green Card: Practical Details

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Issued by | National insurance bureau or insurer on behalf of bureau |
| Validity | 1 month to 12 months (insurer's choice, within policy period) |
| Format | Standardised IVR format; can now be printed (no longer must be green) |
| Country codes | Countries of validity shown as codes; driver must have codes for all countries to be transited |
| Cost | Usually included in policy; sometimes small admin fee |
| If no Green Card at border | May be refused entry or required to purchase frontier/border insurance |

## MTPL Document — What to Carry

| Situation | Document required |
|---|---|
| Driving within EU/EEA | National insurance certificate (no Green Card required) |
| Crossing into TR, UA, BY, RS, etc. | Green Card showing those country codes as valid |
| Roadside check (any country) | Proof of valid insurance (certificate, Green Card, or digital equivalent) |

**Note:** Schengen internal borders do not routinely check insurance, but **eastern EU border crossings** (HU-UA, PL-BY, FI-RU, SK-UA) perform thorough checks including insurance validity.

## CMR Cargo Insurance vs. MTPL: Key Distinction

Commercial transport operators often carry both types of insurance, which serve entirely different purposes:

| | MTPL (Vehicle TPL) | CMR Cargo Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | Third-party bodily injury and property damage caused by the vehicle | Carrier's liability to the shipper for goods loss, damage, or delay |
| Legal basis | Directive 2009/103/EC (mandatory) | CMR Convention Art. 17 (carrier's liability); insured voluntarily |
| Who benefits | Third parties injured by the vehicle | Shipper/consignee of the goods |
| Limit (EU floor) | €6.45M personal / €1.3M property | 8.33 SDR/kg (CMR Art. 23) |
| Mandatory | Yes — vehicle cannot be used on roads without it | Not legally mandatory but commercially standard |

Cross-reference: `references/eu/cmr-liability.md`

## Motor Insurers Bureau / Uninsured Drivers Fund

Every EU member state operates a **Motor Insurers Bureau (MIB)** or equivalent compensation body. These bodies:
- Compensate victims of accidents caused by **uninsured vehicles**
- Compensate victims of accidents caused by **untraced (hit-and-run) vehicles**
- Act as the national guaranteeing body for the Green Card system
- Handle cross-border claims where the insurer is based in another member state

**Key bureaux:**
- DE: Verkehrsopferhilfe (VOH) / GDV
- SE: Trafikförsäkringsföreningen (TFF)
- FR: Fonds de Garantie des Assurances Obligatoires (FGAO)
- GB: Motor Insurers Bureau (MIB)
- PL: Polskie Biuro Ubezpieczycieli Komunikacyjnych (PBUK)
- IE: Motor Insurers Bureau of Ireland (MIBI)

## Claims Procedures

### European Accident Statement (EAS)

The **European Accident Statement (Constat Amiable)** is a standardised two-page form accepted in all EU countries. Both drivers involved complete and sign one form; each driver retains a copy. The form captures:
- Driver and vehicle details of both parties
- Insurer details
- Sketch of the accident scene
- Description of circumstances (tick-box system)
- Witness details

Available free from insurers and national insurance bureaux in all EU languages. Cross-reference: `references/eu/road-accident-procedures.md`

### Claims Process

1. Complete EAS form at scene (if safe)
2. Photograph damage, scene, licence plates, skid marks
3. Note witness details
4. Notify insurer **immediately** (within 24–48 hours; policy conditions vary)
5. Submit claim to insurer in the country where the accident occurred, or through national MIB if the at-fault vehicle is uninsured
6. Time limit: **3 years** from the date of accident in most EU countries

### Direct Claim vs. Green Card Bureau Claim

- **Within EU:** Claim directly to the at-fault vehicle's insurer, or to their appointed claims representative in your home country (required under Art. 21 MID — each insurer must appoint a claims representative in each member state)
- **Non-EU AETR countries:** Claim through the Green Card Bureau of the country where the accident occurred; the bureau pursues recovery through the at-fault insurer

## Penalties

MTPL violations are enforced under national law; EU law sets the obligation but not the penalty:

| Country | Sanction for uninsured driving |
|---|---|
| SE | Trafikförsäkringsavgift — a penalty fee charged by TFF; vehicle may be impounded |
| DE | Fine up to €5,000 + licence suspension; imprisonment possible for wilful evasion |
| FR | Fine up to €3,750 + vehicle immobilisation/seizure |
| GB | Fixed penalty £300 + 6 penalty points; vehicle seizure; unlimited court fine |
| PL | Fine + mandatory insurance payment to UFG (Ubezpieczeniowy Fundusz Gwarancyjny) |
| EU general | Member states must ensure "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" penalties (MID Art. 26a as inserted by Directive 2021/2118) |

**Operating a commercial vehicle without valid MTPL insurance** also risks:
- Carrier's operating licence (Community Licence) suspension
- Liability to third parties without insurer backing — operator personally liable for all damages
