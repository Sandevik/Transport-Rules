# Inland Waterway Transport — ADN and European River Freight

## Overview

Inland waterway transport (IWT) is regulated at two levels: the **ADN** (European Agreement Concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Inland Waterways) under UNECE, and vessel-specific regimes such as the Rhine regime under the CCNR. For road transport operators, IWT is most relevant as a multimodal leg in combined transport operations and as the mode handling heavy bulk cargo between major ports and inland terminals.

## ADN — European Agreement on International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Inland Waterways

### Scope and Legal Basis

ADN is a UNECE agreement that mirrors ADR (road) and RID (rail) but applies to carriage of dangerous goods on inland waterways — rivers, canals, and lakes. It applies to international carriage but is also incorporated into EU law via **Directive 2008/68/EC**, making it mandatory for domestic IWT carriage within EU member states.

ADN applies to movements on:
- **Rhine** (NL, DE, FR, CH)
- **Danube** (DE, AT, SK, HU, HR, RS, BG, RO)
- **Main–Danube Canal** (connecting Rhine and Danube systems)
- **Elbe** (CZ, DE)
- **Oder** (PL, DE)
- **Belgian canals** (Albert Canal, Brussels–Charleroi Canal)
- **Dutch waterways** (Amsterdam–Rhine Canal, Noord-Zeeland)
- **French rivers** (Seine, Rhône, Moselle)

### Classification System

ADN uses the **same UN numbers, hazard classes, and packing groups** as ADR. The 9 hazard classes are identical:

| Class | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Explosives |
| 2 | Gases |
| 3 | Flammable liquids |
| 4.1/4.2/4.3 | Flammable solids, self-reactive, water-reactive |
| 5.1/5.2 | Oxidising, organic peroxides |
| 6.1/6.2 | Toxic, infectious |
| 7 | Radioactive |
| 8 | Corrosive |
| 9 | Miscellaneous |

Key differences from ADR: **quantity thresholds** are vessel-based (larger quantities permitted per voyage), **vessel-specific equipment** requirements apply (firefighting systems, gas detection, ventilation), and **crew training** requirements differ.

### Vessel Types and Certificates

ADN classifies cargo vessels into three main types:

- **Type N** (dry cargo vessel): general cargo, containers, breakbulk; least restrictive DG requirements
- **Type C** (tank vessel): liquids and gases in bulk; full ADN tank vessel requirements apply; double-hull construction typically required for toxic/flammable liquids
- **Types D/E** (special purpose): dredgers, pushers, floating plants; specific rules per vessel

Every vessel carrying DG under ADN must hold an **ADN Certificate of Approval** issued by the competent authority of the flag state. The certificate specifies which UN numbers and quantities may be carried and any special conditions.

### Crew and Expert Requirements

- Vessels carrying DG must have at least one **ADN Expert** (certificated dangerous goods expert) on board or serving as shore-based responsible person
- ADN Expert certificate: requires training course + examination; valid 5 years; renewal by refresher course
- Crew members in direct contact with DG operations (loading/unloading) must have basic ADN training

## Rhine Regime — CCNR

### Central Commission for Navigation on the Rhine (CCNR)

The CCNR (founded 1815, headquartered in Strasbourg) is the oldest international organisation in the world. It governs navigation on the Rhine under the **Mannheim Convention (1868)**. All commercial vessels on the Rhine must comply with both ADN and the Rhine-specific regulations.

Key CCNR regulations:
- **Rhine Vessel Inspection Regulation (RheinSchUO / RVIR)**: technical standards for hull, stability, machinery, fire protection, crew accommodation; issues the **Rhine Certificate** (Schiffsattest/Certificat de Visite)
- **Police Regulations for the Navigation of the Rhine (RPR)**: traffic rules, collision avoidance, speed limits, passing rules
- **Regulations for Rhine Navigation Personnel (RPN)**: crew qualifications; the **Rhine Patent** (Rheinpatent/Brevet du Rhin) is required for the vessel master

### Rhine Certificate and Patent

- **Rhine Certificate**: issued after technical inspection; comparable to a vehicle registration/roadworthiness certificate; must be carried on board; renewed periodically
- **Rhine Patent**: professional qualification for vessel master on the Rhine; requires inland navigation experience, knowledge of Rhine-specific regulations, and examination; separate from the general EU boatmaster licence
- Since 2022, the EU **Boatmaster Certificate** (Directive 2017/2397) provides a harmonised EU-wide qualification; Rhine Patent remains required for Rhine-specific segments

### AIS and Digital Requirements

- All Rhine commercial vessels: mandatory **AIS** (Automatic Identification System) transponder
- **ECDIS** (Electronic Chart Display and Information System) increasingly mandatory; Rhine ECDIS uses IEHG-standard river charts
- **ERI** (Electronic Reporting International): electronic voyage reporting system; replaces paper voyage reports on Rhine and connected waterways

## Key Corridors and Routes

### Rhine/Main/Danube Corridor

- **Rotterdam → Rhine → Main → Main-Danube Canal → Danube → Black Sea**: approximately 3,500 km; connects North Sea ports to Black Sea
- **Rotterdam → Duisburg**: busiest inland waterway corridor in Europe; Duisburg (Duisport) is the largest inland port in the world
- **Duisburg → Frankfurt/Main**: via Rhine/Main; heavy industry and chemical industry traffic

### Rhine/Albert Canal/Seine (Belgium/France)

- **Antwerp → Brussels → France** (via Albert Canal, Brussels-Charleroi Canal, and connecting French waterways)
- **Paris–Le Havre** via Seine: French domestic waterway freight

### Danube Corridor

- **Vienna (AT) → Budapest (HU) → Belgrade (RS) → Constanta (RO/Black Sea)**: Danube Main TEN-T Corridor; bulk cargo, container barge services
- **Danube Commission**: separate international body governing Danube; Belgrade Convention (1948)

## Combined Transport Interface

Under **EU Directive 92/106/EEC**, an inland waterway leg qualifies a shipment as **combined transport**:
- Road legs serving an IWT leg are exempt from national cabotage restrictions during the combined transport operation
- The waterway leg must be at least **100 km** (same threshold as rail leg for combined transport)
- Container barges, swap body barges, and RORO barges all qualify
- Documentation: standard CMR for road leg; inland waterway transport document (CIM-type or carrier's own waybill) for water leg; through bill of lading (FIATA FBL) can cover the entire multimodal journey

---

## Loading Units on Barges

- **ISO containers** (20'/40'): most common unit; standard container barges carry 200–400 TEU per voyage
- **Swap bodies**: European standard swap bodies (C-series 7.15–13.6m) can be loaded on barges using terminal cranes; not stackable in the same way as ISO containers
- **Semi-trailers on push-barges (RORO barges)**: trailer rolled on/off via ramp; operating on selected Rhine and Moselle sections
- **Weight**: vessel deck load limits vary; large Rhine push-barges (185m × 11.4m) can carry up to 3,000 tonnes; weight is rarely the constraint for container barges but may be for heavy project cargo

## Environmental Rules — CDNI Convention

The **CDNI Convention** (Convention on the Collection, Deposit and Reception of Waste Generated during Navigation on the Rhine and Inland Waterways, 1996; entry into force 2009) regulates:
- **Bilge water**: must be collected in certified reception facilities; prohibited to pump overboard
- **Sewage**: toilet waste collection at certified stations; no discharge into waterway
- **Domestic waste**: separated collection; disposal at certified facilities
- **Oily waste (cargo residues)**: tank vessels must deliver residues to certified facilities; pre-washing required before loading different cargo; levy system finances reception facilities

CDNI applies to all vessels on the Rhine, Moselle, Main, Saar, Neckar, and connected waterways.

## Port Procedures

### Rotterdam (NL)

- Europe's largest seaport; major river barge hub at Rotterdam City Hub terminals (Waalhaven, Europoort)
- **PortBase**: Rotterdam's port community system; electronic pre-notification of barge calls mandatory
- Customs: EU port; no customs for EU goods; NCTS transit for non-EU goods
- Barge: Rotterdam uses **barge slot management** at deep-sea terminals; pre-booking of barge slots required at major terminals (APM, ECT)

### Antwerp (BE)

- Second largest European port; Antwerp Inland Shipping Notice (AISN) system for pre-notification
- **NxtPort**: Antwerp port community system

### Duisburg (DE)

- World's largest inland port; duisport group operates multiple terminals
- Customs procedures: ATLAS system (German customs); T1 transit documents for non-EU goods

---

## Multimodal Documentation

- **FIATA FBL** (FIATA Negotiable Multimodal Transport Bill of Lading): covers road + waterway + sea legs; issued by freight forwarder; transferable document of title
- **Through CMR**: CMR consignment note extended to cover waterway leg; used when forwarder/carrier accepts responsibility for entire journey; less common than FBL for pure IWT
- **Inland waterway waybill**: non-negotiable carrier waybill for river transport; no standardised EU form but CCNR and Danube Commission have recommended templates

## Penalties

### ADN Violations (via Directive 2008/68/EC)

Enforcement and penalties are set by each member state under national law implementing the directive. Typical penalties:

- **Transport of DG without valid ADN Certificate of Approval**: vessel detained; operator fined; in DE: €500–€5,000 per violation; in NL: €2,000–€10,000
- **Missing or invalid ADN Expert certification**: fine up to €2,000 (NL); vessel may not proceed
- **Quantity limits exceeded**: cargo may be required to be unloaded; fine + detention

### CCNR/Rhine

- Non-compliance with Rhine Certificate requirements: vessel excluded from Rhine navigation; substantial fines by national Rhine authorities (DE Water and Shipping Directorates)
- Missing Rhine Patent: vessel master prohibited from operating; fine up to €5,000 (DE)
- CDNI violations: waste discharge offences can result in criminal prosecution in DE and NL; fines up to €50,000 for serious discharge offences

### EU/TEN-T

- No direct EU-level fines for IWT; enforcement is member state competence
