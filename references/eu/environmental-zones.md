## Overview

There is **no EU-wide mandate requiring cities to establish low emission zones (LEZ)**. Urban access regulations — including low emission zones, zero-emission zones, and restricted traffic zones — are established independently by national governments and/or individual cities. The EU does not harmonise LEZ standards; each scheme defines its own emission criteria, vehicle categories, enforcement methods, and penalty regime.

The result is a fragmented landscape of dozens of different schemes across Europe, making pre-trip compliance checking essential for cross-border HGV operators.

### The Authoritative Lookup Tool

**Urban Access Regulations database: https://www.urbanaccessregulations.eu/**

This database (managed by ERTICO ITS Europe in cooperation with EC DG MOVE) is the definitive source for checking which restrictions apply in which city. It covers:

- Low emission zones (LEZ)
- Zero emission zones (ZEZ)
- Restricted traffic zones (ZTL / access restrictions)
- Congestion charging zones
- Time-based and weight-based access restrictions
- Map view and city search

Always check this database before planning a route through a city you are not familiar with.

## Germany — Umweltzone (Environmental Zone)

### System Overview

Germany operates the **Umweltzone** system under the **35. BImSchV** (35th Federal Immission Control Ordinance). The scheme uses a **colour-coded sticker (Plakette)** that must be displayed on the windscreen to enter designated zones.

### Sticker Colours and Emission Standards

| Sticker colour | Emission standard | Vehicles permitted in zone |
|---|---|---|
| **Green (Grüne Plakette)** | Euro IV petrol / Euro IV+ diesel (DPF) | All green sticker zones |
| **Yellow (Gelbe Plakette)** | Euro II petrol / Euro III diesel | Yellow + red sticker zones |
| **Red (Rote Plakette)** | Euro I petrol / Euro II diesel | Red sticker zones only (rare) |
| **No sticker** | Below Euro I or Euro II (diesel) | **No access** to any Umweltzone |

> **For HGVs (diesel > 3.5 t):** Green sticker requires **Euro IV** or above with a **diesel particulate filter (DPF)**. Most modern HGVs (Euro V, Euro VI) qualify for the green sticker.

### Cities with Umweltzonen

Approximately **70 German cities** operate Umweltzonen, including:

| City | Zone colour permitted | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Berlin** | Green only | Large zone covering inner city |
| **Munich** (München) | Green only | Inner city and airport approach roads |
| **Hamburg** | Green only | Central areas; expanding |
| **Stuttgart** | Green only; **further restrictions** (diesel Euro 5 ban certain days) | Stuttgart also has Feinstaubalarm diesel driving bans |
| **Frankfurt** | Green only | |
| **Cologne** (Köln) | Green only | |
| **Düsseldorf** | Green only | |

### Practical Requirements for Foreign Vehicles

- Foreign-registered HGVs **must obtain a German Umweltzone sticker** (Plakette) before entry. The sticker is issued based on emission standard.
- Stickers are available from: German vehicle inspection stations (TÜV, DEKRA, GTÜ), some petrol stations, and online via official German portals.
- The sticker costs approximately **EUR 5–12**.
- **No sticker on windscreen = no entry** and a fine applies.

### Stuttgart Additional Restrictions

Stuttgart additionally operates diesel driving bans (Fahrverbote) during high particulate days (Feinstaubalarm). During these periods, Euro 5 diesel trucks may also be banned from the city centre. Check local announcements before entering Stuttgart in winter.

## France — ZFE-m (Zone à Faibles Emissions — mobilité)

### System Overview

France operates the **ZFE-m** (Low Emission Mobility Zone) scheme under Décret n°2016-847. Access requires a **Crit'Air vignette** (certificate of air quality) affixed to the windscreen.

### Crit'Air Classification for Trucks

| Crit'Air class | Emission standard (diesel HGV) | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Crit'Air 1** | Euro VI | Best class; access to all ZFE zones |
| **Crit'Air 2** | Euro V | Access to most zones |
| **Crit'Air 3** | Euro IV | **Banned in Paris ZFE from 2025**; limited in other cities |
| **Crit'Air 4** | Euro III | Banned from most major ZFE zones |
| **Crit'Air 5** | Euro II | Banned from all ZFE zones |
| **No vignette** | Below Euro II | Total ban |

### Cities with ZFE-m

All cities with a population > 150,000 were required to implement ZFE by end of 2024 under French law. Currently active ZFE zones include:

| City | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Paris** | ZFE mandatory since 2021; expanding annually; Crit'Air 3 trucks banned from Paris ring road (Boulevard Périphérique) area from 2025; inner Paris: Crit'Air 1–2 only |
| **Lyon** | ZFE active; Crit'Air 3 progressively restricted |
| **Marseille** | ZFE active |
| **Aix-en-Provence** | ZFE linked to Marseille scheme |
| **Nice** | ZFE active |
| **Bordeaux** | ZFE active |
| **Grenoble** | ZFE active; one of the strictest in France |
| **Strasbourg** | ZFE active |
| **Toulouse** | ZFE active |

### How to Obtain a Crit'Air Vignette

- Foreign operators must order vignettes at: **https://www.certificat-air.gouv.fr** (official French government portal)
- Cost: approximately **EUR 3.67** per vignette plus shipping
- Vignette must be affixed to the lower left of the windscreen (inside)
- **No vignette = fine + possible immobilisation**

## Netherlands — Milieuzone and Zero-Emission Zones

### System Overview

The Netherlands is transitioning to **zero-emission inner-city zones** for commercial vehicles. The Dutch approach goes further than the Crit'Air/sticker system by targeting **zero-emission only** for urban logistics.

### Zero-Emission Zone Rollout (from 2025)

| City | Zero-emission inner city zone date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amsterdam** | 2025 | Inner canal ring (within Singelgracht): zero-emission trucks and vans required; older diesel trucks blocked |
| **Utrecht** | 2025 | City centre logistics: zero-emission required |
| **Rotterdam** | 2025 | Port city; ZEZ for last-mile delivery |
| **The Hague** (Den Haag) | 2025 | City centre ZEZ |
| **Eindhoven** | 2025 | |
| **Leiden** | 2025 | |

30+ Dutch cities have committed to ZEZ by 2025. Access for diesel trucks (even Euro VI) will be refused in ZEZ areas. Electric or hydrogen HGVs only.

### Milieuzone (Existing Emission Zone)

Before ZEZ, some Dutch cities had a "Milieuzone" requiring minimum Euro III or Euro V diesel standard. These are being replaced or upgraded to ZEZ.

### Practical Implications for Foreign Operators

- Route planning must **avoid** Amsterdam, Utrecht, Rotterdam city centres unless operating a zero-emission vehicle
- For B2B deliveries in ZEZ areas, operators may need local partnerships with zero-emission vehicle fleets
- Check the urbanaccessregulations.eu database for precise zone boundaries and exemptions

## United Kingdom — ULEZ and Clean Air Zones (CAZ)

### London LEZ (Low Emission Zone)

The **London LEZ** is one of the oldest and most comprehensive LEZs in Europe, operated by **Transport for London (TfL)**.

| Vehicle category | Minimum standard required | Daily charge if non-compliant |
|---|---|---|
| HGV > 3.5 t | **Euro VI** (or Euro V for older schemes) | **£100 per day** (as of 2023) |
| Buses > 5 t | Euro VI | £100 per day |
| Coaches > 5 t | Euro VI | £100 per day |

The London LEZ covers virtually the **entire Greater London area** (inside the M25 motorway is approximate guide — exact boundary shown at tfl.gov.uk/lezlondon).

### London ULEZ (Ultra Low Emission Zone)

The ULEZ applies to **lighter vehicles** (cars and vans up to 3.5 t) within Greater London — not the primary concern for HGV operators, who are covered by LEZ instead.

### UK Clean Air Zones (CAZ)

Outside London, the UK government's **Clean Air Zone framework** requires certain cities to implement vehicle emission standards:

| City | CAZ Class | HGV impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Birmingham** | Class D | Charges for non-compliant HGVs (non-Euro VI diesel) |
| **Bristol** | Class D | Charges for non-compliant HGVs |
| **Bradford** | Class C | Charges |
| **Portsmouth** | Class B | Charges for larger vehicles |
| **Bath** | Class D | Charges |
| **Sheffield** | Class D | Active from 2023 |
| **Newcastle / Gateshead** | Class D | Active |

**Class D** = includes HGVs, buses, taxis, LGVs, and private cars. Most expensive category.

**Checking compliance:** Use the UK government's CAZ checker at https://www.gov.uk/clean-air-zones

## Italy — ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato)

### System Overview

Italian cities use **ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato)** — restricted traffic zones that limit access to a designated area, typically the historic city centre. Entry is controlled by electronic bollards, cameras, and time-based gates.

> **Important:** ZTL is not purely emission-based — it is a **general access restriction**. Even a Euro VI truck may not be permitted in a ZTL zone if it is a commercial vehicle and the zone prohibits commercial traffic during certain hours.

### Key ZTL Systems

| City | ZTL type | HGV impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Milan** | **Area C** + **Area B** | Area B: Euro 4 petrol / Euro 5 diesel minimum required; below = banned; Area C = daily charge €5 for permitted vehicles | Area B covers most of Milan (within Bastioni); HGVs face time restrictions even if compliant |
| **Rome** | Historic centre ZTL | Commercial vehicle access restricted by time window (typically 06:00–18:00 prohibited for through traffic) | Camera-enforced; foreign plates are not exempt |
| **Florence** | Historic ZTL | Very restricted; delivery time windows only (06:00–10:00 typically) | |
| **Venice** | No road access | Vehicles cannot enter the historic island; deliveries by boat | |
| **Bologna** | ZTL active | Historical centre restricted | |
| **Naples** | ZTL + traffic restrictions | Complex scheme; local knowledge essential | |

### Delivery Time Windows

Many ZTL zones allow commercial vehicles **only during specific delivery time windows** (e.g., 06:00–10:00 or 22:00–05:00). Outside these windows, even compliant vehicles are prohibited. Local operators typically handle last-mile distribution.

## Sweden — Miljözon 1, 2, 3

### System Overview

Sweden operates a **three-tier environmental zone system** under **SFS 2018:696** (Förordning om miljözoner). The three zones differ in the emission standards required for access.

### Zone Tiers

| Zone | Required emission standard (diesel HGV) | Who controls zone designation |
|---|---|---|
| **Miljözon 1** | Euro V minimum (for trucks/buses) | Most common; Swedish city centres |
| **Miljözon 2** | Euro VI (stricter) | Tighter urban areas |
| **Miljözon 3** | Zero-emission only | Future designation; not yet in widespread use |

### Swedish Cities with Miljözon

| City | Zone designation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stockholm** | Miljözon 1 | Inner city; trucks ≤ Euro IV excluded |
| **Gothenburg** (Göteborg) | Miljözon 1 | Central districts |
| **Malmö** | Miljözon 1 | Central districts |
| **Uppsala** | Miljözon 1 | City centre |
| **Helsingborg** | Miljözon 1 | |

**Example — Sweden:** Transportstyrelsen manages the Swedish environmental zone (Miljözon) framework. Municipalities must apply to Transportstyrelsen to designate a Miljözon area. Enforcement is by local police (Polisen) and municipal parking enforcement. See `references/countries/se/environment-law.md` for full Swedish detail.

## Norway — Low Emission and Congestion Charge

### Status

Norway is developing low emission zone frameworks, but as of 2026 most restrictions apply via **congestion charges (bomring)** rather than emission-based access bans for HGVs.

| City | Congestion charge | LEZ status |
|---|---|---|
| **Oslo** | Yes — bomring applies to trucks; higher rates for diesel vs. electric | LEZ framework under development |
| **Bergen** | Yes — bomring | LEZ under development |
| **Stavanger** | Yes — bomring | |

Oslo has **emission-based differentiated congestion charges** — Euro VI diesel trucks pay lower bomring rates than older trucks. Zero-emission trucks may be exempt from bomring charges.

## Belgium — Brussels LEZ

### Brussels Low Emission Zone

The **Brussels Capital Region** operates a mandatory LEZ covering the entire Brussels Region (19 municipalities).

| Vehicle type | Minimum standard | Entry ban |
|---|---|---|
| Diesel trucks > 3.5 t (Euro III or below) | Euro IV minimum | **Banned** from Brussels LEZ |
| Diesel trucks Euro IV+ | Permitted | Check current phase-in schedule |
| Petrol trucks | Euro II minimum | Below Euro II banned |

Foreign vehicles are not exempt. Enforcement is by camera (ANPR — Automatic Number Plate Recognition). The Brussels LEZ is progressively tightening each year.

**Check status:** https://mobilite-mobiliteit.brussels/en/lez

## How to Check Before Entry — Practical Guidance

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Check urbanaccessregulations.eu** | Search for the city of destination; confirms zone type, boundaries, and emission requirements |
| **2. Identify your Euro standard** | Confirm from vehicle registration documents or V5/Zulassungsbescheinigung |
| **3. Obtain required vignettes/stickers in advance** | Crit'Air (FR), Umweltzone sticker (DE) — cannot always be obtained on the day |
| **4. Check time windows** | ZTL zones in Italy and other cities may only allow HGV access in specific time slots |
| **5. Check for exemptions** | Essential deliveries, emergency vehicles, or local authority pre-clearance may apply |
| **6. Consider CAZ checker (UK)** | https://www.gov.uk/clean-air-zones for GB zones |
| **7. Contact city logistics office** | For unusual vehicles or loads, directly contact the city's transport authority |

## Penalties

| Country | Typical penalty for non-compliant HGV entry |
|---|---|
| **DE** Germany (Umweltzone) | EUR 80 fine per infraction; possible vehicle immobilisation |
| **FR** France (ZFE without Crit'Air) | EUR 135–375 fine; Crit'Air 3+ trucks: immediate removal from ZFE zone possible |
| **NL** Netherlands (ZEZ) | EUR 350+ per infraction (municipal fine) |
| **GB** United Kingdom (London LEZ) | £100/day non-compliance charge; failure to pay = £500 penalty charge notice (PCN) |
| **GB** United Kingdom (UK CAZ) | £100/day charge; failure to pay = escalating PCN up to £1,000 |
| **IT** Italy (ZTL) | EUR 80–300 per infraction; foreign-registered vehicles tracked by ANPR; fines sent to home country via EU mutual enforcement |
| **SE** Sweden (Miljözon) | SEK 1,000–5,000 per infraction (sanktionsavgift) |
| **BE** Belgium (Brussels LEZ) | EUR 350 per infraction |

> **Foreign vehicle enforcement:** EU Directive 2011/82/EU requires mutual exchange of vehicle registration data for traffic offences. Fines issued in one EU member state can be enforced against foreign-registered vehicles. German Umweltzone and Italian ZTL fines are routinely forwarded to home country authorities.
