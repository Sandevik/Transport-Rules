## Overview

EU Directive 2006/126/EC (the Third Driving Licence Directive) sets minimum medical fitness standards for all licence holders through its **Annex III**. Group 2 licences — covering categories **C, CE, C1, C1E** (goods vehicles) and **D, DE, D1, D1E** (passenger vehicles) — are subject to significantly stricter requirements than Group 1 (private/light vehicle) licences, because Group 2 drivers operate heavy commercial vehicles on public roads with increased public safety implications.

The Directive establishes **minimum standards**. Member states may apply stricter requirements but may not issue Group 2 licences to applicants who fail the EU minima.

**Group 2 licence categories for HGV/professional drivers:**

| Category | Vehicle type | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Goods vehicles > 3.5 t GVW (rigid) | All commercial truck drivers |
| **CE** | C + trailer > 750 kg (articulated lorry) | Most common HGV combination |
| **C1** | Goods vehicles 3.5–7.5 t | Medium trucks, vans |
| **C1E** | C1 + trailer, combination ≤ 12 t | Medium combination |
| **D** | Buses/coaches > 8 seats (rigid) | Bus and coach drivers |
| **DE** | D + trailer > 750 kg | Coach with trailer |

## Medical Examination Process

### Who Must Be Examined

All applicants for Group 2 licences must pass a medical examination before initial issue. Renewal requires re-examination at every renewal cycle. Examinations must be carried out by **a licensed medical practitioner** recognised by the competent national authority.

### Re-examination Intervals (Annex III, Section 5)

| Age group | Re-examination interval |
|---|---|
| Up to age 45 | Every **5 years** (at licence renewal) |
| Age 45 and above | Every **year** (annual medical certificate required) |

> Member states may apply shorter intervals. Several states (e.g. Germany, Netherlands) require annual examination from age 50 or 55 rather than 45.

### National Examination Bodies

| Country | Competent authority / examining body |
|---|---|
| **SE** (Sweden) | General practitioner (GP); Transportstyrelsen issues the licence. Driver self-declares using standardised form or medical certificate. |
| **DE** (Germany) | Examination by a traffic medicine specialist (Amtsarzt, or Facharzt für Arbeitsmedizin). KBA (Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt) is the national authority. |
| **GB** (United Kingdom) | DVLA (Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency) manages medical fitness; applicants complete D4 form + GP examination; specialist referral if needed. |
| **FR** (France) | Préfecture-approved medical commission (commission médicale préfectorale); ONISR data. |
| **NL** (Netherlands) | CBR (Centraal Bureau Rijvaardigheidsbewijzen) manages medical attestation. |
| **BE** (Belgium) | CARA (Centre d'Analyse du Risque Automobile) examines fitness. |

## Vision Requirements (Annex III, Section 6)

### Minimum Visual Acuity — Group 2

| Measurement | Required standard |
|---|---|
| Better eye (binocular if using both eyes) | ≥ **0.8** (Snellen/decimal) |
| Worse eye | ≥ **0.1** |
| With corrective lenses | Values above must be met **with correction** if needed |

> **Comparison with Group 1:** Group 1 requires only 0.5 binocular; Group 2 standard is considerably stricter.

### Visual Field

- Binocular horizontal visual field: minimum **160°** (Annex III Section 6.2)
- No significant defect in the central 120° of the visual field
- No significant defect in the binocular visual field that might affect safe driving

### Diplopia (Double Vision)

- **Diplopia disqualifies** Group 2 applicants unless the condition has been present since birth, is stable and well-compensated, and the driver has demonstrated safe driving ability with specialist confirmation
- Monocular vision: **disqualifying** for Group 2 licences unless very exceptional circumstances and specialist review

### Colour Vision

- Colour vision deficiency (colour blindness) is **not automatically disqualifying** for Group 2
- Assessed on a case-by-case basis; ability to distinguish traffic signals is the key criterion
- Severe colour blindness may prompt additional assessment

### Contact Lenses and Glasses

- Permitted provided the minimum acuity values are met with correction
- The corrective requirement must be noted on the licence

## Cardiovascular Requirements (Annex III, Section 3)

### General Principles

Cardiovascular conditions are assessed for risk of sudden incapacitation. Group 2 drivers face stricter controls given the higher road risk.

### Key Standards

| Condition | Group 2 ruling |
|---|---|
| **Severe cardiac arrhythmia** (e.g., uncontrolled AF, ventricular arrhythmia) | **Disqualifying** unless treated and stable with specialist clearance |
| **Angina at rest** | **Disqualifying** (risk of sudden incapacitation at the wheel) |
| **Angina on exertion** (controlled, no rest angina) | Case-by-case; may be permitted with specialist review and periodic check |
| **Controlled hypertension** | **Permitted** provided blood pressure is treated and monitored |
| **Pacemaker fitted** | Permitted for Group 2 with **periodic specialist review** (typically annual); stable rhythm required |
| **Post-myocardial infarction (heart attack)** | Minimum **6 weeks off driving** before return to Group 2; cardiac function assessment required; specialist clearance needed; ongoing annual review |
| **Heart failure** | Symptomatic heart failure: **disqualifying**; well-controlled LVEF ≥ 35% may be permitted on specialist recommendation |
| **Aortic stenosis / other valve disease** | Significant valve disease: disqualifying unless specialist confirms no risk of sudden incapacitation |

### Blood Pressure

- Severe hypertension (systolic > 180 / diastolic > 110 mmHg) that is untreated: **disqualifying** until controlled
- Controlled hypertension with no complications: **permitted** (with annual review from age 45)

## Diabetes (Annex III, Section 10 — as amended by Directive 2016/1106/EU)

### Background

Directive 2016/1106/EU (entered into force 31 October 2016) amended Annex III to allow insulin-treated diabetic drivers to hold Group 2 licences subject to conditions. Previously, insulin-treated diabetes was an absolute bar to Group 2.

### Current Rules for Insulin-Treated Diabetes (Group 2)

Insulin-treated drivers **may** hold Group 2 licences provided all of the following conditions are met:

1. **Annual specialist assessment** by an endocrinologist or diabetologist confirming adequate glycaemic control
2. **Regular glucose monitoring** — at least twice daily on driving days and at relevant times (before driving, every 2 hours while driving). Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) systems are accepted where national authority approves.
3. **Hypoglycaemia awareness** must be intact — a history of severe hypoglycaemia (requiring third-party assistance) in the previous 12 months disqualifies the driver until specialist confirms awareness is restored
4. **No severe hypoglycaemic episodes** while driving in the past 12 months
5. **Driver must not drive** if blood glucose is below **5.0 mmol/L** (90 mg/dL); must wait until glucose is above this threshold before beginning/resuming driving
6. The competent authority **may require** that the driving licence notes the insulin-treatment requirement
7. Licence validity for insulin-treated Group 2 drivers: **maximum 3 years** (vs. 5 years standard), unless national authority specifies shorter

### Oral Medication / Diet-Controlled Diabetes

Diabetes managed by diet alone or oral medication only (no insulin): **permitted** for Group 2 provided no severe hypoglycaemia risk and no other complications affecting fitness (e.g., severe peripheral neuropathy, advanced retinopathy affecting vision).

## Epilepsy and Seizures (Annex III, Section 7)

### Group 2 Standards — Significantly Stricter Than Group 1

| Condition | Group 1 | Group 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Seizure-free period (untreated) | **1 year** seizure-free | **5 years** seizure-free **off anti-epileptic medication** |
| Treated epilepsy (anti-epileptic drugs) | 1 year seizure-free on medication | **Generally disqualifying** for Group 2 unless specialist review confirms exceptional circumstances |
| Single unprovoked seizure | 6 months seizure-free (Group 1) | **5 years** seizure-free before Group 2 permitted |
| Provoked seizure (identifiable cause removed) | Shorter wait permitted | Extended wait; specialist assessment |
| Seizures only while asleep (pure nocturnal epilepsy) | Separate rules (1+ year pattern) | Longer assessment period required; specialist review |

> The 5-year seizure-free requirement for Group 2 (without medication) reflects the significantly higher road safety risk posed by sudden incapacitation when driving a heavy goods vehicle.

### Specialist Review Required

In all epilepsy cases, a **neurologist's report** must accompany the medical examination for Group 2 assessment. The examining doctor cannot approve Group 2 for epilepsy without neurology specialist input.

## Sleep Apnoea / OSA (Annex III, Section 12)

Sleep apnoea (Obstructive Sleep Apnoea, OSA) is a significant road safety risk due to excessive daytime sleepiness and microsleep episodes.

### Rules for Group 2

| Status | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Undiagnosed / untreated severe OSA** | **Disqualifying** until diagnosis and treatment commenced |
| **Diagnosed moderate/severe OSA — not yet treated** | **Disqualifying** pending treatment |
| **OSA treated with CPAP — compliant** | Permitted subject to **annual specialist review** and documented CPAP compliance (typically ≥ 4 hours/night on ≥ 70% of nights) |
| **OSA — mild, without significant daytime sleepiness** | Case-by-case; may be permitted without CPAP if specialist confirms no impairment |

### Practical Requirements

- CPAP data download/compliance report typically required at annual renewal
- If driver removes CPAP or stops using it: driving fitness is suspended until compliance re-established
- Sleep specialist (sleep medicine / respiratory medicine / ENT) must confirm compliance and fitness

## Psychiatric and Neurological Conditions (Annex III, Sections 8–9)

### General Approach

Psychiatric and neurological conditions are assessed **case-by-case** for Group 2. The key question is: does the condition create a risk of sudden incapacitation, severely impaired judgement, or dangerous behaviour while driving?

| Condition | Typical Group 2 ruling |
|---|---|
| **Schizophrenia / psychosis** | Disqualifying during active episode; may be permitted in stable remission with specialist review and medication compliance |
| **Severe depression** | Disqualifying during acute episode; return subject to psychiatrist clearance |
| **Bipolar disorder** | Case-by-case; unstable or rapid-cycling: disqualifying; stable on medication with specialist review: may be permitted |
| **Dementia / significant cognitive impairment** | **Disqualifying** |
| **Severe head injury / TBI** | Assessment depends on severity and recovery; neurologist required |
| **Parkinson's disease** | Typically disqualifying for Group 2 due to progressive nature; specialist review |
| **Multiple sclerosis** | Case-by-case depending on functional impact; specialist review |

### Substance and Alcohol Dependency

- **Alcohol dependency or misuse** at a level that impairs fitness: **disqualifying**
- **Drug dependency** (any substance): **disqualifying**
- Recovery from dependency: may be permitted after documented sustained abstinence (typically 1–2 years), with ongoing specialist review

## Summary — Key Group 2 Thresholds

| Standard | Group 1 | Group 2 (C, CE, D etc.) |
|---|---|---|
| Vision — better eye | ≥ 0.5 | ≥ **0.8** |
| Vision — worse eye | ≥ 0.2 | ≥ **0.1** |
| Binocular visual field | ≥ 120° | ≥ **160°** |
| Seizure-free period (untreated) | 1 year | **5 years** |
| Re-examination interval (from age 45) | Not mandated EU-wide | **Annual** |
| Insulin-treated diabetes | Permitted (conditions) | Permitted since 2016 (stricter conditions) |
| Uncontrolled OSA | Disqualifying | Disqualifying + **annual CPAP compliance check** |
| Post-MI return | Specialist clearance | Min. **6 weeks + cardiac function assessment** |

## Penalties

Medical fitness violations are not directly subject to the EU penalty framework — penalties apply under national road traffic and licensing law. The consequences of driving without meeting medical fitness requirements include:

| Consequence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Licence revocation / suspension** | National authority may revoke licence immediately upon receiving medical evidence of unfitness |
| **Criminal liability** | Driving while unfit (knowingly) may constitute reckless endangerment or equivalent criminal offence |
| **Insurance invalidation** | Driving while knowingly unfit may void commercial motor insurance |
| **Operator liability** | Haulage operators who knowingly deploy an unfit driver may face operator licence sanctions under Regulation 1071/2009 |

**Sweden (Transportstyrelsen):** Körkortslagen (SFS 1998:488) — driving licence revoked if medical requirements not met. Failure to report a condition that affects fitness is a criminal offence (Chapter 9 § 2 Körkortslagen).

**Germany (KBA):** §11 FeV (Fahrerlaubnisverordnung) — authorities may require medical assessment at any time. Failure to comply results in licence revocation.

**UK (DVLA):** Road Traffic Act 1988 Section 94 — driver must notify DVLA of any medical condition that affects fitness; failure to notify is a criminal offence (up to £1,000 fine).
