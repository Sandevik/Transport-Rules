## Overview

An **exceptional/abnormal load** (also called "special transport," "convoi exceptionnel," or "Schwertransport") is any vehicle or load that exceeds the standard legal limits set by Directive 96/53/EC (or national equivalents for non-EU states). There is **no harmonised EU permit system** — each member state operates its own permitting regime. This file establishes the common thresholds, principles, and vocabulary.

Directive 96/53/EC sets the limits that, when exceeded, place a vehicle into the exceptional load category:

| Parameter | Standard EU limit (Directive 96/53/EC) | Exceeding this = exceptional load |
|-----------|---------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Gross vehicle weight | 40 t (road train) / 44 t (combined transport) | > 40–44 t depending on configuration |
| Single axle load | 10 t (non-drive) / 11.5 t (drive) | Any axle above its limit |
| Width | 2.55 m (2.60 m for reefer) | > 2.55 m |
| Height | 4.00 m (Directive; many states use 4.2–4.3 m nationally) | > national limit |
| Length (road train) | 18.75 m | > 18.75 m |

### Indivisible loads

A key concept in most EU permitting regimes is that exceptional loads must generally be **indivisible** — the cargo cannot reasonably be split into standard-sized units. Divisible cargo (e.g. bulk aggregate) is typically not eligible for exceptional load permits; it must be transported in standard vehicles.

---

## EU Harmonization Status: None

There is **no EU directive or regulation governing the issuance of exceptional load permits**. Directive 96/53/EC defines the maximum standard limits but explicitly leaves permit authority to member states (Article 4 — special authorisations). The EU has studied harmonization (COM(2022) 527 study) but has not legislated. This means:

- A carrier moving an oversized transformer from Germany to Poland needs **separate permits** from Germany and Poland
- Route approval is country-specific, not cross-border
- Fees, escort requirements, and processing times vary significantly
- There is no mutual recognition of permits between EU member states

The UNECE Consolidated Resolution R.E.3 (most recently revised) provides non-binding guidance and encourages bilateral agreements, but these are limited.

## Permit Types (Common Across EU)

### 1. Route-specific single-trip permit
- Issued for one or more specific journeys on a defined route
- Most common for very large, heavy, or unique loads
- Requires route survey (bridges, overhead cables, roundabouts)
- Processing: typically 5–20 working days depending on country

### 2. Annual/blanket permits
- Valid for repeated movements of similar loads within defined parameters
- Usually limited to loads not exceeding certain thresholds (e.g. max 5 m wide, max 100 t)
- Route may be fixed or operator may select from approved categories
- Common in DE (§46 StVO Ausnahmegenehmigung), NL, UK

### 3. Type permits (by load category)
- Some countries issue permits by load type (e.g. "wind turbine components") rather than individual trips
- Operator must still notify authorities of each movement

## Common Weight and Dimension Thresholds Triggering Permits

These are the most common cross-EU trigger points. Individual countries vary — see country files.

| Dimension/weight | Typical permit threshold | Notes |
|----------------|------------------------|-------|
| Width | > 2.55 m (or national standard width) | Nearly universal |
| Width requiring convoy/escort | > 3.0–3.5 m | Varies by country |
| Width requiring police escort | > 4.5–5.0 m | Varies; FR: >4.5 m; DE: >5 m |
| Height | > 4.0–4.3 m (country-specific) | Overhead infrastructure concern |
| Length (combination) | > 18.75–20 m | Turning radius concern |
| Gross weight | > 40–44 t | Varies by axle configuration |
| Axle load | > 10–11.5 t | Varies by axle type |
| Point load / single axle | > 12–16 t (for bridges) | Country-specific bridge tables |

## Route Survey Requirements

For exceptional loads, virtually all EU states require a **route survey** before a permit is issued:

- Check clearance heights (bridges, overhead wires, gantries)
- Verify weight-bearing capacity of bridges on the planned route
- Identify sharp bends, roundabouts, narrow sections requiring traffic management
- Overhead power line clearances (typically ≥ 5 m plus safety margin)
- Results feed into permit conditions (time windows, speed, escort)

Route surveys may be done by:
- The carrier/transport company
- The issuing authority
- A specialist route survey company

## Speed Limits for Exceptional Loads

| Load category | Typical speed range | Common EU limits |
|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Standard abnormal load | 40–80 km/h | DE: typically 40–60 km/h; FR: 50–80 km/h |
| Very wide loads (> 4.0 m) | 40–50 km/h | Most states |
| Extremely heavy loads | 25–40 km/h | Near max axle limits |
| Convoy with police escort | 40–60 km/h | Police-set speed |

Most permits specify a **maximum speed**, not a required speed. Drivers must also observe posted road speed limits if lower.

---

## Night and Weekend Restrictions

Abnormal loads are typically restricted to **daylight hours and weekdays** to minimise disruption:

| Country | Night ban | Weekend ban | Holiday ban |
|---------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Germany | Dusk to dawn for wide loads | Saturday afternoon and Sunday | Public holidays |
| France | Variable by department | Saturday afternoon to Monday morning (some) | Public holidays |
| Austria | Night (variable by permit) | Case-by-case | Public holidays |
| Netherlands | Night for very wide loads | Sundays (case-by-case) | — |
| Poland | Night for wide loads | Saturdays and Sundays | Public holidays |
| Sweden | Night for very wide loads | Case-by-case | — |

**Standard pattern across EU:** Wide loads (> 3.0–3.5 m) are generally prohibited from Friday 22:00 to Monday 06:00 and on public holidays, though exact times vary by permit and country.

## Escort (Pilot) Vehicle Requirements

### Pilot/escort vehicles

| Condition | Typical requirement |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Width > 3.0 m | 1 escort vehicle (front or rear) |
| Width > 4.0 m | 2 escort vehicles (front and rear) |
| Length > 30–40 m | 1–2 escort vehicles |
| Combination > 50 m | Multiple escorts, possibly police |

Escort vehicles must typically be equipped with:
- Amber/yellow rotating or flashing beacon (bar light)
- "OVERSIZE LOAD" / "TRANSPORT EXCEPTIONNEL" sign boards (country-specific)
- Radio communication with the load vehicle
- First aid equipment (country-specific)

Pilot vehicle drivers typically require specific certification/training (varies by country — see country files).

### Police escort thresholds

Police escort is required (automatically or by permit condition) when loads exceed:

| Dimension | Threshold for mandatory police escort |
|-----------|-------------------------------------|
| Width | > 4.5–5.0 m (country-specific) |
| Length | > 50–60 m combined convoy length |
| Weight | > 100–150 t (highly variable) |
| Multiple intersections/complex route | At authority's discretion |

Police escort must be requested separately and well in advance. Lead times of 2–4 weeks are common for police arrangements.

---

## Advance Notification

Most EU states require **advance notification** to road authorities, tunnel operators, and bridge managers for loads above certain thresholds:

- Notification periods: typically 2 business days to 4 weeks depending on load
- Recipient: issuing authority, motorway operator, local road authority (varies)
- Bridge owners/managers must be notified for loads with point loads exceeding standard bridge tables

---

## Permit Application Principles

1. **Identify route** including start, end, and all roads
2. **Calculate dimensions and weights** (including tare + payload, all axle loads)
3. **Apply in each country separately** — no single EU-wide application
4. **Allow processing time** — 5 to 20 working days minimum; 4+ weeks for complex loads
5. **Check permit conditions** — speed limits, time windows, escort requirements
6. **Notify police** in advance if escort required
7. **Carry permit documents** in vehicle at all times during transport

---

## Cross-Border Operations

Moving an exceptional load across EU borders requires a **separate permit for each country traversed**. Applications must be coordinated so time windows and escort arrangements align at border crossings. There is no automatic carry-over of conditions.

Practical sequence:
1. Apply for all country permits simultaneously
2. Identify the most restrictive common time window
3. Arrange escorts in each country (different companies)
4. Ensure route survey covers the full corridor

---

## Penalties

Penalties for operating an exceptional load without a valid permit or in breach of permit conditions are **purely national** (see country files for specifics). General patterns:

- Operating without permit: significant fines (typically €500–€5,000+), immobilisation of vehicle
- Exceeding permit conditions (e.g., wrong route, wrong time): fine + possible permit revocation
- Bridge damage from overloaded vehicle: civil liability + criminal prosecution possible
- Repeat violations: suspension of transport licence (under EU Regulation 1071/2009 fitness/repute provisions)
