# AETR — Undertaking (Transport Company) Obligations

**Framework:** AETR Agreement, Article 10. Sets out the responsibilities of transport operators toward drivers and compliance.

**Sources:**
- https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/aetr-agreement-and-status — UNECE: AETR Agreement text
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22010A0317%2801%29 — EUR-Lex: AETR consolidated text

**Last verified: 2026-05-31**

## Overview

AETR Article 10 places affirmative duties on transport undertakings (operators, carriers). Liability for AETR violations is shared between the driver and the undertaking — an undertaking cannot avoid responsibility simply because the driver committed the violation, if the undertaking's scheduling or commercial pressure caused or enabled it.

---

## Core Obligations (Article 10)

### 1. No Incentives to Violate AETR
An undertaking **must not** remunerate drivers or grant bonuses linked to:
- Distances traveled
- Speed of delivery
- Volume of goods transported

…where such payment structure **incentivizes endangering road safety or violating AETR rules**.

Performance-based pay is not prohibited outright, but any scheme that effectively pressures drivers to skip required breaks or exceed driving limits is an AETR violation by the undertaking.

### 2. Schedule Compliance
The undertaking must **organize driver work schedules** in a way that allows drivers to comply with AETR. This includes:
- Realistic planning of journey durations
- Allocation of sufficient calendar time for required rest periods
- Not assigning more work than a driver can legally complete

### 3. Regular Compliance Monitoring
The undertaking must **periodically check** whether drivers are complying with AETR, including:
- Reviewing tachograph data downloads (vehicle unit: every 90 days; driver card: every 28 days)
- Identifying patterns of infringement
- Taking corrective action if violations are found

### 4. No Instructions to Violate
The undertaking **must not** instruct, direct, or implicitly require drivers to violate AETR rules. Instructions to "make the deadline no matter what" that result in rest-time violations can constitute an undertaking violation.

### 5. Record Retention
Undertakings must retain tachograph and related compliance records for at least **12 months** and must make them available for inspection by enforcement authorities on request.

## Liability Framework

| Scenario | Liable party |
|----------|-------------|
| Driver voluntarily violates without company knowledge or pressure | Driver primarily liable |
| Undertaking sets impossible schedules that necessitate violations | Undertaking liable; driver may have reduced liability |
| Undertaking instructs or pressures driver to violate | Undertaking liable |
| Both pressure and driver choice involved | Joint liability; enforcement determines split |

---

## Comparison with EU 561/2006 Article 10

AETR Article 10 and EU 561/2006 Article 10 are substantively identical. The practical difference is enforcement:

| Area | EU 561/2006 | AETR |
|------|------------|------|
| Enforcement network | ERRU (Enterprise Risk Rating Unit) cross-border data sharing | National authorities only; no ERRU |
| Sanctions on undertaking | National law (varies by country); some have unlimited liability | National law of each contracting party |
| Home-country inspection | EU: inspection can be triggered by foreign country data | AETR: limited cross-border enforcement |
