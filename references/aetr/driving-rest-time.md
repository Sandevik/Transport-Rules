# AETR — Driving and Rest Time Rules

**Framework:** AETR (European Agreement Concerning the Work of Crews of Vehicles Engaged in International Road Transport), UNECE. This file is the baseline for **Tier 2 countries** (non-EU/EEA AETR signatories: CH, TR, RU, UA, MA, AZ, KZ, etc.).

**Note for EU member states:** Within EU/EEA territory, EU Regulation 561/2006 governs (see `references/eu/driving-rest-time.md`). AETR applies when a vehicle crosses into a non-EU AETR country and for the entire journey when AETR countries are included in the route.

**Sources:**
- https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/aetr-agreement-and-status — UNECE: AETR Agreement text and contracting party status
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22010A0317%2801%29 — EUR-Lex: AETR consolidated text (as published in EU OJ)

## Overview

The AETR Agreement (originally 1970, substantially revised and aligned with EU 561/2006 in 2010) governs driving and rest times for international road transport involving at least one AETR contracting party outside the EU/EEA. Since 2010, AETR rules are substantively identical to EU 561/2006. The legal basis differs: AETR is an international treaty, not an EU regulation.

**56 AETR contracting parties** include all 27 EU member states plus: Albania, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Liechtenstein, Moldova, Monaco, Montenegro, Morocco, North Macedonia, Norway, Russia, San Marino, Serbia, Switzerland, Tajikistan, Tunisia, Turkey, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, and others.

## Core Rules (Aligned with EU 561/2006 since 2010)

The AETR rules below are substantively identical to EU 561/2006. See `references/eu/driving-rest-time.md` for the full annotated text.

| Rule | Limit | AETR Article |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Normal daily driving | **9 hours** | Art. 6(1) |
| Extended daily driving (max 2×/week) | **10 hours** | Art. 6(1) |
| Weekly driving maximum | **56 hours** | Art. 6(2) |
| Biweekly driving maximum | **90 hours** | Art. 6(3) |
| Break after 4h30min driving | **45 min** (or 15+30 split) | Art. 7 |
| Regular daily rest | **11 consecutive hours** | Art. 8(1) |
| Reduced daily rest (max 3×/week) | **9 consecutive hours** | Art. 8(1) |
| Normal weekly rest | **45 consecutive hours** | Art. 8(6) |
| Reduced weekly rest | **24 consecutive hours** | Art. 8(6) |
| Weekly rest compensation window | **Within 3 weeks** | Art. 8(6) |

## Key Differences from EU 561/2006

| Topic | EU 561/2006 | AETR |
|-------|------------|------|
| Legal basis | EU Regulation (directly applicable) | International treaty (national ratification required) |
| Enforcement | EU enforcement network (ERRU risk rating) | National authorities only; no ERRU access |
| Advance payment at roadside | Permitted (varies by country) | **NOT permitted** — AETR Article 22ter prohibits cash advance payments at roadside |
| Smart tachograph deadlines | EU retrofit deadlines apply | AETR Annex 1B sets own tachograph requirements (see aetr/tachograph.md) |
| Cab prohibition for weekly rest | Applies (Mobility Package 2022) | Not explicitly adopted in AETR text; national law may vary |
| Return home every 4 weeks | Mobility Package requirement | Not in AETR text |

---

## When AETR Applies vs EU 561/2006

- **Journey entirely within EU/EEA**: EU 561/2006 applies
- **Journey through at least one non-EU AETR country**: AETR applies for the **entire journey** (including EU segments)
- Example: Sweden → Germany → Turkey = AETR applies from start to finish
- Example: Sweden → Denmark → Germany = EU 561/2006 applies (all EU countries)

## If You Accidentally Exceed Limits (AETR journeys)

The practical steps are the same as under EU 561/2006 — stop and rest, record the reason, do not continue driving. See `references/eu/driving-rest-time.md` for the full step-by-step.

**Key AETR-specific differences:**

| Issue | AETR rule |
|-------|-----------|
| Advance payment at roadside | **Prohibited** — AETR Art. 22ter. Officers cannot demand cash on the spot. Any fine must be processed through administrative channels in the driver's country of establishment. |
| Enforcement authority | National transport authority in the country where the check occurs. No ERRU cross-border sharing (AETR countries are outside ERRU). |
| Jurisdiction for follow-up | Infringements may be reported to the home country authority; cross-border debt collection is not as automatic as within the EU. |
| Exceptional circumstances | AETR Art. 12 mirrors EU Art. 12 — same narrow emergency exception applies. Note reason on tachograph immediately. |

**Practical tip for AETR countries:** Ensure the driver carries a copy of the AETR agreement Art. 22ter when operating in non-EU countries. If an enforcement officer demands advance payment, the driver may politely decline by citing this article — but should remain cooperative and request written documentation of any alleged infringement instead.
