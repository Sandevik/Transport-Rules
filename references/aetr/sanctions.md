# AETR — Sanctions and Penalties

**Framework:** AETR Agreement, Articles 22–22ter. Covers the penalties framework and the prohibition on roadside advance payment of fines.

**Sources:**
- https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/aetr-agreement-and-status — UNECE: AETR Agreement text
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22010A0317%2801%29 — EUR-Lex: AETR consolidated text

**Last verified: 2026-05-31**

---

## Overview

AETR leaves the determination of specific penalty amounts to each contracting party's national law. What AETR sets directly are:
1. The **obligation to have effective, proportionate, and dissuasive penalties**
2. The **prohibition on collecting advance payments (deposits/cash) at the roadside** (Article 22ter)
3. Limits on how long a vehicle may be detained

---

## National Penalty Determination

Each contracting party must establish national penalties for AETR violations. AETR requires only that penalties be:
- **Effective** — actually applied
- **Proportionate** — scaled to the severity of the infringement
- **Dissuasive** — sufficient to deter violations

Specific fines vary widely across contracting parties. EU member states harmonize infringement categories under Directive 2006/22/EC (roadside enforcement) but each sets its own fine amounts.

---

## Article 22ter — Prohibition on Advance Payment at Roadside

This is AETR's most operationally significant difference from EU practice:

> **AETR Article 22ter: Contracting parties shall NOT require payment of a fine, deposit, or financial guarantee at the roadside as a condition for allowing the vehicle to continue its journey.**

### What This Means in Practice

| Situation | EU 561/2006 territory | AETR-governed journey |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Roadside fine for driving time violation | Some EU countries can require immediate payment or deposit (e.g., FR, DE, PL) | **NOT permitted** under AETR — vehicle cannot be detained for non-payment |
| Vehicle immobilization for non-payment | Possible in some EU countries | Not permitted by AETR; only permitted for safety-critical reasons (tachograph tampering, etc.) |
| Issuing a formal penalty notice | Permitted and common | Permitted — but collection is through administrative/judicial process, not roadside |

> **Practical note for operators:** On AETR-governed routes (e.g., journeys through Switzerland, Turkey, Ukraine, etc.), inspectors from those countries may issue penalty notices, but cannot require immediate cash payment or deposits at the roadside. If a foreign enforcement officer demands immediate payment, the driver can invoke Article 22ter.

## Infringement Categories

While specific fines are national, infringements are generally graded by severity. Common category structure used by EU members (also relevant for AETR operations in/through EU territory):

| Severity | Example infringements |
|----------|-----------------------|
| **Most serious** | Tachograph manipulation or tampering; driving with fraudulent records; driving time exceeded by >50% |
| **Very serious** | Missing driver card; daily driving exceeded by >2 hours; rest reduced by >50% |
| **Serious** | Daily driving exceeded by 1–2 hours; weekly rest reduced by 1–2 hours; no printout when required |
| **Minor** | Administrative records incomplete; failure to switch tachograph mode correctly |

---

## Vehicle Detention

A vehicle may be detained by enforcement authorities **only for the time strictly necessary to complete the inspection** or, in cases of serious risk to road safety (e.g., driver without any rest), until the safety issue is resolved.

Detention is **not permitted** as leverage for payment under AETR.

---

## Mutual Recognition of Sanctions

AETR does not establish a multilateral system for enforcing penalties across contracting parties' borders (unlike EU cross-border enforcement frameworks). Penalties issued in one AETR country are enforceable only in that country's jurisdiction unless bilateral agreements provide otherwise.
