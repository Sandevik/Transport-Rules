# AETR — Application Scope

**Framework:** AETR Agreement, Articles 1–4. Defines which vehicles and journeys trigger AETR obligations.

**Sources:**
- https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/aetr-agreement-and-status — UNECE: AETR Agreement text and contracting party status
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22010A0317%2801%29 — EUR-Lex: AETR consolidated text

**Last verified: 2026-05-31**

---

## When AETR Applies

AETR applies to **international road transport** (carrying passengers or goods) where:
1. The journey crosses the territory of **at least one contracting party** that is not an EU/EEA member state, **AND**
2. The vehicle falls within the scope categories below.

If **any leg** of the journey passes through a non-EU AETR country, AETR applies for the **entire journey** — including segments through EU territory.

| Journey | Applicable rules |
|---------|-----------------|
| Sweden → Germany → Turkey | AETR (whole route) |
| Sweden → Denmark → Germany | EU 561/2006 (all EU) |
| Germany → Switzerland → Italy | AETR (whole route; CH is non-EU AETR) |
| France → Switzerland → France | AETR (whole route; CH triggers it) |

---

## Vehicles in Scope

### Goods Vehicles
- Vehicles (including combinations) used for the **carriage of goods** with a **permissible maximum weight exceeding 3.5 tonnes**
- Weight includes any trailer or semi-trailer

### Passenger Vehicles
- Vehicles **constructed for the carriage of more than 9 persons** including the driver (coaches/buses)
- Applies to vehicles intended for regular or occasional international passenger services

---

## Vehicles NOT in Scope

Article 13 lists full exemptions — see `references/aetr/exemptions.md`. Key scope-level exclusions:

- Goods vehicles ≤ 3.5 t permissible maximum weight (including trailer)
- Vehicles with maximum authorized speed ≤ 40 km/h
- Purely domestic operations (no crossing of an AETR-only country)

## Contracting Parties (56 countries)

**EU/EEA member states (27 EU + Norway, Liechtenstein, Iceland):** Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, plus Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein.

**Non-EU AETR signatories (the countries that trigger AETR):**
Albania, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, Monaco, Montenegro, Morocco, North Macedonia, Russia, San Marino, Serbia, Switzerland, Tajikistan, Tunisia, Turkey, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, Uzbekistan.

> **Note:** Switzerland (CH) is the most frequently encountered non-EU AETR country in European freight operations. Any route passing through Switzerland activates AETR for the full journey.

---

## Relationship to EU 561/2006

For EU member states, EU 561/2006 (and Regulation 165/2014 for tachographs) applies to domestic and intra-EU operations. When AETR applies:

- AETR rules override EU 561/2006 for the journey
- Since 2010 the substantive rules are almost identical (AETR was realigned with EU 561/2006)
- The key practical differences are in **enforcement mechanisms** and **tachograph retrofit deadlines** — not in the driving/rest limits themselves

See `references/aetr/driving-rest-time.md` for the rule comparison table.
