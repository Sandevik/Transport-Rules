# AETR — Enforcement and Roadside Inspections

**Framework:** AETR Agreement, Articles 15–22. Governs inspection powers, procedures, and what authorities check during roadside controls.

**Sources:**
- https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/aetr-agreement-and-status — UNECE: AETR Agreement text
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22010A0317%2801%29 — EUR-Lex: AETR consolidated text

**Last verified: 2026-05-31**

---

## Overview

AETR Articles 15–22 establish the enforcement framework. Each contracting party designates national competent authorities to conduct inspections. Unlike EU enforcement (which uses ERRU cross-border risk rating), AETR enforcement is purely national — there is no multilateral data-sharing system between AETR countries.

---

## Competent Authorities

Each contracting party designates enforcement authorities empowered to conduct:
- **Roadside inspections** of vehicles and drivers in transit
- **Premises inspections** (at the undertaking's base)
- Cross-territory cooperation through bilateral arrangements

---

## What Must Be Available for Roadside Inspection

Drivers must be able to produce the following on demand:

| Document | Coverage period required |
|----------|------------------------|
| Driver card (with vehicle unit printout if card missing or malfunctioning) | Current day + preceding **28 days** |
| Tachograph charts (analogue) — if applicable | Current day + preceding **28 days** |
| Manual entries (where tachograph was not in use) | Current day + preceding **28 days** |
| Driver record book entries — if record book used as fallback (see `references/aetr/driver-documentation.md`) | Covering all days without tachograph data |
| Driving licence | Valid for the vehicle category |

> **28-day rule:** Inspectors check the current day plus the previous 28 calendar days. Drivers must carry this data — either on the driver card or as printouts/charts. Data older than 28 days need not be carried in the cab.

---

## Inspection Scope

During a roadside check, enforcement authorities may inspect:

1. **Driving periods** — verify daily and weekly driving limits
2. **Breaks** — verify 45-minute break requirements after 4h30min driving
3. **Daily rest** — verify required rest between shifts
4. **Weekly rest** — verify weekly and reduced weekly rest compliance
5. **Tachograph operation** — check the device is functioning, not tampered with, and correctly recording
6. **Driver card validity** — confirm card is not expired, revoked, or belonging to another driver
7. **Manual entries** — check completeness and accuracy of manual records
8. **Undertaking documentation** — at premises inspections, schedules, payroll, and records

---

## Tachograph Inspection

Inspectors are empowered to:
- Read data directly from the tachograph vehicle unit
- Request driver card readout
- Review printouts
- Check for signs of tampering, manipulation, or interference with recording
- Verify calibration seals and calibration certificates

Using a **manipulation device** or interfering with tachograph recording is a serious offense in all contracting parties and subject to the highest penalty categories.

---

## Inspection Duration

AETR does not permit authorities to detain a vehicle longer than **necessary for the inspection**. Prolonged detention to pressure payment of fines on the spot is prohibited (see `references/aetr/sanctions.md` — Article 22ter prohibition on advance payment).

---

## Cross-Border Enforcement

Unlike the EU's ERRU system, AETR does not establish a central database for sharing infringement data between contracting parties. However:

- Each contracting party may share information bilaterally
- Some AETR countries have bilateral arrangements for mutual enforcement recognition
- Undertakings registered in EU member states are subject to ERRU for EU-leg violations, even on AETR-covered journeys

---

## Premises Inspections (Undertaking Level)

Beyond roadside checks, competent authorities may inspect transport undertakings at their premises to verify:
- Tachograph data downloads (vehicle unit every 90 days; driver card every 28 days)
- Record retention (12 months minimum)
- Work scheduling records
- Driver employment and remuneration arrangements
