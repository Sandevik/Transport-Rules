## Overview

Ukraine is not an EU member state. Since June 2022, the EU and Ukraine have maintained a **wartime transport liberalisation arrangement** that suspends the normal bilateral permit requirement for EU and Ukrainian hauliers operating between the EU and Ukraine. This arrangement has been extended repeatedly and is the dominant operational framework for EU–Ukraine road freight.

For third-country (non-EU) operators and for pre-liberalisation reference, the standard permit system (bilateral permits, CEMT/ITF multilateral permits) applies. Ukraine is a TIR contracting party.

## CRITICAL: EU-Ukraine Wartime Transport Liberalisation

> **This section governs the vast majority of current EU-Ukraine road freight operations.**

### Background

Prior to the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine (February 2022), EU–Ukraine road transport operated under a standard bilateral permit system. The invasion severely disrupted supply chains and required emergency measures.

**From June 2022:** The EU and Ukraine concluded a bilateral arrangement suspending the requirement for **bilateral road transport permits** for:
- EU hauliers (holding an EU Community Licence) operating to/from Ukraine
- Ukrainian hauliers (holding a Ukrainian road transport authorisation) operating to/from the EU
- Both bilateral (direct EU–UA) and transit operations through third countries on the EU–UA corridor

### Current Status (as of 2026)

The liberalisation arrangement has been **extended multiple times** since its original June 2022 adoption. Extensions have been agreed by the EU-Ukraine Joint Committee under the EU-Ukraine Association Agreement framework.

- **Original validity:** June 2022 – June 2023
- **Extended:** Multiple extensions through 2023, 2024, and into 2025–2026
- **2025 extension:** Extended to cover operations through end of 2025 / mid-2026; verify current expiry date with DG MOVE or national competent authority before operations
- **Status as of 2026-06-01:** The arrangement is understood to remain in force; check https://transport.ec.europa.eu for the latest extension decision

### What the Liberalisation Covers

| Operation Type | Permit required? |
|----------------|-----------------|
| EU haulier bilateral transport (EU ↔ Ukraine) | **No** — permit-free under liberalisation |
| Ukrainian haulier bilateral transport (Ukraine ↔ EU) | **No** — permit-free under liberalisation |
| EU haulier transit through Ukraine | **No** — permit-free |
| Ukrainian haulier transit through EU | **No** — permit-free |
| Third-country (non-EU) haulier operating to/from Ukraine | **Yes** — bilateral or CEMT permit required |
| Cabotage within Ukraine by EU haulier | **No** — cabotage remains prohibited |

### Required Documents Under Liberalisation

Even without a bilateral permit, EU hauliers must carry:
- Valid **EU Community Licence** (certified true copy on vehicle)
- **CMR consignment note**
- **Driver CPC qualification card** (EU-issued)
- Vehicle registration, tachograph records (AETR applies)
- Insurance documents
- Any cargo-specific documents (phytosanitary, customs, etc.)

## Standard Permit System (Pre-Liberalisation / Third-Country Operators)

For third-country operators (non-EU, non-Ukrainian) and as background for when the liberalisation eventually expires:

### Permit Types

| Permit Type | Description | Issuing Authority |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Bilateral transport permit | For direct transport between Ukraine and a specific country | Ukrtransinspekciya / Ministry of Infrastructure |
| CEMT/ITF multilateral permit | For operations between CEMT member countries transiting Ukraine | National CEMT allocation body |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Ukraine without loading/unloading | Ukrainian border/customs authorities |

### CEMT/ITF Permits

Ukraine is a member of the International Transport Forum (ITF, formerly CEMT). **ITF/CEMT multilateral permits are valid for transit through Ukraine** for operators from other ITF member countries. This is the primary route for non-EU operators (e.g. from Turkey, Kazakhstan) transiting Ukraine.

Annual ITF permits and short-term (30-day) permits are both available.

### Permit Application

- Foreign operators apply through their national competent authority or transport association
- Ukrainian operators apply through Ukrtransinspekciya (State Inspectorate for Safety on Land Transport) for outbound permits
- Annual quota allocations are negotiated bilaterally

## TIR Convention

Ukraine is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR Carnets are widely used for customs transit operations through Ukraine:
- Issued in Ukraine by the Association of International Road Carriers of Ukraine (AsMAP Ukraine), the national IRU guaranteeing association
- Used extensively for road freight between EU and Turkey, Middle East, and Central Asia transiting Ukraine
- Requires TIR-approved vehicle and valid carnet

## Special Border Procedures (Wartime Context)

Ukrainian border crossing procedures have been significantly affected by the ongoing war:

- **Increased security checks** at all border crossings
- **Prioritisation of humanitarian aid and essential goods** — dedicated lanes at some crossings
- **Queuing and delays** remain significant at key crossings (Medyka/Shehyni with Poland, Krakovets/Korczowa)
- **Western land borders** (with Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Moldova) are the main operational routes; eastern borders non-operational
- **E-Queue systems** (electronic pre-registration for border crossing time slots) operate at major Polish–Ukrainian crossings
- Drivers should monitor crossing conditions in real time via Ukrainian Border Guards website

**Key operational crossings for freight:**
- Krakovets–Korczowa (Ukraine–Poland, M-11)
- Shehyni–Medyka (Ukraine–Poland)
- Uzhhorod–Vyšné Nemecké (Ukraine–Slovakia)
- Chop–Záhony (Ukraine–Hungary)
- Porubne–Siret (Ukraine–Romania)
- Palanca/Kuchurhan (Ukraine–Moldova)

## Penalties

Penalties are imposed under Ukrainian legislation on road transport. Amounts are expressed in UAH (Ukrainian hryvnia). Note that wartime conditions may affect enforcement priorities.

| Violation | Penalty (UAH) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required permit (non-EU operators when liberalisation does not apply) | 34,000–68,000 UAH | Vehicle may be detained |
| Permit conditions exceeded (weight, route) | 17,000–34,000 UAH | Per violation |
| Operating without TIR Carnet when required | 34,000–68,000 UAH + goods detention | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 17,000–51,000 UAH | |
| Driving without required CPC documentation | 8,500–17,000 UAH | |

*Note: UAH amounts are indicative; Ukrainian transport fines are periodically revised. Verify current amounts with Ukrtransinspekciya.*
