## Overview

The Netherlands is one of Europe's top combined transport nations, anchored by the Port of Rotterdam — the largest seaport in Europe. The Rotterdam–Rhine corridor is a key inland waterway and rail route. The Netherlands has no general HGV weekend or night driving bans, so the primary benefits of combined transport status here are the 44t weight allowance and documentation simplification for container freight.

---

## Weight Allowance

5+ axle vehicles operating as part of qualifying combined transport journeys may carry **44 tonnes** (vs. standard 40t, or 50t for some LZV configurations — see dimensions-weights.md for national LZV rules).

## Key Intermodal Infrastructure

| Terminal/Port | Location | Type |
|---------------|----------|------|
| Port of Rotterdam (Maasvlakte, Waalhaven, ECT, EUROMAX) | Rotterdam | World's largest container port in Europe |
| Amsterdam (Westpoort, Ceres) | Amsterdam | Container + bulk |
| Tilburg (Waalhaven) | Tilburg | Rail intermodal hub |
| Venlo (Greenport Venlo) | Venlo | Cross-docking + intermodal |
| Nijmegen | Nijmegen | Inland waterway terminal |

**Inland waterways**: Rhine, Maas, Waal — the Netherlands has the most developed inland waterway freight network in Europe. Barge transport qualifying as the non-road leg fully satisfies 92/106/EEC requirements.

---

## LZV (EcoCombi) and Combined Transport

Dutch LZV (Lang Zware Voertuigen / Long Heavy Vehicles) up to 25.25m are a national programme (see dimensions-weights.md) distinct from combined transport. Combined transport focuses on container/swap-body rail/waterway links; LZV is a road-only exception. These are separate legal frameworks.

---

## Documentation Required

- Waterway or rail booking/completion confirmation from terminal or barge operator
- CMR waybill for road leg
- Vehicle registration confirming ≥5 axles (for 44t)

---

## Deviations from EU Baseline

No significant deviations from Directive 92/106/EEC.
