## Overview

Turkey is one of the most significant transit countries in the world for road freight, bridging Europe with the Middle East, Central Asia, and beyond. International road freight to, from, and through Turkey requires either a **bilateral road transport permit (izin belgesi)** or operates under the **TIR Convention**. Turkey has a well-developed bilateral permit system with most European countries and is a major participant in the TIR customs transit system.

Turkey is not an EU member state and is not party to the EU's Community Licence system for road haulage. EU hauliers operating in Turkey require either a valid bilateral permit under the EU–Turkey bilateral transport agreement framework, or a TIR Carnet for transit movements.

Key corridors involving Turkey:
- **Trans-European to Middle East:** Western Europe → Bulgaria/Greece → Turkey → Iran/Iraq/Gulf states
- **Central Asia corridor:** Turkey → Georgia → Azerbaijan → Kazakhstan/Central Asia (Middle Corridor)
- **Balkans transit:** Turkey → Greece/Bulgaria → EU

## TIR Convention

Turkey is a contracting party to the **TIR Convention (Customs Convention on the International Transport of Goods under Cover of TIR Carnets, Geneva 1975)**. Turkey is one of the most active TIR countries globally, with a very high volume of TIR movements.

**How TIR works in Turkey:**
- TIR Carnets allow sealed goods to transit Turkey without opening/inspection at each customs post
- Turkish customs accepts TIR Carnets issued by IRU-affiliated national associations
- In Turkey, TIR Carnets are issued by **TOBB** (the Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Turkey) acting as the national guaranteeing association
- Each TIR Carnet covers one journey; a maximum guarantee of EUR 100,000 applies per carnet

**TIR requirements at Turkish border crossings:**
- TIR-approved vehicle with sealed loading compartment and TIR plates
- Valid TIR Carnet (not expired, all vouchers intact)
- Turkish customs will note vehicle/seal details on the carnet volet
- NCTS (New Computerised Transit System) may apply for EU-originating TIR movements at the Turkish border entry point

**Key Turkish TIR border crossings:**
- Kapıkule (Bulgaria border) — busiest EU entry point
- İpsala (Greece border)
- Habur (Iraq border) — major Middle East corridor
- Gürbulak (Iran border)
- Sarp (Georgia border)

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits (İzin Belgesi)

For movements that are not conducted under TIR, EU and non-EU hauliers operating to, from, or through Turkey require a **bilateral road transport permit (izin belgesi)**. Turkey has concluded bilateral road transport agreements with most European and many other countries.

### Permit Types

| Permit Type | Turkish Term | Description |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral permit | İkili taşıma izni | For direct transport between Turkey and the permit-issuing country |
| Transit permit | Transit geçiş belgesi | For vehicles transiting Turkey without loading/unloading in Turkey |
| Third-country permit | Üçüncü ülke izni | For transport involving a third country (e.g. Poland–Turkey–Iran) |
| Empty vehicle permit | Boş araç izni | For unladen movements under certain bilateral frameworks |

### Permit Allocation and Quotas

Bilateral permits operate under **annual quota systems** negotiated between Turkey and each partner country. Quotas are exchanged at Joint Committee meetings typically held annually.

- **EU Member States:** Each EU member state has its own bilateral agreement with Turkey. Quota sizes vary by country (Germany, Bulgaria, Romania, Poland, Hungary tend to have larger quotas due to high traffic volumes). EU enlargement has not created a single EU-level permit vis-à-vis Turkey; each member state maintains its own bilateral arrangement.
- **ECMT/ITF multilateral permits:** Turkey is a member of the International Transport Forum (ITF, formerly CEMT). ITF multilateral permits are **valid in Turkey** and provide an alternative to bilateral permits for ECMT member country operators. Annual and short-term (30-day) ITF permits are both accepted.
- Turkey does not recognise EU Community Licences as a substitute for bilateral permits (unlike intra-EU operations).

### Permit Application — For Foreign Operators

Foreign operators obtain permits through their own **national transport association or competent authority**, which receives an allocation of Turkish bilateral permits each year:

- In the UK: apply via DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency); single-journey permits available at £8
- In Sweden: apply via Transportstyrelsen or the Swedish Transport Association (Sveriges Åkeriföretag)
- In Germany: apply via BGL (Federal Association for Goods Transport, Logistics and Disposal)
- Each permit covers one complete return journey (outward + return = one permit)

**Required when transiting through Turkey onwards to a third country:** a bilateral permit is mandatory even if the vehicle holds an EU Community Licence for the EU portion of the journey.

### Permit Application — Turkish Operators (Inbound)

Turkish operators wishing to operate into EU member states apply through **TOBB or the relevant Turkish transport association**. Annual quota allocations are distributed to Turkish operators through TOBB.

### Key Countries with Bilateral Agreements with Turkey

All EU member states, plus: Albania, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Georgia, Iran, Iraq, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kosovo, Kyrgyzstan, Libya, Macedonia, Moldova, Montenegro, Morocco, Pakistan, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Serbia, Syria, Tajikistan, Tunisia, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, United Kingdom, Uzbekistan.

## Community Licence — Position for EU Hauliers

EU Community Licences (issued under EU Regulation 1072/2009) are **not valid substitutes for Turkish bilateral permits**. The EU–Turkey Customs Union covers goods but does not extend to road transport market access. EU hauliers must either:
1. Obtain a valid bilateral permit through their national allocation
2. Use an ITF/CEMT multilateral permit (Turkey is a member)
3. Operate under TIR Convention (for customs transit)

## Documentary Requirements at Turkish Border Crossings

Drivers must carry and present to Turkish customs and transport authorities:

| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Bilateral transport permit (izin belgesi) | Original; must show current validity and correct country pair |
| OR TIR Carnet | If operating under TIR Convention |
| OR ITF/CEMT permit | If using multilateral permit |
| CMR consignment note | Standard international road freight document |
| Vehicle registration certificate | |
| Driver licence (national + IDP if required) | |
| Certificate of professional competence (CPC) | Driver qualification card |
| Vehicle roadworthiness certificate | |
| Tachograph records | AETR applies on Turkey journeys |
| Insurance certificate (third-party liability) | |

## Penalties

Penalties are imposed under Turkish road transport legislation (primarily the Road Transport Law No. 4925 and associated regulations). Amounts are updated periodically (typically annually) by the Ministry of Transport.

| Violation | Penalty (TRY) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without a required bilateral permit | 15,000–50,000 TRY | Vehicle may be immobilised until permit produced or fine paid |
| Operating with expired permit | 10,000–30,000 TRY | |
| Permit does not match vehicle/load type | 10,000–25,000 TRY | |
| Exceeding permitted weight limits | Variable per tonne excess | Additional axle load fines apply |
| AETR tachograph violation | 5,000–20,000 TRY | |
| Operating without TIR Carnet when required | 20,000–60,000 TRY | Detention of vehicle and goods |
| Cabotage violation (Turkish domestic load) | 30,000–100,000 TRY + vehicle detention | Foreign hauliers prohibited from Turkish domestic transport |

*Note: TRY amounts are indicative based on 2024–2025 enforcement practice; verify current rates with UAB or TOBB as Turkish inflation means annual revaluation.*
