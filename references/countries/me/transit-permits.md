# Montenegro — Transit Permits and International Road Transport

**EU Baseline:** Not applicable — standalone national rules. Montenegro is an EU accession candidate (opened accession negotiations 2012; most advanced Western Balkans candidate).

**Last verified: 2026-06-01**

## Overview

International road freight to, from, and through Montenegro requires either a **bilateral road transport permit** or an **ITF/CEMT multilateral permit**. Montenegro is an EU candidate country but EU Community Licences do not provide automatic access to the Montenegrin road transport market.

Montenegro is a **full member of the International Transport Forum (ITF, formerly CEMT)**, so ITF/CEMT multilateral permits from other ITF members are accepted. Montenegro uses the **euro (EUR)** as its currency (unilaterally adopted; not an EU member but uses EUR).

Montenegro is a **NATO member** (since 2017) and among the most advanced EU accession candidates in the Western Balkans. Its road transport legislation is being progressively aligned with EU standards.

Montenegro's strategic position connects Serbia/Bosnia to the Adriatic coast. The **Bar-Boljare motorway** (under construction) will eventually link Montenegro's Adriatic coast to Serbia and the broader E763 corridor (Budapest–Belgrade–Bar).

## ITF/CEMT Multilateral Permits

Montenegro is a **full ITF member**. EU hauliers primarily use ITF/CEMT multilateral permits:

| Permit Type | Validity | Notes |
|-------------|----------|-------|
| Annual ITF permit | Calendar year | Minimum Euro III standard |
| Short-term ITF permit (30-day) | 30 consecutive days | |

Cabotage within Montenegro is not permitted under ITF permits.

---

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits

Montenegro has bilateral road transport agreements with EU member states individually and with neighbouring countries.

| Permit Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral permit | For direct transport between Montenegro and partner country |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Montenegro |
| Third-country permit | For transport involving a third country |

Key bilateral partners: Serbia, Croatia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Albania, Kosovo, Italy (Adriatic ferry connection), and EU member states.

---

## TIR Convention

Montenegro is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR Carnets are used for international transit operations.

## Adriatic Ferry Operations

Montenegro's main port **Bar** has RoRo ferry connections to Italian Adriatic ports (Bari, Ancona) operated by Montenegrin and Italian shipping lines. Road freight can use this maritime leg, requiring:
- Bilateral permits covering Italy/EU side of journey
- CMR consignment note
- Appropriate customs documents for goods

## Penalties

Penalties under Montenegrin road transport law. Montenegro uses **EUR** as its currency.

| Violation | Penalty (EUR) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required permit | 2,000–8,000 EUR | Vehicle detention possible |
| Permit conditions exceeded | 1,000–4,000 EUR | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 500–3,000 EUR | |
| Overloaded vehicle | Variable per tonne excess | |
| Cabotage violation | 3,000–10,000 EUR | |

*Note: EUR amounts are indicative; verify current rates with Montenegrin transport authorities.*
