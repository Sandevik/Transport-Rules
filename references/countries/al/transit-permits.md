# Albania — Transit Permits and International Road Transport

**EU Baseline:** Not applicable — standalone national rules. Albania is an EU accession candidate (candidate status since 2014; accession negotiations opened 2022).

**Last verified: 2026-06-01**

## Overview

International road freight to, from, and through Albania requires either a **bilateral road transport permit** or an **ITF/CEMT multilateral permit**. Albania is an EU candidate country but EU Community Licences do not provide automatic market access.

Albania is a **full member of the International Transport Forum (ITF, formerly CEMT)** and a **NATO member** (since 2009). ITF/CEMT multilateral permits from other ITF member countries are accepted. Albania is also a member of the **CEFTA (Central European Free Trade Agreement)** trading bloc with other Western Balkan countries.

The national currency is the **ALL (Albanian lek)**.

Albania's road network connects to the **Via Egnatia (E90)** east–west corridor and to the **Corridor VIII** (Durrës–Tirana–Skopje–Sofia–Varna), a priority Western Balkans transport corridor. The **Adriatic–Ionian highway** project also runs through Albania on the coastal route.

---

## ITF/CEMT Multilateral Permits

Albania is a **full ITF member**:

| Permit Type | Validity | Notes |
|-------------|----------|-------|
| Annual ITF permit | Calendar year | Minimum Euro III standard |
| Short-term ITF permit (30-day) | 30 consecutive days | |

ITF permits authorise bilateral transport and transit; cabotage within Albania is prohibited under ITF permits.

---

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits

Albania has bilateral road transport agreements with EU member states and neighbouring countries.

| Permit Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral permit | For direct transport between Albania and partner country |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Albania |
| Third-country permit | For transport involving a third country |

Key bilateral partners: Italy (main trading partner, Adriatic ferry connection), Greece, North Macedonia, Kosovo, Montenegro, Serbia, and EU member states.

### Italy–Albania Adriatic Ferry Operations

Italy is Albania's primary trading partner and the main route for European road freight is the **Adriatic ferry** crossing:
- **Durrës port** ↔ **Bari/Ancona/Brindisi** (Italy)
- **Vlorë port** ↔ **Brindisi** (Italy)
- RoRo ferry operations require bilateral permits on both sides; CMR consignment notes; Italian territory uses Community Licence

---

## TIR Convention

Albania is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR Carnets are used for international transit operations, particularly for traffic on Corridor VIII connecting Durrës with Bulgaria.

---

## Corridor VIII — Transit Through Albania

**Corridor VIII** connects the Adriatic (Durrës port) through Albania and North Macedonia to Bulgaria (Sofia/Varna on the Black Sea):
- Durrës → Tirana → Elbasan → Pogradec (Ohrid Lake) → North Macedonia

This corridor is a priority infrastructure project for the Western Balkans. Road infrastructure upgrades are ongoing.

| Violation | Penalty (ALL) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|

| Permit conditions exceeded | 50,000–150,000 ALL | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 30,000–100,000 ALL | |
| Overloaded vehicle | Variable per tonne excess | |
| Cabotage violation | 150,000–400,000 ALL | |
