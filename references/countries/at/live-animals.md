## Overview

Austria applies Regulation (EC) 1/2005 as EU law. The BMLRT (Bundesministerium für Land- und Forstwirtschaft, Regionen und Tourismus) is the national competent authority, with enforcement by Landesveterinärbehörden (state veterinary authorities). Austria's Tierschutzgesetz (TSchG) provides additional national welfare standards that in some areas exceed EU minimum requirements. Austria is a significant transit country for live cattle and pigs between Eastern/Central Europe.

## National Specifics

**Tierschutzgesetz (TSchG) additions:**
- Austrian animal welfare law may impose stricter minimum space requirements and rest periods than the EU baseline in certain circumstances
- State veterinary inspectors (Amtstierärzte) conduct roadside checks, particularly on Brenner and Tauern transit routes
- Transporters who repeatedly violate welfare rules may have Austrian authorisation revoked independent of EU-level sanctions

**Long-distance transit through Alps:**
- Austria is a key inspection point for cattle and pig transports transiting from Eastern Europe to Italy/Spain
- Veterinary rest points (Kontrollstellen) are designated on the Brenner and Tauern corridors

## Documentation

- Type 2 authorisation (copy must be in vehicle)
- Driver Certificate of Competence (for type 2 journeys)
- Journey log (for journeys >8 hours)
- Veterinary certificate of fitness for transport (Gesundheitsbescheinigung)
- Relevant animal movement/health certificates

## Penalties

| Violation | Penalty (EUR) | Legal basis |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Transporting without Type 2 authorisation | Up to €15,000 | TSchG §38 |
| Failure to maintain journey log | Up to €3,750 | VO (EG) 1/2005 + TSchG |
| Exceeding journey times | Up to €15,000 | TSchG §38 |
| Welfare violation causing suffering | Up to €7,500 (repeat: up to €15,000) | TSchG §38 |
