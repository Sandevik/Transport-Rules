## Overview

Austria applies EU emission standards directly as uniform EU law. The BMK (Bundesministerium für Klimaschutz, Umwelt, Energie, Mobilität, Innovation und Technologie) is responsible for transport emission policy. Austria has specific national measures targeting HGV transit through the Alps, which creates additional operational constraints beyond standard EU emission rules.

---

## Euro VII Application

No Austrian national deviations from the EU Euro VII timeline:
- New type approvals: **1 July 2026**
- All new registrations: **1 July 2027**

CO2 manufacturer targets apply as EU law (-15%/2025, -45%/2030, -65%/2035, -90%/2040). ZEV mandate: 35% by 2030, 70% by 2035.

---

## Alpine Transit Restrictions (Emission-Related)

Austria uses emission class differentiation in its Brenner corridor management:

**Sectoral Driving Ban (Sektoral-Fahrverbot) — Brenner Corridor:**
- Certain goods categories banned from the Brenner motorway (A13) for >7.5t vehicles
- Categories subject to the ban include construction materials, stone/earth, motor vehicles, steel, waste
- Exemptions available for combined transport and certain high-value goods
- Environmental zone around Innsbruck restricts older-class HGVs

**Night Driving Ban:**
- Austria enforces a general night ban (22:00–05:00) and Sunday ban for HGVs >7.5t on all roads (with exceptions for combined transport and essential goods)

---

## Vienna Environmental Zone

Vienna has implemented air quality zones affecting HGVs. Euro IV minimum required in city centre areas.

---

## National ZEV Incentives

- **Investitionsprämie** for zero-emission commercial vehicles: grants available through AWS (Austria Wirtschaftsservice)
- **KLIEN** (Klima- und Energiefonds): funding for fleet electrification
- ZEV trucks exempt from NoVA (Normverbrauchsabgabe) vehicle registration tax

---

## Deviations from EU Baseline

No deviations from EU emission standards. Austria adds alpine transit management beyond EU minimum requirements.
