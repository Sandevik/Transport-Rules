# Germany — Collective Agreement for Road Transport (Manteltarifvertrag)

**Sources:**
- https://www.bgl-ev.de/ — BGL: Bundesverband Güterkraftverkehr Logistik und Entsorgung (employer association)
- https://www.verdi.de/ — ver.di: Vereinte Dienstleistungsgewerkschaft (union)
- https://www.bmas.de/ — BMAS: Bundesministerium für Arbeit und Soziales (for allgemeinverbindlich declarations)

**Last verified: 2026-06-02**

---

## Overview

Germany's road transport sector is governed by a **Manteltarifvertrag (MTV)** — a framework collective agreement covering general working conditions — together with separate **Entgelttarifverträge** (wage agreements). These are negotiated between the employer association **BGL (Bundesverband Güterkraftverkehr Logistik und Entsorgung)** and the trade union **ver.di (Vereinte Dienstleistungsgewerkschaft)**.

Unlike the Swedish Transportavtalet or the Dutch CAO, German transport collective agreements are **not declared allgemeinverbindlich** (generally applicable) across the sector by default. Only BGL member employers are formally bound. However, in practice the agreements serve as an industry benchmark, and many non-member companies follow the agreed minimum conditions.

> **Note:** Specific current wage rates are not included here as they are renegotiated regularly. Verify current Entgelttarifvertrag rates via BGL (bgl-ev.de) or ver.di.

---

## Regional Structure

German collective bargaining in transport operates on **two levels**:

| Level | Agreement | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **National (Bundes-) level** | Manteltarifvertrag (MTV) — general conditions, working time, overtime rules | Framework conditions for all BGL members |
| **Länder (state) level** | Entgelttarifverträge — actual wage scales | Regional wage variations per Bundesland |

Wages differ by Bundesland — for example, wages in Bavaria (Bayern) or North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW) may differ from those in eastern German Länder.

---

## Statutory Floor: MiLoG

Regardless of collective agreement coverage, all workers in Germany are protected by the **Mindestlohngesetz (MiLoG)** — the national statutory minimum wage law. This is a hard floor: no collective agreement can fall below it, and it applies to all employers including posted workers from other countries.

The base statutory rate was set at **EUR 12.00/hour** (gross) from 1 October 2022 per **MiLoG § 1** (confirmed via gesetze-im-internet.de). Subsequent ordinances by the Mindestlohnkommission have adjusted this rate — verify the current rate at https://www.mindestlohn.de/

---

## Arbeitnehmer-Entsendegesetz (AEntG) — Posted Workers

Under **§ 4 AEntG**, minimum wage rates from collective agreements in the logistics/transport sector can be declared **allgemeinverbindlich** (generally applicable), making them binding on foreign employers posting workers to Germany. However, not all transport wage agreements have been extended this way — check the BMAS register for currently extended agreements.

## Framework

The MTV covers:

| Topic | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Working time reference period | Set in agreement |
| Overtime rates | Premium rates above standard hours |
| Night and weekend work | Premiums for unsocial hours |
| Subsistence allowances | Spesen (Verpflegungsmehraufwand) for days away from home |
| Annual leave (Urlaub) | Minimum 24–30 days depending on seniority/agreement period |
| Notice periods (Kündigungsfristen) | Per agreement and statutory KSchG |
| Sick pay (Entgeltfortzahlung) | Employer pays 6 weeks (100% salary) per EFZG — statutory, not just collective agreement |

### Sick Pay (Entgeltfortzahlung)
Germany has a **statutory** 6-week employer sick pay obligation under the **Entgeltfortzahlungsgesetz (EFZG) §3**:
- Days 1–42 (6 weeks): Employer pays **100% of normal salary** — no waiting day deduction
- Day 43 onwards: Krankenkasse (statutory health insurance) pays **Krankengeld** (~70% of gross salary, up to the Beitragsbemessungsgrenze)

This is superior to the Swedish karensdag system — no first-day deduction under German law.

### Subsistence Allowances (Spesen / Verpflegungsmehraufwand)

German tax law sets flat-rate tax-free allowances for drivers away from home:

| Away from home | Tax-free daily rate |
|---|---|
| Full calendar day away (24h) | EUR 28 |
| Partial day (8+ hours) | EUR 14 |
| Overnight away (employer-paid accommodation) | EUR 20 (Übernachtungspauschale) |

*These are Steuerrecht (tax law) rates — collective agreements may pay higher amounts, but amounts above the Steuerrecht limit are subject to payroll tax.*

## Enforcement

| Authority | Role |
|---|---|
| Zoll (Customs / Finanzkontrolle Schwarzarbeit — FKS) | Enforcement of minimum wages (MiLoG) at roadside and company level |
| BALM | Transport compliance (driving time, tachograph) |
| Arbeitsgericht | Labour court for collective agreement disputes |
| ver.di | Union enforcement via civil law claims |

---

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Paying below MiLoG minimum wage | Fine up to EUR 500,000; back pay | Mindestlohngesetz § 21 |
| Not paying sick pay | Back pay + interest | EFZG |
| Breach of collective agreement | Vertragsstrafe (contractual penalty); Arbeitsgericht claim | Tarifvertragsgesetz (TVG) |
