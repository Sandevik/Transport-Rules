## Overview

German domestic road freight is governed by a well-developed statutory framework that, unlike Sweden, includes **specific statutory provisions** for freight contracts in the **HGB (Handelsgesetzbuch — German Commercial Code)**. The HGB freight provisions (§§ 407–450) apply as a matter of law and cannot be fully excluded by contract, though parties may deviate from most provisions (with some mandatory exceptions) by agreement.

In practice, most German freight forwarding contracts incorporate **ADSp 2017** (Allgemeine Deutsche Spediteurbedingungen), which modifies and supplements the HGB default rules.

## HGB §§ 407–450 — Statutory Road Freight Law

### Scope (HGB § 407)

HGB §§ 407–450 apply to **Frachtverträge** (freight contracts) where goods are carried by a carrier (Frachtführer) for reward. They apply to:
- Domestic German road freight
- Domestic rail, inland waterway, and air freight (with sector-specific modifications)
- Do **not** apply to international road freight where CMR applies (CMR takes precedence)

### Key HGB Provisions

| Section | Topic | Key rule |
|---------|-------|----------|
| § 407 | Contract definition | The carrier (Frachtführer) undertakes to carry goods and deliver to consignee |
| § 408 | Frachtbrief (consignment note) | Carrier may require issuance of Frachtbrief; provides evidence of contract |
| § 409 | Multiple originals | Parties may agree on number of copies |
| § 411 | Dangerous goods | Sender must disclose dangerous nature; carrier may refuse or take safety measures |
| § 412 | Loading/unloading | Unless agreed otherwise, sender loads, carrier unloads |
| § 413 | Delivery instructions | Carrier must follow sender's instructions; changing instructions if delivery impossible |
| § 414 | Sender's liability | Sender liable for damage caused by defective packing or dangerous goods disclosure failure |
| § 415 | Withdrawal before loading | Sender may withdraw goods; pays compensation |
| § 418 | Changed instructions after loading | Consignee has right of disposal after taking over document rights |
| § 421 | Delivery | Carrier must deliver to designated consignee |
| § 422 | Consignee rights | Consignee acquiring rights on acceptance of delivery |
| § 423 | Freight payment | Freight due on delivery unless prepaid |
| § 425 | Carrier liability | Carrier liable for loss, damage, or delay from taking-over to delivery |
| § 426 | Exemptions | Force majeure, inherent vice, sender's fault, inadequate packing by sender |
| § 427 | Special risks | Carrier partially exonerated where damage arises from agreed open loading, inherent liability to wastage, etc. |
| § 428 | Unavoidable events | Carrier not liable for unforeseeable events it could not prevent |
| § 429 | Liability limits | See below — €5/kg for loss/damage |
| § 431 | Delay compensation | Up to 3× freight charge for delay |
| § 432 | Unlimited liability | Wilful misconduct or gross negligence of carrier — limits do not apply |
| § 438 | Claims time bar | 1 year (3 years for wilful misconduct) |
| § 440 | Lien | Carrier has lien over goods for freight and costs |
| § 447 | Successive carriers | Joint and several liability; apportionment rules |
| § 449 | Derogations | Certain provisions may be modified by written agreement (§§ 414, 418, 422, 423 etc.) |
| § 450 | Application to forwarders | Where forwarder acts as principal carrier, HGB §§ 407–450 apply |

## Liability Under HGB §§ 425–432

### Liability Standard (§ 425)

Unlike ABV 2019 (fault-based), HGB § 425 imposes **strict liability** on the carrier for loss, damage, and delay occurring between taking-over and delivery. This is aligned with CMR's approach — the carrier must establish a defence rather than the sender proving fault.

**Defences available to the carrier (§ 426, § 427, § 428):**
- Force majeure (unforeseeable, unavoidable circumstances)
- Inherent vice of the goods
- Inadequate packing by sender (where carrier could not reasonably discover the defect)
- Natural properties of the goods causing wastage
- Sender's or consignee's instructions

### Liability Limits (§ 429)

| Damage type | HGB § 429 limit |
|-------------|----------------|
| Loss or damage to goods | **€5.00 per kilogram** of gross weight of goods affected |
| Maximum per consignment | **€100,000** (per loss event, unless higher value declared) |
| Delay | Up to **3× freight charge** |

**Comparison with CMR:**
- CMR: 8.33 SDR/kg ≈ €11–12/kg (based on gross weight of lost/damaged goods)
- HGB: €5/kg (per affected goods) — lower than CMR for most cargo types

*Note: CMR's limit (8.33 SDR/kg) is higher per kg than HGB's €5/kg. However, HGB §449 allows parties to agree higher limits by written contract, and the ADSp 2017 provides modifications.*

### Higher Declared Value

Like CMR and ABV, the sender may declare a higher value (HGB § 432). If a higher value is agreed and shown on the Frachtbrief, the carrier's liability is limited to the declared amount.

### Unlimited Liability — § 432 (Wilful Misconduct / Gross Negligence)

HGB § 432 removes the liability cap in cases of **wilful misconduct (Vorsatz)** or **gross negligence (grobe Fahrlässigkeit)** by the carrier. This is stricter than CMR, which only removes the cap for wilful misconduct (not gross negligence).

Practical implication: German courts frequently find gross negligence where a carrier fails to follow basic security procedures (leaving vehicle unattended with keys in ignition, allowing theft from an unsecured depot) — in such cases unlimited liability applies.

## ADSp 2017 — Allgemeine Deutsche Spediteurbedingungen

### What ADSp Is

ADSp 2017 (Allgemeine Deutsche Spediteurbedingungen — General German Freight Forwarder Standard Terms) are the standard terms for **freight forwarding** (Spedition) contracts in Germany. They are issued jointly by:
- Bundesverband Spedition und Logistik (DSLV — German Freight Forwarder Association)
- Various shipper and industry organisations

ADSp 2017 applies to contracts with **freight forwarders (Spediteure)** but not directly to carrier (Frachtführer) contracts. However, many transport operators in Germany operate both as carriers and as forwarders depending on the transaction.

### ADSp 2017 vs. HGB: Key Differences

| Issue | HGB default (carrier) | ADSp 2017 (forwarder) |
|-------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Scope | Carrier contracts (Frachtvertrag) | Forwarder contracts (Speditionsvertrag) |
| Liability limit per kg | €5/kg | **€5/kg** (same as HGB — ADSp 2017 adopts HGB limit) |
| Liability cap per claim | €100,000 | **€1,000,000** per damage event (higher than HGB default) |
| Aggregate annual cap | Not in HGB | Typically negotiated in framework agreements |
| Gross negligence | Unlimited liability (§ 432 HGB) | ADSp clauses attempt to limit gross negligence liability — but German courts have found some such clauses void under AGB-Kontrolle (standard terms control) |
| Storage | Not in HGB freight provisions | ADSp addresses warehousing |
| Multi-modal | HGB has separate provisions per mode | ADSp covers all modes |

**Important:** ADSp 2017 clauses that attempt to limit liability for gross negligence are subject to judicial scrutiny under German AGB law (§§ 305–310 BGB) and may be found void (unenforceable) as unfair standard terms. German courts have repeatedly struck down such clauses. Freight forwarders should not assume ADSp fully shields them from gross negligence liability.

### ADSp 2017 on Dangerous Goods

ADSp 2017 contains specific provisions on dangerous goods:
- Sender must declare dangerous goods and provide required documentation
- If sender fails to declare, the forwarder may exercise special rights (abandon goods, seek compensation for additional costs)
- Forwarder's liability for dangerous goods incidents caused by undisclosed dangerous goods lies with the sender

## GüKG — Güterkraftverkehrsgesetz (Road Freight Transport Act)

The **GüKG** regulates the **public law** aspects of commercial road freight in Germany — the operator licence, cabotage rules, and road transport market access. It is the implementation of EU Regulation 1071/2009 and 1072/2009 in German national law.

### Key GüKG Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Operator licence (Güterkraftverkehrserlaubnis) | Required for commercial road freight with vehicles >3.5t GVW |
| National transport licence | For domestic German transport (EU Gemeinschaftslizenz for international) |
| Financial standing | Must maintain financial standing — initial capital of €9,000 (first vehicle) + €5,000 each additional |
| Professional competence | Traffic manager (Verkehrsleiter) must hold CPC or equivalent certificate |
| Good repute | Operator and traffic manager must be of good repute — no disqualifying convictions |
| Cabotage rules | International carriers from other EU states may perform max. 3 cabotage operations in 7 days following an incoming international delivery |

### GüKG Enforcement

GüKG violations are enforced by **BALM** (Bundesamt für Logistik und Mobilität, formerly BAG):
- Roadside controls (toll system data crosschecked against licences)
- Random checks at loading facilities and border crossings
- Company-level audits

## Claims and Time Limits Under HGB

### Notification of Damage

| Situation | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Apparent damage (visible at delivery) | Note on delivery receipt at time of delivery (Vorbehalt) |
| Non-apparent damage | Written notice within **7 days** of delivery |
| Total loss | No notification required for claim — but prompt notification assists investigation |

### Time Bar (§ 438 HGB)

| Claim type | Limitation period |
|-----------|-------------------|
| General loss, damage, delay | **1 year** from delivery (or scheduled delivery date) |
| Wilful misconduct by carrier | **3 years** |

## Applicable Courts and Jurisdiction

- **Amtsgericht (AG)** — for claims up to €5,000
- **Landgericht (LG)** — for claims above €5,000; specialist commercial chambers (Kammer für Handelssachen) in larger cities
- **Oberlandesgericht (OLG)** — appeal court
- **Bundesgerichtshof (BGH)** — final appeal; BGH has significant case law on HGB/ADSp liability issues

**Arbitration:** Parties may agree to arbitration. The DIS (Deutsche Institution für Schiedsgerichtsbarkeit) is the main German arbitration institution. Arbitration clauses in ADSp must comply with AGB law requirements to be enforceable.

## Practical Comparison: HGB (DE) vs. ABV (SE) vs. CMR

| Issue | HGB §§407-450 (DE domestic) | ABV 2019 (SE domestic) | CMR (international) |
|-------|-----------------------------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Source | Statutory (mandatory) | Contractual (agreed) | Treaty (mandatory for international) |
| Liability standard | Strict | Fault-based | Strict |
| Liability limit | €5/kg; cap €100,000 | 150 SEK/kg (~€13/kg) | 8.33 SDR/kg (~€11/kg) |
| Gross negligence | Unlimited liability | General contract law | Only wilful misconduct — unlimited |
| Delay limit | 3× freight charge | Freight charge | Freight charge |
| Time bar | 1 year (3 for wilful) | 1 year | 1 year (3 for wilful) |
| Non-apparent damage notice | 7 days | 7 days | 7 days |
| Governing law | German law | Swedish law | CMR Convention |
| Courts | German courts / DIS | Swedish courts / SCC | Any CMR-state court with jurisdiction |

## Penalties

HGB violations are primarily civil — they result in civil liability, not regulatory penalties. Regulatory (GüKG) violations carry administrative penalties:

| Violation | Consequence |
|-----------|------------|
| Operating without operator licence (GüKG) | Fine up to €20,000; vehicle immobilisation; BALM enforcement |
| Carrier fails to pay HGB § 429 liability (loss/damage) | Civil judgment; possible enforcement through Zwangsvollstreckung |
| ADSp limitation clause found void (gross negligence) | Unlimited liability to shipper for full commercial value |
| Cabotage violation (>3 operations in 7 days) | Fine up to €30,000 per violation (BALM) |
| Failure to disclose dangerous goods (GüKG/GGVSEB) | Fine; potential criminal liability if injury results |
