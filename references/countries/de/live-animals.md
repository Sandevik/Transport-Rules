## Overview

Germany applies Regulation (EC) 1/2005 directly as uniform EU law. The competent authority is the BMEL (Bundesministerium für Ernährung und Landwirtschaft), with enforcement delegated to the Länder (state) veterinary offices. Germany is a major transit country for live animals between Northern/Eastern and Southern Europe. Germany's Tierschutzgesetz (Animal Protection Act) provides additional national animal welfare requirements that may be stricter than the EU baseline.

---

## Authorisation Requirements

| Journey type | Authorisation | Requirements |
|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| ≤65 km from departure | Type 1 (registration) | Basic training, vehicle requirements |
| >65 km | Type 2 (authorisation) | Valid 5 years; veterinary certificate of fitness; CoC for driver/attendant |
| Long journeys >8h | Type 2 | Journey log required; temperature monitoring; watering/feeding records |

Type 2 authorisations for German-registered transporters are issued by the Landesveterinärbehörde (state veterinary authority) of the transporter's registered state.

---

## Driver Certificate of Competence

For type 2 (long-distance) animal transport, the driver and attendant must hold a **Certificate of Competence (CoC)** issued by a national authority. German CoCs are issued by the Länder veterinary authorities.

---

## Journey Logs

Required for all journeys exceeding 8 hours. Must include:
- Departure and destination details
- Feeding and watering intervals
- Rest stops
- Veterinary check signatures (for very long journeys)

---

## National Specifics (Tierschutzgesetz)

Germany's Tierschutzgesetz (TierSchG) may impose stricter standards than EU minimum requirements:
- Stricter enforcement of space allowances
- State veterinary inspectors may inspect loads at rest points
- Germany has been proactive in enforcing EU long-journey rules for export cattle

---

## Border Inspection Posts (BIPs) for Third-Country Imports

Major German BIPs for live animal imports from non-EU countries:
- Hamburg port (Hafentierärztlicher Dienst)
- Frankfurt airport (Luftfrachtumschlag, veterinary check)
- Saarbrücken (road BIP for third-country road imports)

## Enforcement

| Authority | Full name | Website |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| BMEL | Bundesministerium für Ernährung und Landwirtschaft (national policy) | https://www.bmel.de/ |
| Landesveterinärbehörden | State veterinary authorities (Länder) — enforcement and authorisation | varies by Bundesland |
| BVL | Bundesamt für Verbraucherschutz und Lebensmittelsicherheit | https://www.bvl.bund.de/ |

**Advance payment at roadside:** YES — veterinary inspectors can stop and detain vehicles; security deposits may be demanded for serious violations.

---

## Penalties

| Violation | Penalty (EUR) | Legal basis |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Transporting animals without valid Type 2 authorisation | Up to €25,000 | TierSchG §18 |
| Failure to maintain journey log | Up to €5,000 | VO (EG) 1/2005 + TierSchG |
| Exceeding maximum journey times | Up to €25,000 | TierSchG §18 |
| Failure to provide feed/water at required intervals | Up to €25,000 | TierSchG §18 |
| Overcrowding animals beyond space allowances | Up to €25,000 | TierSchG §18 |
