## Overview

The UK operates a standalone framework post-Brexit. UK weight and dimension limits were originally derived from EU Directive 96/53/EC and retained as domestic law (Road Vehicles (Authorised Weight) Regulations 1998, as amended by SI 2023 amendments). Limits are broadly equivalent to EU rules with some differences — notably the UK maximum GVW of **44t** for a 6-axle articulated vehicle (vs EU 40t standard), and no statutory maximum height. DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency) is the primary authority.

| Semi-trailer + tractor | 16.5m | SI 1998/3111 |

## Oversize/Overweight — STGO Categories

Vehicles exceeding authorised weights use the Special Types General Order (STGO SI 2003/1998):

| Category | Max GVW | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| STGO Cat 1 | 80t | Highway authority notification; min 2 days notice |
| STGO Cat 2 | 150t | Notice to highway authority + police; 5 days advance |
| STGO Cat 3 | Unlimited | Secretary of State individual order; police escort |

- Pilot vehicle required: width > 3.5m or length > 25.9m (STGO Cat 1+)
- Application: via highway authority (Highways England / National Highways for motorways; local authorities for other roads)

## Bridge Restrictions

UK has no national BK-class system. Individual bridges have posted weight limits (signed in tonnes). The bridge assessment standard is **BD 86** (Design Manual for Roads and Bridges). Structural weight limits below 7.5t are signed; operators must check route for bridge restrictions before moving abnormal loads.

## Penalties

| Violation | Penalty (GBP) | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overloading | Up to £5,000 per axle | Road Traffic Act 1988 |
| No STGO authorisation | Up to £2,500 | STGO / RTA 1988 |
| Exceeding gross weight | Up to £5,000 | Road Traffic Act 1988 |
| Bridge damage (liable) | Civil damages claim | Common law + Highways Act 1980 |
