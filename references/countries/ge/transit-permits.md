## Overview

Georgia is not an EU member state but has concluded a **Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area (DCFTA) Agreement** with the EU (part of the EU–Georgia Association Agreement, provisionally applied since 2014). The DCFTA includes provisions on transport services, but Georgia does not yet have full access to the EU road transport market on par with EU member states. EU hauliers operating in Georgia require bilateral permits or ITF/CEMT permits.

Georgia is strategically significant as a **key hub on the Middle Corridor (Trans-Caspian International Transport Route — TITR)**, connecting the EU via Turkey and the Black Sea to Azerbaijan, the Caspian Sea, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, and onward to China. The Middle Corridor has gained major importance since 2022 as an alternative to routes through Russia/Belarus.

Georgia is a **full member of TRACECA** (Transport Corridor Europe–Caucasus–Asia), an EU-supported programme promoting transport links between Europe and Central Asia.

---

## EU–Georgia DCFTA — Transport Context

The EU–Georgia Association Agreement (including DCFTA) covers:
- Regulatory approximation of Georgian transport legislation to EU standards
- Road safety standards
- Technical requirements for vehicles
- Professional driver qualification standards (gradual approximation to EU CPC system)

**Road market access:** Georgia has not concluded a full EU-level road transport liberalisation agreement equivalent to EU–Ukraine wartime arrangement. Bilateral permits between EU member states and Georgia remain the standard mechanism.

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits

### Permit Structure

Georgia maintains bilateral road transport agreements with most European countries and many Asian countries. EU member states each have their own bilateral agreement with Georgia.

| Permit Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral transport permit | For direct transport between Georgia and the partner country |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Georgia without loading/unloading |
| Third-country permit | For transport involving a third country |

### UK-Georgia Bilateral Permits

Under the UK-Georgia bilateral agreement:
- Single-journey permits available via DVSA (£8)
- Own account journeys require a permit

### ITF/CEMT Multilateral Permits

Georgia is a **full member of the International Transport Forum (ITF)**. ITF/CEMT multilateral permits (annual and 30-day) are **valid in Georgia** for operations by operators from other ITF member countries:
- This is the primary route for EU hauliers, avoiding the need for country-specific bilateral permits
- Annual or short-term ITF permits must meet minimum Euro emission standard requirements
- Permits authorise bilateral and transit operations; cabotage remains prohibited

### Permit Application

- Foreign operators: apply through national competent authority or transport association
- Georgian operators: apply through the Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development or the relevant national agency
- Annual quota allocations under bilateral agreements; ITF permits allocated by national ITF body

## TIR Convention

Georgia is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR is widely used for Georgia's international road freight:

- National guaranteeing association: **GICC (Georgian International Carriers' Council)** or equivalent IRU-affiliated body
- TIR is essential for transit movements on the Middle Corridor: EU → Turkey → Georgia → Azerbaijan (or via Black Sea ferry)
- TIR Carnets simplify customs transit through multiple countries on the corridor

## Middle Corridor (Trans-Caspian International Transport Route — TITR)

Georgia plays a central role in the **Middle Corridor**, which has emerged as a major alternative to the Northern Corridor (through Russia/Belarus) since the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

### Route

EU → (Turkey overland or Black Sea ferry) → **Georgia** → Azerbaijan → (Caspian Sea ferry: Alat or Baku → Aktau/Turkmenbashi) → Kazakhstan / Uzbekistan / Turkmenistan → China

### Georgia's Key Role

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Entry from Turkey | Sarpi border crossing (A2 motorway, near Batumi on Black Sea coast) |
| Black Sea ferry entry | Poti port or Batumi port (from Romanian, Ukrainian, or Turkish Black Sea ports) |
| Transit through Georgia | Batumi/Poti → Tbilisi → Red Bridge (Georgian–Azerbaijani border) |
| Exit to Azerbaijan | Red Bridge (Krasny Most) border crossing on the Georgian Military Highway, or Lower Lars alternatives |

### Operational Considerations

- **Sarpi border** (Georgia–Turkey): major crossing on E70 motorway; peak queuing times can be significant
- **Red Bridge** (Georgia–Azerbaijan): main eastern crossing; commercial freight flows are subject to processing times
- **Poti and Batumi ports**: significant RoRo and container infrastructure for Black Sea ferry connections
- Georgia's road infrastructure has been substantially upgraded; the E60 (east–west) and S1 (north–south via Tbilisi ring) are key arteries

### Recent TRACECA Developments (2024–2026)

- TRACECA's 18th Annual Meeting (May 2026) in Astana focused on "development of international road transportation"
- Multimodal digital corridor development being advanced jointly with UNECE
- Infrastructure investments: road corridors in Georgia, rail modernisation, maritime facility expansion at Poti and Batumi

## Penalties

Penalties under Georgian road transport legislation (Law on Road Transport and related regulations). Amounts in GEL (Georgian lari).

| Violation | Penalty (GEL) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required permit | 2,000–5,000 GEL | Vehicle may be detained |
| Permit expired or conditions exceeded | 1,000–3,000 GEL | |
| Overloaded vehicle | 1,000–4,000 GEL + per-tonne excess charge | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 500–2,000 GEL | |
| Driving without CPC documentation | 500–1,500 GEL | |
| Cabotage violation (Georgian domestic load by foreign carrier) | 3,000–6,000 GEL | |

*Note: GEL amounts are indicative; Georgian transport fines are updated periodically. Verify current rates with the Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development.*
