## Overview

Denmark implements Directive 92/106/EEC through national transport law. Denmark's geography — a peninsula and islands connected by bridges and ferries — makes maritime and fixed-link combined transport central to its logistics. The Great Belt Bridge and Øresund Bridge connect Jutland to Zealand and Sweden; ferry connections serve Germany, Norway, Sweden, Poland, and the Baltic. Denmark has no general HGV weekend driving bans.

---

## Weight Allowance

5+ axle combinations on qualifying combined transport road legs: **44 tonnes**.

---

## Key Intermodal Infrastructure

| Terminal | Location | Connections |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Aarhus port | Jutland | Container; major Danish port |
| Copenhagen (Nordhavn) | Zealand | Container + Ro-Ro |
| Fredericia | Jutland | Rail hub; Jutland/bridges nexus |
| Padborg | German border | Major road/rail interchange with Germany |

**Ferry routes qualifying as combined transport maritime legs:**
- Frederikshavn–Oslo (Norway)
- Rødby–Puttgarden (Germany, Femern Belt)
- Copenhagen–Rostock (Germany)
- Esbjerg–Immingham (UK)
- Gedser–Rostock (Germany)

The planned **Femern Belt Fixed Link** (tunnel between Denmark and Germany) will create a major new combined transport corridor when completed (~2029).

---

## Documentation Required

- Ferry or rail terminal booking/confirmation
- CMR waybill for road leg
- Vehicle registration confirming ≥5 axles (for 44t)
