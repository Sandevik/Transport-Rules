## Overview

Serbia is a candidate country for EU membership but is not yet an EU member state. International road freight to, from, and through Serbia requires either a **bilateral road transport permit** or an **ITF/CEMT multilateral permit**. EU hauliers holding an EU Community Licence **do not** automatically have unrestricted access to Serbia; a bilateral permit or ITF/CEMT permit is required.

Serbia is a **full member of the International Transport Forum (ITF, formerly CEMT)**, meaning ITF/CEMT multilateral permits issued in any other ITF member country are valid for operations in and through Serbia. This is the primary route for EU hauliers.

Serbia is a strategically important transit country on the **E-75 corridor** (Budapest–Novi Sad–Belgrade–Niš–Skopje–Thessaloniki), one of the main European land routes between Central Europe and Greece/Turkey.

---

## ITF/CEMT Multilateral Permits

Serbia is a **full ITF member** and accepts ITF/CEMT multilateral permits. This is the most commonly used permit mechanism for EU hauliers operating in Serbia.

### Permit Types Accepted in Serbia

| Type | Validity | Euro emission class required |
|------|----------|------------------------------|
| Annual ITF permit | Calendar year | Minimum Euro III (standard); Euro V or VI for "Green" permits |
| Short-term ITF permit (30-day) | 30 consecutive days | Minimum Euro III |

### Conditions for ITF Permits in Serbia

- The permit must be for a vehicle registered in an ITF member country
- The vehicle must meet the minimum Euro emission standard stated on the permit
- The permit must be carried in the vehicle and presented to enforcement authorities
- ITF permits authorise bilateral transport (between the permit country and Serbia) and transit through Serbia
- **Cabotage within Serbia is not permitted** under ITF multilateral permits

### Allocation

ITF/CEMT permits are allocated annually by each member country's competent authority. EU operators apply through their national allocation body.

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits

Serbia has concluded bilateral road transport agreements with most European countries. Bilateral permits are an alternative to ITF/CEMT permits.

### EU–Serbia Bilateral Agreement

Serbia has individual bilateral road transport agreements with each EU member state (not a single EU-level agreement, as Serbia is not an EU member). Key features:
- **Quotas** are exchanged annually at Joint Commission meetings between Serbia's Ministry of Construction, Transport and Infrastructure and each partner country's authority
- EU member states with high traffic volumes with Serbia (Germany, Hungary, Austria, Slovenia, Romania, Bulgaria, Croatia) typically have larger bilateral quotas
- Permits are issued by the competent authority/transport association in the haulier's home country

### Permit Types

| Permit Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral permit | For direct transport between Serbia and the permit-issuing country |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Serbia without loading/unloading |
| Third-country permit | For transport involving a third country other than Serbia |

### Permit Allocation in Serbia

Serbian operators apply for outbound permits through the **Directorate for Road Transport** under the Ministry of Construction, Transport and Infrastructure, and/or through:
- **Udruženje prevoznika** — Serbian road carriers' association, involved in bilateral permit distribution

## TIR Convention

Serbia is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR Carnets are used for customs transit through Serbia:
- National guaranteeing association in Serbia: **APPS (Asocijacija prevoznika i speditera Srbije)** or the relevant IRU-affiliated association
- TIR is widely used for operations between EU and Turkey, the Middle East, and other destinations using the E-75 corridor through Serbia

## E-75 Corridor — Transit Through Serbia

The E-75 motorway (European route) is the primary north–south road freight corridor through Serbia:

- **Entry from Hungary:** Horgoš border crossing (A1 motorway) — busiest freight crossing with EU
- **Transit:** Subotica → Novi Sad → Belgrade → Niš
- **Exit to North Macedonia:** Preševo/Tabanovce border crossing
- **Exit to Bulgaria:** Dimitrovgrad border crossing (E-80)
- **Exit to Romania/Croatia/Bosnia:** Various crossings

**Key border crossings:**

| Crossing | Partner Country | Road | Notes |
|----------|----------------|------|-------|
| Horgoš | Hungary | A1/M5 | Main freight entry from EU |
| Batrovci | Croatia | E-70 | Major west–east corridor |
| Šid | Croatia | E-70 | |
| Gradina | Bulgaria | E-80 | |
| Preševo | North Macedonia | E-75 | Main E-75 south |
| Vatin | Romania | E-70 | |

**HGV restrictions:** Serbia has weekend driving bans for heavy goods vehicles on certain roads during summer months (typically July–August); verify current restrictions with the Ministry.

## Community Licence — Position for EU Hauliers

EU Community Licences are **not a substitute for Serbian bilateral or ITF permits**. EU hauliers need:
1. An ITF/CEMT multilateral permit (most common route), OR
2. A bilateral permit under the relevant EU member state–Serbia bilateral agreement
3. TIR Carnet (for customs transit movements)

## Penalties

Penalties under the Serbian Law on Road Transport and related regulations. Amounts in RSD (Serbian dinar).

| Violation | Penalty (RSD) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required permit (bilateral or CEMT) | 300,000–600,000 RSD | Vehicle immobilisation possible |
| Permit does not match vehicle/journey | 150,000–300,000 RSD | |
| Cabotage violation (Serbian domestic load by foreign carrier) | 300,000–600,000 RSD + vehicle detention | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 100,000–300,000 RSD | Driver and/or company |
| Overweight vehicle | Variable per tonne excess | Additional road damage charge |
| Operating without driver CPC | 100,000–200,000 RSD | |

*Note: RSD amounts are indicative; Serbian transport fines are updated periodically. Verify current rates with the Serbian Ministry of Construction, Transport and Infrastructure or Directorate for Road Transport.*
