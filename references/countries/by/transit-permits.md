## Overview

Belarus is not an EU member state. Since April 2022, EU sanctions (Council Regulation (EU) 2022/576) prohibit Belarusian road transport operators from operating in the EU. EU carriers face severe restrictions for operations involving Belarus. The pre-sanction bilateral permit system is described below for historical/context reference only. **Current operations involving Belarus require careful legal review.**

---

## ⚠ CRITICAL SANCTIONS WARNING

> **Belarusian road transport operators are PROHIBITED from operating in the EU under Council Regulation (EU) 2022/576 (in force from 9 April 2022). EU-based carriers face severe restrictions for operations involving Belarus. Read this section before any Belarus-related transport planning.**

---

## EU Sanctions on Belarus Road Transport (Since April 2022)

### Council Regulation (EU) 2022/576

Adopted 8 April 2022 as part of the fourth package of EU sanctions against Belarus (following the forced landing of Ryanair flight 4978 and associated human rights violations, and Belarus's role in facilitating the Russian invasion of Ukraine).

**Key prohibition:** Belarusian road transport operators **may not carry goods by road in the EU** — including transit through EU member states.

**Specifically prohibited:**
- Belarusian hauliers transporting goods **to, from, through, or within** EU member states
- EU hauliers are NOT prohibited from entering Belarus, but practical and legal complications are severe
- Bilateral permit issuance to Belarusian operators by EU member states has effectively ceased

**Exemptions that may apply:**
- Mail (postal services) — potentially exempt under specific humanitarian carve-outs
- Humanitarian aid operations — check current derogation status with competent authority
- Transit through Belarus (by EU hauliers going east, e.g. to Russia/Kazakhstan) — note UK has parallel restrictions; border crossing with Russia/Belarus complications

**Practical effect:**
- Belarusian trucks cannot legally enter any EU member state to collect or deliver goods
- EU freight formerly handled by Belarusian hauliers has shifted to Polish, Lithuanian, and other EU carriers
- Polish border crossings (Kukuryki, Terespol) and Lithuanian crossings (Medininkai, Šalčininkai) see EU-side freight loading/unloading at border logistics hubs

### UK Parallel Sanctions

The United Kingdom implemented parallel restrictions:
- Belarusian road transport operators cannot operate in Great Britain
- Bilateral permits for Belarus are currently not available through UK DVSA: guidance states "Currently cannot get these bilateral permits...because of the Russian invasion"
- UK hauliers face significant practical barriers for Belarus operations

### Other Countries

Several other non-EU countries (e.g. Norway, Canada) have imposed similar restrictions. EU candidate countries are expected to align.

## Current Operational Status for EU Hauliers

| Scenario | Status |
|----------|--------|
| EU haulier → Belarus (bilateral, delivering/collecting) | Legally possible but high risk; consult legal/compliance counsel; bilateral permit in principle available but practical barriers severe |
| EU haulier transiting Belarus to Russia | Extremely complex; Russian sanctions compound issues; avoid unless essential |
| Belarusian haulier → EU | **PROHIBITED under EU Regulation 2022/576** |
| TIR transit through Belarus by EU haulier | Technically possible using TIR Convention but Belarus-Russia border situation, sanctions enforcement, and insurance complications make this high risk |
| Belarusian transit through EU | **PROHIBITED** |

**Recommendation:** EU and UK operators should avoid all transport involving Belarus unless they have obtained specific legal advice and confirmed compliance with all applicable sanctions. Violations carry severe penalties.

## Pre-Sanction Permit System (Historical Reference)

Prior to April 2022, Belarus operated a standard bilateral road transport permit system:

### Permit Types (Pre-2022)

| Permit Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Bilateral transport permit | For direct transport between Belarus and a specific country |
| CEMT/ITF multilateral permit | Belarus is a member of the ITF (formerly CEMT) |
| Transit permit | For vehicles transiting Belarus without loading/unloading |

### CEMT/ITF Status

Belarus is a **member of the International Transport Forum (ITF)**. However, ITF membership and multilateral permit acceptance does not override the EU sanctions regime for EU member state operators.

### Bilateral Agreements (Pre-2022)

Belarus had bilateral road transport agreements with all EU member states, Russia, Ukraine, and most other European and CIS countries. These agreements are effectively suspended between Belarus and EU member states due to sanctions.

### TIR Convention

Belarus is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**. TIR Carnets have historically been widely used for transit through Belarus on the East–West corridor (EU ↔ Russia/Central Asia). The collapse of normal Belarus–EU road transport has dramatically reduced TIR movements through Belarus.

## Permit Application (Pre-Sanction / Third-Country Operators)

For operators from countries not subject to the above sanctions (e.g. operators from certain CIS countries):
- Foreign operators applied through their national competent authority for bilateral permits exchanged with Belarus
- Belarus Ministry of Transport allocated outbound permits to Belarusian carriers
- The **BelTransInspekciya** (Belarusian Transport Inspection) handled enforcement and compliance

## Penalties

Penalties under Belarusian road transport law. Note: BYR (Belarusian ruble) is the currency (ISO: BYN since 2016 currency reform).

| Violation | Penalty (BYN) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required permit | 700–2,000 BYN | Vehicle detention possible |
| Permit conditions exceeded | 350–1,400 BYN | |
| Tachograph violation | 700–2,000 BYN | |
| Overloaded vehicle | Variable per tonne excess | |

*Note: Enforcement by Belarusian authorities against EU operators is essentially moot given sanctions; however, EU penalties for sanctions violations are severe (criminal prosecution, fines, loss of operator licence).*

## EU Sanctions Violations — Consequences for EU/UK Operators

Violations of EU Council Regulation 2022/576 by EU-based operators:

| Consequence | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| Criminal prosecution | Under national implementing law; penalties vary by member state |
| Administrative fines | Substantial fines at member state level |
| Loss of operator licence / Community Licence | |
| Confiscation of vehicle/goods | In egregious cases |

EU member states are required to implement effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions for violations of EU restrictive measures.
