## Overview

Morocco is an important destination for European road freight, particularly from Spain and France via the ferry connections across the Strait of Gibraltar. Morocco is not an EU member state. International road freight to Morocco requires a **bilateral road transport permit** or equivalent authorisation. Morocco has concluded bilateral road transport agreements with EU member states and other countries.

Morocco is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention**, enabling TIR transit and facilitating customs clearance for international freight. Morocco is also an associated member of the International Transport Forum (ITF), with partial CEMT/ITF permit arrangements.

The primary entry points for road freight from Europe:
- **Tanger Med port** — main RoRo ferry terminal (from Algeciras, Spain)
- **Nador** — alternative port (from Almería, Spain)
- **Ceuta/Melilla** — Spanish enclaves with land border crossings into Morocco

---

## EU–Morocco Association Agreement (Transport Provisions)

Morocco and the EU have concluded an **Association Agreement (in force since 2000)** and subsequent agreements. The **Euro-Mediterranean Aviation Agreement** and trade facilitation measures address transport, but **road transport market access** is governed primarily through bilateral member state agreements rather than a single EU-level road transport treaty.

Key points:
- No EU-level market access agreement for road haulage (unlike EU–Norway or EU–Ukraine arrangements)
- Each EU member state maintains its own bilateral road transport agreement with Morocco
- Spain and France have the most significant agreements due to geographic proximity and trade volumes

## Bilateral Road Transport Permits

### Permit Structure

| Permit Type | Description | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Single-journey bilateral permit | For one complete journey (outward + return) | Standard permit for most EU operators |
| Multiple-journey permit (15 return trips) | Valid for up to 15 return trips during validity period | Available under some bilateral agreements (e.g. UK-Morocco) |
| Empty entry permit | For unladen vehicles entering Morocco | Separate permit under some agreements |
| Special (Hors contingent) permits | For film/exhibition equipment or exceptional loads | Available under some bilateral frameworks |

### UK-Morocco Bilateral Permits

Under the UK-Morocco bilateral agreement (maintained post-Brexit):
- Single-journey permit: £8 (apply via DVSA)
- Multiple-journey permit (15 return trips): £50
- Empty entry permit: £8
- Film/exhibition equipment permit: £8
- Own account journeys (transport of own goods): permit required
- Return rule: special entry permits must be returned within 15 days of expiry

### Permit Allocation

- Permits are allocated annually under quota systems negotiated between Morocco and each partner country
- EU operators apply through their national competent authority
- Quota allocations are discussed at Joint Commission meetings (typically annually)
- Spain and France typically have larger quota allocations due to high traffic volumes

## TIR Convention

Morocco is a **contracting party to the TIR Convention** (customs convention on international transport of goods under TIR Carnets).

- TIR Carnets are the primary customs transit document for road freight into Morocco
- National guaranteeing association in Morocco: **FENIROUTES** (Fédération Nationale des Transporteurs Routiers) as IRU-affiliated association
- TIR is widely used for Morocco-bound freight from Spain, France, and other European countries
- TIR Carnets are particularly important for goods continuing beyond Morocco into sub-Saharan Africa via Moroccan territory (though full TIR transit to sub-Saharan countries requires all countries to be TIR contracting parties)

---

## CEMT/ITF Permits

Morocco has an **associated status** with the International Transport Forum (ITF). The position on ITF/CEMT multilateral permit validity for Morocco is:
- Morocco is not a full ITF member; CEMT multilateral permits are **not universally valid** for operations in Morocco
- Verify current ITF permit acceptance with your national allocation body before relying on CEMT permits for Moroccan operations
- Bilateral permits or TIR-based arrangements are the primary mechanism

The main route from Europe to Morocco involves a sea crossing. Documentary requirements for RoRo ferry operations:

### Nador Port (from Almería, Spain)

- Alternative to Tanger Med, primarily for vehicles from Eastern Spain
- Similar documentary requirements
- Note: Nador–Melilla land crossing also operational

### Ceuta–Fnideq Land Crossing

- Spanish enclave of Ceuta has a land border with Morocco at Fnideq (formerly Castillejos)
- Used by some road freight but subject to periodic closures and restrictions
- Full Moroccan customs procedures apply

## Moroccan Customs Procedures

- **Tanger Med** is a major logistics hub with a free zone; substantial customs processing infrastructure
- Goods entering Morocco are subject to Moroccan customs declaration (D.U.M. — Déclaration Unique de Marchandises)
- A **customs agent/freight forwarder in Morocco** is typically required for import clearance
- ATA Carnets may be used for temporary import of certain goods (equipment, exhibition goods)
- Temporary admission (admission temporaire) for vehicles and equipment used in Morocco

## Penalties

Penalties under Moroccan road transport legislation (Code de la Route and related transport laws). Amounts in MAD (Moroccan dirham).

| Violation | Penalty (MAD) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Operating without required bilateral permit | 5,000–20,000 MAD | Vehicle may be immobilised |
| Permit expired or conditions exceeded | 3,000–10,000 MAD | |
| Overloaded vehicle | 2,000–15,000 MAD + per-tonne excess charge | |
| AETR tachograph violation | 2,000–8,000 MAD | |
| Driving without required documentation | 1,000–5,000 MAD | |
| Cabotage violation (domestic load by foreign carrier) | 10,000–30,000 MAD + vehicle detention | |

*Note: MAD amounts are indicative based on current regulatory practice; verify current rates with the Moroccan Ministry of Transport.*
