## Overview

Sweden applies EU Regulation 561/2006 with no significant deviations from the core driving/rest rules. The main Sweden-specific elements are: the national exemption scheme (agriculture, island operations), the warning system introduced 1 January 2026, and the SEK-denominated penalty schedule. For domestic light vehicle (≤3.5t) rules, see `se/light-vehicles.md`.

---

## Deviations from EU Baseline

No deviations from EU 561/2006 core rules (driving times, rest times, breaks). Sweden fully applies the EU standard.

---

## National Exemptions

Swedish national exemptions from EU 561/2006 (beyond the EU Article 3 exemptions) — see `se/exceptions.md` for full detail:
- **Agriculture/forestry**: within 100km radius from home base
- **Island operations**: certain island-specific routes
- Light vehicles ≤3.5t in domestic transport: separate rules apply (see `se/light-vehicles.md`)

**Lookback at roadside:** 56 days (changed 31 December 2024; previously 28 days)
- Driver must produce: driver card, tachograph printouts/attestation covering last 56 days

**AETR operations:** No advance payment (förskottsbelopp) permitted at roadside for AETR violations.

### Tachograph Printout (Remsa) — Swedish Context

The Swedish term **remsa** (literally "strip/tape") refers to the paper printout from the tachograph printer. The obligation to produce and annotate a remsa follows EU Reg. 165/2014 Art. 35 — see `references/eu/smart-tachograph.md` § "Printouts" for the full rules.

**Summary for Swedish drivers:**
- A remsa must be produced at the **start and end of each working day** whenever the **förarkort (driver card) is missing, damaged, malfunctioning, or not yet received**
- The driver must **handwrite on the remsa**: full name, förarkort number (or driving licence number if unknown), date, reason, and signature
- At a **vägkontroll (roadside check)**: Polismyndigheten will ask for the remsa for the current day and preceding 28 days
- Carry requirement: current day + 28 preceding days — see `eu/smart-tachograph.md` for full carry rules
- Employer must retain printouts for **at least 12 months** after receipt

**Förarkort lost or stolen:** Report immediately to Transportstyrelsen and apply for replacement. Produce start/end remsas daily until the replacement card arrives.

### Tachograph Manipulation — Swedish Specifics

Tachograph manipulation under Swedish law (SFS 2004:865 + EU Reg. 165/2014):
- Installing or using a manipulation device: criminal prosecution (brottsbalken)
- Driving with broken or bypassed tachograph: **körförbud** (prohibition from driving) + sanktionsavgift
- Using another person's driver card: criminal offence; driver card suspended by Transportstyrelsen

Driver cards issued and revoked by **Transportstyrelsen**. Lost/stolen: report to Transportstyrelsen, apply for replacement; carry written declaration in vehicle until replacement arrives.

### What to do if you accidentally exceed limits (Sweden)

If a driver realises they have exceeded driving time limits:
1. **Stop and take the required rest** — this is the immediate priority; continuing to drive compounds the violation
2. **Record on the tachograph** — use manual entry or written note explaining the exceptional circumstance (Article 12 exception applies for genuine emergencies only)
3. **At roadside check**: the officer will assess the severity class (MI/SI/VSI/MSI — see `eu/enforcement.md`). For minor first-time overdrives, the Swedish warning system (varningssystem, from 1 January 2026) may apply — Transportstyrelsen may issue a warning instead of a sanktionsavgift
4. **Police may collect advance payment (förskottsbelopp)** on the spot for serious violations; Transportstyrelsen later determines the final sanction
5. **Company obligation**: transport companies must review tachograph data and self-correct scheduling; systematic violations lead to **företagskontroll** (company control) by Transportstyrelsen

For penalty amounts in SEK, see `references/countries/se/penalties.md`.

## Warning System (from 1 January 2026)

A new Swedish warning system (varningssystem) applies for minor first-time violations:
- Transportstyrelsen decides whether to issue a warning instead of a sanction fee
- Police may still collect advance payment at roadside; Transportstyrelsen refunds if a warning is later issued instead
- Warnings NOT available if: previous violations exist within the lookback period, systematic breaches occur, or safety risks are present
- Applies to transport companies (not individual drivers)

For the full Swedish penalty schedule in SEK, see `se/penalties.md`.

Summary of driving/rest time violations:
- Documentation failure (3 instances): 20 000 / 40 000 / 60 000 SEK per vehicle (max 800 000 SEK cap)
- Individual driver violations: fines issued by police at roadside
