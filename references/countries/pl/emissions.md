## Overview

Poland applies EU emission standards as direct EU law. The Ministry of Infrastructure is responsible for transport emission policy. Poland is in the process of implementing Clean Transport Zones (Strefy Czystego Transportu — SCT) in major cities. Warsaw and Kraków have introduced or are planning SCT, though comprehensive HGV LEZ coverage is less advanced than in Western EU countries.

---

## Euro VII Application

No Polish national deviations from EU Euro VII timeline:
- New type approvals: **1 July 2026**
- All new registrations: **1 July 2027**

CO2 manufacturer targets apply as EU law.

---

## National Clean Transport Zones (Strefy Czystego Transportu)

Poland's Act on Electromobility and Alternative Fuels (ustawa o elektromobilności) allows municipalities to establish SCT (Clean Transport Zones):

**Warsaw**: SCT implementation progressing; inner city access restrictions for older diesel vehicles being phased in.

**Kraków**: SCT/clean air measures; Kraków is also one of Europe's most polluted cities — restrictions on coal heating have driven cleaner transport policies.

Other cities planning SCT: Wrocław, Poznań, Gdańsk.

---

## National ZEV Incentives

- **NFOŚIGW** (National Fund for Environmental Protection and Water Management): grants for ZEV commercial vehicles and charging infrastructure
- **e-Bus/e-Truck programmes**: EU RRF-funded subsidies
- Reduced vehicle registration costs for ZEV

---

## Deviations from EU Baseline

No deviations from EU emission standards. Poland adds developing SCT network.
