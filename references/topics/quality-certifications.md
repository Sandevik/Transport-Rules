## Overview

Certifications serve several purposes for road transport operators: regulatory compliance (HACCP, GDP), customer requirements (SQAS for chemical shippers, TAPA for high-value cargo), public procurement (FORS for UK construction), and operational excellence signals (ISO 9001, ISO 39001). They differ in scope, cost, renewal cycle, and the type of auditing involved.

This document provides a reference overview of the principal certifications relevant to European road transport operators.

## ISO 9001 — Quality Management System

### What It Is
ISO 9001 is the world's most widely adopted quality management standard. It provides a framework for consistently meeting customer requirements and improving processes over time. Certification is granted by an accredited **certification body (CB)** following an audit.

### What Auditors Check
- Document control and record management
- Customer focus and satisfaction measurement
- Risk-based thinking and process management
- Corrective and preventive action systems
- Management review processes
- Supplier evaluation and monitoring
- Internal audit programme
- Continual improvement evidence

### For Transport Operators: Key Focus Areas
- Order handling and dispatch processes
- Subcontractor/supplier management
- Driver and vehicle resource planning
- Customer complaint handling
- Claims and incident management

### Certification Structure
| Phase | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Initial certification | Stage 1 (document review) + Stage 2 (full audit) |
| Surveillance audits | Annually in years 1 and 2 of the 3-year cycle |
| Recertification audit | Every 3 years |

### Typical Cost
- **Certification body fee:** €2,000–€6,000/year (depending on company size and number of sites)
- **Consultancy/preparation:** €3,000–€10,000 for initial certification (optional but common)
- **Internal resource:** Significant — typically a quality manager or quality management function

## ISO 14001 — Environmental Management System

### What It Is
ISO 14001 provides a framework for managing an organisation's environmental impacts. For transport operators, this typically covers fuel consumption, emissions, waste management, and environmental risk management.

### What Auditors Check
- Environmental aspects register (identifying significant impacts — e.g., diesel fuel use, refrigerant leaks)
- Environmental objectives and targets (e.g., fuel efficiency improvement, emissions reduction)
- Legal compliance register (EU Emissions Trading System, national environmental permits)
- Emergency preparedness for environmental incidents (fuel spill response)
- Energy monitoring and reporting
- Continual improvement evidence

### For Transport Operators: Key Focus Areas
- Fleet fuel monitoring and emissions tracking
- Alternative fuel/EV transition planning
- Refrigerant management (HFC/HFO compliance)
- Tyre and waste oil management
- Driver eco-driving training programmes

### Certification Structure and Cost
Same 3-year cycle with annual surveillance as ISO 9001.
- **Certification body fee:** €2,000–€5,000/year
- Often certified alongside ISO 9001 and ISO 45001 as an **Integrated Management System (IMS)** — reduces overall audit cost

## ISO 45001 — Occupational Health & Safety Management

### What It Is
ISO 45001 (published 2018, replacing OHSAS 18001) is the international standard for occupational health and safety management systems. It addresses the management of OH&S risks in the workplace.

For transport operators, workplace hazards include: driver fatigue, manual handling (loading/unloading), vehicle-related injuries, chemical exposure (ADR), working at height (curtainsider straps), and depot/yard safety.

### What Auditors Check
- Hazard identification and risk assessment (for all workplace activities)
- Incident investigation and root cause analysis
- Health and safety objectives and improvement programmes
- Worker participation and consultation mechanisms
- Management of contractors and visitors
- Legal compliance (national H&S law)
- Emergency preparedness
- Driver fatigue risk management

### Certification Structure and Cost
Same 3-year cycle.
- **Certification body fee:** €2,000–€5,000/year

## ISO 39001 — Road Traffic Safety Management System

### What It Is
ISO 39001 is specifically designed for organisations that interact with the road traffic system — including transport operators, logistics companies, and fleet operators. It focuses on reducing road traffic fatalities and serious injuries attributable to the organisation.

### What Auditors Check
- Road traffic safety (RTS) policy
- Fleet risk assessment and incident data analysis
- Speed management (including speed limiter compliance)
- Driver selection, training, and assessment
- Fatigue management
- Vehicle safety features (ADAS, autonomous emergency braking, lane departure warning)
- Journey planning and route risk assessment
- Alcohol and drugs policy

### Business Case
Transport operators with ISO 39001 typically demonstrate:
- Lower accident rates → lower insurance premiums
- Better driver recruitment and retention (safety culture signal)
- Competitive advantage in tenders requiring road safety commitments

### Certification Structure and Cost
- **Certification body fee:** €2,000–€5,000/year
- **Risk reduction value:** Insurance premium reductions of 5–15% reported by certified operators

## SQAS — Safety & Quality Assessment System

### What It Is
SQAS is a European assessment system developed and managed by **CEFIC** (the European Chemical Industry Council) for evaluating the safety and quality of logistics service providers supplying to the chemical industry. It is not a "certification" per se — there is no pass/fail. Instead, it produces a standardised report that chemical company customers use to assess suitability.

### Who Requires It
Major chemical manufacturers require SQAS assessment before approving a transport operator for chemical transport contracts:
- BASF, INEOS, Dow Chemical, Evonik, Covestro, LyondellBasell, Shell Chemicals, and most large European chemical producers
- Operators transporting ADR-classified chemicals by road (tank or packaged) for these customers will almost certainly be asked for a current SQAS report

### What the Assessor Checks
SQAS assessments are conducted by trained independent assessors. The questionnaire covers:

| Area | Key items |
|------|-----------|
| Management | Quality management system; management review; corrective actions |
| Health, Safety, Environment | Incident reporting; near-miss reporting; HSE policy |
| Transport safety | ADR compliance; vehicle standards; driver training (ADR Chapters 8.2) |
| Security | Anti-theft measures; cargo security; access control at depot |
| Product quality | Contamination prevention; tank cleaning; temperature control |
| Training | Driver training records; competency assessment |
| Subcontractor management | Verification that subcontractors also meet requirements |

### Cost
- **SQAS assessment fee:** €3,000–€5,000 per assessment
- **Validity:** Reports are valid for 3 years; annual update questionnaire required in intervening years
- **Database access:** Customers access SQAS reports via the CEFIC-administered SQAS database (operator must grant access to each customer)

## TAPA TSR — Trucking Security Requirements

### What It Is
TAPA (Transported Asset Protection Association) TSR is a security standard specifically for road transport of high-value, theft-attractive cargo. It defines minimum security requirements for trucking operations including vehicle security hardware, driver procedures, and parking security.

### Who Requires It
- Electronics manufacturers and distributors (consumer electronics, semiconductors, mobile phones)
- Pharmaceutical companies transporting high-value medicines
- Luxury goods manufacturers
- Tobacco companies

### TSR Levels
TAPA TSR is tiered:
| Level | Security Requirements |
|-------|----------------------|
| TSR A | Highest — GPS tracking, driver monitoring, secure parking at TAPA-approved facilities, armed escort option |
| TSR B | Medium — GPS tracking, driver check-in protocols, approved parking |
| TSR C | Entry-level — basic GPS, procedural requirements |

### What Auditors Check
- GPS/telematics equipment functionality and coverage
- Driver security procedures (communication protocols, route secrecy)
- Vehicle security hardware (additional locks, alarm systems)
- Parking security (TAPA-approved parking locations where applicable)
- Incident response procedures
- Subcontractor security vetting

### Cost
- **Audit fee:** €2,000–€4,000 per certification/renewal
- **Hardware investment:** Telematics, additional locking, alarm systems can add €1,000–€5,000 per vehicle
- **Validity:** 1-year certification with annual re-audit

## GDP — Good Distribution Practice (Pharmaceutical Logistics)

### What It Is
**Good Distribution Practice (GDP)** guidelines establish quality standards for the storage and distribution of medicinal products (prescription medicines, vaccines, clinical trial materials). Compliance with GDP is a **regulatory requirement** for companies involved in the pharmaceutical supply chain in the EU.

**EU GDP Guidelines:** Revised in 2013 and updated; issued under the auspices of the European Medicines Agency (EMA) and EU Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) framework.

### Who Must Comply
- **Wholesale distributors** of medicines (licensed under EU Directive 2001/83/EC)
- **Transport operators** carrying temperature-sensitive pharmaceuticals are expected to operate in compliance with GDP — customers (pharmaceutical companies) routinely require GDP-compliant transport partners
- **Technically:** Transport companies do not hold a GDP "licence" (that applies to wholesale distributors) — but they are expected to implement GDP principles and may be audited by pharmaceutical customers

### What Auditors Check
- Temperature-controlled vehicle fleet (validated temperature mapping of refrigerated vehicles)
- Temperature monitoring systems (continuous data loggers, alarm systems)
- Qualification and validation of temperature-controlled equipment
- Written procedures for all critical operations (loading, unloading, excursion handling)
- Training records for all staff handling medicinal products
- Subcontractor qualification
- Security measures (tamper-evident packaging, access control)
- Deviation and complaint handling

### Cost
- **GDP qualification audit by pharma customer:** No direct cost to operator (customer bears cost)
- **Self-preparation and system implementation:** €10,000–€50,000+ depending on fleet size and systems
- **Temperature monitoring equipment:** €500–€2,000 per vehicle/trailer

## BRC Global Standard for Storage & Distribution

### What It Is
The **BRCGS (Brand Reputation Compliance Global Standards) Storage & Distribution** standard is widely required by major food retailers and grocery companies for their logistics service providers.

### Who Requires It
- Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Lidl, Aldi, REWE, Edeka, and other major grocery retailers
- Food manufacturers supplying to major retailers
- Any operator seeking to handle temperature-controlled food products for retail supply chains

### Grades
| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| AA | Highest — full compliance, minimal non-conformances |
| A | High compliance |
| B | Some non-conformances corrected |
| C / D | Non-conformances; may not satisfy customer requirements |

### What Auditors Check
- Food safety management system
- HACCP implementation for storage and distribution activities
- Site security and pest control
- Temperature control (ambient, chilled, frozen)
- Vehicle hygiene and maintenance
- Labelling and traceability
- Customer complaint handling

### Cost
- **Certification body audit fee:** €2,500–€6,000 per audit
- **Annual re-audit:** Required (BRC uses annual certification cycle)

## HACCP — Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points

### Legal Basis
Under **EU Regulation 852/2004** on the hygiene of foodstuffs, **all food business operators** — including those involved in the storage and distribution of food — must implement **HACCP-based procedures**. This is a legal requirement, not optional.

### For Transport Operators: Scope
Food business operator status applies to transport companies when:
- Carrying food products (even packaged goods in sealed containers — certain risks apply)
- Operating temperature-controlled vehicles for food distribution
- Operating food depots or cross-docking facilities handling food

### What HACCP Implementation Requires
1. **Hazard Analysis** — identify all biological, chemical, and physical hazards associated with each step of the transport/storage process
2. **Critical Control Points (CCPs)** — identify the points in the process where controls are essential to prevent hazards (e.g., temperature at loading, temperature during transit, hygiene at unloading)
3. **Critical Limits** — define the maximum/minimum values (e.g., temperature must not exceed +5°C for chilled food)
4. **Monitoring procedures** — how CCPs are monitored and by whom
5. **Corrective actions** — what to do if a CCP is not under control (temperature excursion during transit)
6. **Verification procedures** — confirming the HACCP system is working
7. **Records** — full documentation of the above

HACCP implementation is not separately certified in most cases — it is a prerequisite for BRC, ISO 22000, or similar certifications. A standalone HACCP plan is legally required and audited by food safety authorities (in SE: Livsmedelsverket).

## Eco-Lighthouse (Miljøfyrtårn)

### What It Is
Eco-Lighthouse is a Norwegian environmental certification scheme managed by **Stiftelsen Miljøfyrtårn**. It is particularly relevant for Norwegian operations but increasingly recognised in Nordic sustainability procurement.

### Who Requires It
- Norwegian public procurement (many municipalities and public bodies require Eco-Lighthouse or equivalent)
- Customers with Nordic sustainability commitments
- Transport operators tendering for public transport contracts in Norway

### What Auditors Check
- Energy consumption and CO2 emissions (fleet and premises)
- Waste management and recycling
- Purchasing policies (eco-friendly products)
- Working environment and HR practices
- Transport-specific criteria (fuel efficiency, alternative fuels)

### Cost
- **Certification fee:** NOK 2,900–15,000 depending on company size
- **Annual renewal:** Required; annual re-certification against updated criteria
- **Validity:** Formally certified for 3 years with annual check-in

## FORS — Fleet Operator Recognition Scheme

### What It Is
FORS is a UK-based accreditation scheme for fleet operators. It was originally developed to improve safety, fuel efficiency, and environmental performance of fleets operating in London.

### Who Requires It
- Operators working on the London Construction Programme
- Contractors working on Crossrail, HS2, and other major UK infrastructure projects
- Operators supplying to London public authorities
- Companies responding to UK public sector transport tenders

### Levels
| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Bronze | Entry-level compliance with minimum FORS standards |
| Silver | Enhanced standards (fuel monitoring, driver eyesight testing, cycle safety) |
| Gold | Highest — comprehensive safety, fuel, environment, and management standards |

### What Auditors Check (Bronze Level)
- Operator licence compliance
- Vehicle safety checks (daily walkaround records)
- Driver licence checking and CPC compliance
- Tachograph compliance
- Incident reporting and management
- Fuel consumption monitoring
- Noise, emissions, and environmental compliance

### Cost
- **Annual membership fee:** £600–£1,500 (Bronze); higher for Silver/Gold
- **Audit fee:** Included in membership for initial assessment; additional for Silver/Gold
- **Non-UK operators:** FORS has limited recognition outside the UK — primarily a UK-market requirement

## ISO 28000 — Supply Chain Security Management System

### What It Is
ISO 28000 provides a framework for supply chain security management. It is designed for organisations operating critical supply chains where security threats (theft, terrorism, smuggling, tampering) are a significant risk.

### Who Requires It
- High-security logistics (pharmaceuticals, electronics, luxury goods)
- Port operators and freight forwarders in sensitive supply chains
- Defence sector logistics
- Operators handling cargo subject to C-TPAT (US Customs Trade Partnership Against Terrorism) or AEO (Authorised Economic Operator) requirements

### What Auditors Check
- Security risk assessment and threat analysis
- Physical security of premises and vehicles
- Personnel security (background checks, access control)
- IT and data security for logistics systems
- Incident response planning
- Business continuity planning
- Supply chain partner security vetting

### Certification Structure and Cost
Same 3-year ISO cycle.
- **Certification body fee:** €4,000–€10,000/year (higher than ISO 9001 due to specialist auditor requirement)

## Choosing the Right Certifications

| Operator type | Recommended certifications |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| General haulage, domestic/EU | ISO 9001 (competitive advantage); ISO 45001 (H&S duty of care) |
| International haulage | ISO 9001 + consider ISO 14001 (customer ESG requirements growing) |
| Chemical transport (ADR) | SQAS (essential for chemical shipper customers) + ISO 9001 |
| Pharmaceutical / cold-chain | GDP compliance + BRC S&D or ISO 15378 + ISO 9001 |
| Food distribution | HACCP (legal requirement) + BRC S&D (retailer requirement) |
| High-value cargo (electronics, pharma) | TAPA TSR (A or B level) + possibly ISO 28000 |
| Norwegian operations | Eco-Lighthouse + ISO 9001 |
| UK fleet (London / infrastructure) | FORS Bronze minimum; Silver/Gold for major contracts |
| Fleet operators (safety focus) | ISO 39001 (road safety) |

## Penalties

Certifications are generally voluntary (except HACCP) — penalties arise from customer contract requirements and regulatory obligations:

| Issue | Consequence |
|-------|------------|
| HACCP not implemented (food operator) | EU Regulation 852/2004 violation; national food authority prosecution; in SE: Livsmedelsverket fine and/or prohibition on handling food |
| GDP non-compliance (pharmaceutical transport) | Pharmaceutical customer audit failure; loss of contract; potential regulatory action by national medicines authority |
| Loss of SQAS accreditation | Loss of contracts with chemical shipper customers requiring current SQAS report |
| TAPA TSR lapse | Customer contract termination; insurance implications if security breach occurs |
| BRC S&D certification lapse | Major retailer contract suspension until re-certification |
| ISO 9001 lapse | Loss of competitive advantage in tenders; potential contract breach where ISO 9001 was a contractual requirement |
