## Overview

This file provides the **legal speed limits** for heavy goods vehicles (HGVs, lorries/trucks) by road type across all major countries covered by this knowledge base. These are the statutory maximum speeds — they are distinct from, and often lower than, the speed limiter setting required by EU law (90 km/h for vehicles > 3.5 t under Directive 92/6/EEC).

> **Important:** EU law (Directive 92/6/EEC) requires speed limiters set to **90 km/h** on vehicles > 12 t, and from 2005 also on vehicles 3.5–12 t. However, the **legal speed limit** for HGVs is typically **80 km/h** on motorways — meaning a truck may be mechanically capable of 90 km/h but legally restricted to 80 km/h. Exceeding 80 km/h is a legal violation even if the speed limiter does not prevent it.

## Special Circumstances and Reductions

### Bad Weather / Winter Speed Reductions

| Country | Reduction rule |
|---|---|
| **SE** Sweden | Posted variable message signs (VMS) on E-roads override standard limits; winter: many roads reduced to 70 km/h by signage |
| **NO** Norway | Posted variable limits; winter conditions on E6/E18: often 60–70 km/h for trucks |
| **FI** Finland | Winter road condition signs override statutory limits; frequently 60–70 km/h for trucks in icy conditions |
| **AT** Austria | Alpine routes: winter tyre requirements; reduced speed limits posted at tunnels and mountain passes |
| **DE** Germany | Dynamic speed signs (variable limit signs) on motorways; no statutory winter truck reduction, but enforcement of braking distance rules |

### Towing / Combination Vehicle Reductions

Some countries apply lower limits for vehicle combinations (tractor + trailer) vs. rigid trucks:

| Country | Articulated / road train limit | Rigid truck limit |
|---|---|---|
| **DE** Germany | Same 80 km/h motorway | Same 80 km/h |
| **ES** Spain | **80 km/h** motorway (articulated) | **90 km/h** motorway (rigid > 3.5 t) |
| **FR** France | Same 90 km/h motorway | Same 90 km/h |
| **IT** Italy | Same 80 km/h motorway | Same 80 km/h |
| **GB** United Kingdom | Same limits apply to combinations | Same limits by GVW |

### Oversized / Exceptional Loads

Vehicles carrying exceptional loads (over legal dimensions or weights — see references/eu/oversize-loads.md) are subject to **individually set movement orders** that specify maximum speed. Typical movement order speeds:

| Load type | Typical speed limit in movement order |
|---|---|
| Wide load (> 3.0 m) on motorway | 50–70 km/h (country-specific) |
| Extra-long load (escorted) | 50 km/h maximum in many jurisdictions |
| Night-only transport (e.g., DE: 21:00–06:00) | As per movement order |
| Multi-axle heavy load (> 80 t) | 30–40 km/h in some jurisdictions |

Movement orders are issued by national authorities (in DE: Straßenverkehrsbehörde; in SE: Transportstyrelsen). Speed requirements are specified in the permit document itself.

### Speed Limiter vs. Legal Limit

| Type | Setting | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Speed limiter (mandatory) | **90 km/h** for N2/N3 vehicles (> 3.5 t) | Directive 92/6/EEC |
| Motorway legal limit (most EU states) | **80 km/h** | National law |
| Practical consequence | Driver may do 90 km/h on motorway without vehicle preventing it — but is committing a legal violation above 80 km/h in most states | — |

## Penalties for HGV Speeding

Penalties are set by national law. Typical ranges for trucks:

| Country | 1–10 km/h over | 11–20 km/h over | 21–30 km/h over | > 30 km/h over |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DE** | €30 OWi | €100–€160; 1 Punkt | €160–€240; 1 Punkt; 1-month ban | €200–€680; 2 Punkte; licence suspension |
| **FR** | €68–€135 | €135 | €135 + possible suspension | €1,500; licence suspension |
| **SE** | Variable fine (böter) | Dagböter scale | Dagböter + possible licence suspension | Criminal speeding (grovt fortkörning) |
| **GB** | Fixed penalty £100; 3 points | Up to £1,000; 3–6 points | Up to £2,500; possible disqualification | Unlimited fine; disqualification |
| **PL** | PLN 50 | PLN 100–200 | PLN 200–400 | PLN 500–2,500; licence suspension |

Note: **Professional drivers** often face higher fines than private car drivers in countries with income-scaled fine systems (NO, FI, CH, SE).
