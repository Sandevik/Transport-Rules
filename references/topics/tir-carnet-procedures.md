## Overview

The **TIR Convention** (Transport International Routier) is a UNECE multilateral customs agreement that allows road freight to transit multiple countries under a **single customs guarantee**, avoiding opening of sealed loads at each border. It replaces national transit documents for journeys involving one or more non-EU countries that are TIR contracting parties.

The TIR system rests on four pillars:
1. **Secure vehicles/containers** — approved and physically sealed
2. **International guarantee chain** — IRU and national associations cover customs duties up to €100,000 per carnet if duties are evaded
3. **TIR Carnet** — the international customs document accompanying the vehicle
4. **Mutual recognition** — contracting parties accept TIR as sufficient transit authority

As of 2026, **76 contracting parties** have acceded to the TIR Convention, covering all EU/EEA states plus Turkey, Ukraine, Morocco, Georgia, Belarus, Russia, Central Asian states, and others.

## Legal Framework

| Instrument | Content |
|---|---|
| TIR Convention 1975 | Core treaty; defines carnet structure, procedures, vehicle approval, guarantee chain |
| TIR Administrative Committee | UNECE body that administers the convention and authorises guaranteeing associations |
| IRU (International Road Transport Union) | Authorised by TIR Administrative Committee to manage the TIR system; issues carnets through affiliated national associations |
| EU UCC Regulation 952/2013 | Within the EU, TIR carnets are processed through NCTS; TIR remains the guarantee document |

## Vehicle Requirements for TIR Approval

To carry goods under TIR, the vehicle or container must be **approved** by a competent authority:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **TIR approval plate** | Metal plate marked "TIR" affixed at front and rear of vehicle while operating under TIR |
| **Sealed loading compartment** | No openings accessible from outside when doors are locked and customs seals applied; no concealed spaces; no gaps between floor, walls, and roof exceeding 1mm |
| **Approved construction** | Walls, floor, and roof of materials that cannot be penetrated without leaving visible traces; hinges and locking devices of approved type |
| **Annual inspection** | Vehicle must be inspected annually by competent authority (customs or delegated body); inspection certificate must be carried |

Container approvals follow ISO/TC 104 standards and are managed separately (BIC — Bureau International des Conteneurs).

## TIR Carnet Structure

### Physical Format

The carnet is a booklet consisting of:

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Cover page (front)** | Vehicle details, operator, guaranteeing association, validity dates |
| **Sets of volets** | Each set = two pages (volet 1 + counterfoil 1 for departure country; volet 2 + counterfoil 2 for destination country); one set used per country traversed |
| **Colour coding** | Outward journey: yellow pages; return journey: white pages (for vehicles returning with goods) |
| **Manifest pages** | Detailed goods description, package count, marks, weight; completed by operator before departure |

### Number of Volet Sets

A standard TIR Carnet has **14 volets** (7 journeys). A heavy-duty carnet has up to **20 volets**. For journeys through many countries, operators must calculate sets needed in advance (2 per transit country + 2 for destination).

## Carnet Issuance

### Who Issues TIR Carnets

Carnets are issued by **IRU-affiliated national guaranteeing associations**. The driver's employer must be a member of, or hold a contract with, the relevant association:

| Country | Guaranteeing Association |
|---|---|
| TR (Turkey) | TOBB (Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Turkey) |
| FR (France) | FNTR (Fédération Nationale des Transporteurs Routiers) |
| DE (Germany) | BGL (Bundesverband Güterkraftverkehr Logistik und Entsorgung) |
| IT (Italy) | Confartigianato Trasporti / FIATA members |
| PL (Poland) | ZMPD (Association of International Road Carriers) |
| NL (Netherlands) | NLT (Koninklijk Nederlands Transport) |
| SE (Sweden) | Transportföretagen / via Nordic associations |
| UA (Ukraine) | ASMAP-Ukraine |
| TR | TOBB |
| BY (Belarus) | BAMAP |
| GE (Georgia) | GRAA |

### Cost and Procurement

- Standard carnet: **€30–60** per booklet (varies by country and association)
- Frequent users (trucking companies) typically maintain a stock of pre-purchased carnets
- Carnet validity: issued for a specific journey; validity dates stated on cover page
- Digital TIR carnets (eTIR): being piloted under UNECE eTIR project; not yet universally accepted

## Border Crossing Procedure (Step-by-Step)

### Departure Country (e.g., DE)

1. Driver presents TIR Carnet to customs officer at departure customs office
2. Customs officer verifies:
   - Goods manifest (matches physical load)
   - Vehicle approval status
   - Operator's TIR authorisation
3. Customs applies **customs seals** to vehicle doors/container
4. Customs completes **Volet 1** (departure voucher): notes seal numbers, vehicle reg, goods details
5. **Counterfoil 1** is detached and returned to driver — driver retains this throughout journey
6. Within EU: NCTS electronic notification is also sent; TIR carnet travels with vehicle as guarantee

### At Each Transit Country Border

7. Driver presents carnet at border control
8. Entering customs: checks seals intact; notes details; retains the **volet** for that country
9. Driver retains corresponding **counterfoil**
10. If seals are broken or goods missing: customs may open vehicle, detain carnet, initiate investigation — guaranteeing association is notified

### At Destination Country Customs Office

11. Driver presents carnet and remaining volets to customs at final destination
12. Customs verifies goods against manifest; opens vehicle (breaking seals)
13. Final **volet** retained by customs; counterfoil returned to driver
14. Customs issues **customs discharge** — this is critical proof the carnet was properly completed

### After Journey

15. Driver returns unused volets + all counterfoils to **issuing guaranteeing association** within the timeframe stated on the carnet cover (typically 2–3 months)
16. Association verifies all countries have discharged; carnet closed
17. If any country has NOT discharged within the period: that country's customs authority may issue a **claim** against the guaranteeing chain

## NCTS Integration (EU Internal Processing)

Within the European Union, TIR carnets are processed electronically through **NCTS (New Computerised Transit System)**:

- TIR carnets are registered in NCTS at the office of departure
- NCTS sends electronic pre-arrival notifications to transit and destination offices
- Physical volets are still required as the guarantee document, but paper stamping at EU internal border crossings has been largely eliminated
- At the destination, NCTS issues electronic discharge; association is notified via SafeTIR
- For **entry from non-EU countries** (e.g., TR-BG border), NCTS processes the EU leg from the EU border office of entry

---

## SafeTIR — Electronic Tracking

**SafeTIR** is IRU's real-time electronic system for tracking TIR carnet status:

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Customs notifications | Departure and destination customs offices notify IRU via SafeTIR when carnet starts and ends |
| Fraud detection | Alerts issued if a carnet is presented at an unexpected location or duplicated |
| Guaranteeing association access | Real-time visibility of their issued carnets |
| Claim processing | Electronic claim workflow between customs authorities and associations |

SafeTIR significantly reduces TIR fraud (false customs discharges) which was a major problem in the 1990s–2000s.

## Lost or Damaged Carnet

If a TIR Carnet is lost, stolen, or damaged during transit:

1. Report **immediately** to:
   - Local customs authority
   - Issuing guaranteeing association
2. Local customs may issue a **substitute document** (replacement transit declaration under national procedure)
3. Association may arrange a **replacement carnet** in coordination with the relevant customs authority
4. In countries without an association representative: contact the nearest IRU correspondent or national customs headquarters
5. Police report recommended (for stolen carnets)

**Do not attempt to continue under TIR without valid carnet** — vehicle may be detained at next border.

## Common Mistakes and Compliance Issues

| Issue | Consequence | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Seals broken in transit** (not by customs) | Investigation; potential claim against guarantee; goods may be impounded | Never allow unauthorised access to sealed compartment; report any break-in immediately |
| **Wrong goods declared on manifest** | Customs fraud charge; carnet invalidated; claim for full customs duties | Verify manifest against actual load before departure; consignor must provide accurate data |
| **Incomplete customs notation** (volet not stamped at border) | Country treated as not discharged; claim issued | Ensure each border post stamps both volet and counterfoil; do not leave without stamp |
| **Carnet used after expiry date** | Invalid; goods treated as smuggled | Check expiry date on cover; re-issue carnet if journey delayed beyond validity |
| **Wrong number of volets** (too few for countries traversed) | Cannot complete TIR transit; must convert to national transit document | Count: 2 volets per transit country + 2 for destination; add spare set |
| **Vehicle not TIR-approved** (approval expired) | Cannot operate under TIR; full customs duty payable at each border | Schedule annual vehicle inspection well before expiry |

## Penalties

TIR violations are treated as customs offences under national law:

| Violation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Failure to carry TIR Carnet in required country | Vehicle detained; goods impounded; national transit duty payable at border |
| Goods discrepancy between manifest and actual | Customs fraud investigation; full duty + penalties; criminal charges possible |
| Guarantee claim triggered (duties evaded) | IRU/association recovers from operator; operator may be banned from TIR system |
| Operating with expired TIR approval on vehicle | Vehicle detained; conversion to national transit |
| Revocation of TIR access by IRU | Operator excluded from global TIR system; major commercial impact for international carriers |

**EU enforcement:** Serious TIR violations are recorded in NCTS and may trigger OLAF (European Anti-Fraud Office) investigation for cross-border duty evasion.
