## Overview

The **CEMT/ITF multilateral permit system** is a scheme allowing commercial road freight transport between member countries using a single multilateral permit, bypassing the need for bilateral transport agreements between every pair of countries. It was the principal mechanism for international road freight market access before the EU's Community Licence system replaced it for intra-EU transport.

Today, CEMT/ITF permits remain essential for:
- Transport between **EU states and non-EU ITF members** (TR, UA, RS, GE, MA, UK post-Brexit, etc.)
- Operations in countries where bilateral quotas are exhausted
- Third-country operators requiring access to multiple European states

## History and Governance

| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1953** | CEMT (Conférence Européenne des Ministres des Transports) founded; multilateral permit system created |
| **2006** | CEMT renamed to **ITF (International Transport Forum)**; relocated under OECD umbrella; headquarters Paris |
| **2011** | UK joins as full ITF member |
| **Post-Brexit (2021)** | UK-EU bilateral permits (separate ECMT system) became critical; UK no longer in EU Community Licence |
| **Annual** | ITF Secretariat publishes quota allocations for coming year; distributed to member authorities |

All 27 EU member states + Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein

**Non-EU European members** (CEMT/ITF permits critical):
- GB (United Kingdom), RS (Serbia), ME (Montenegro), MK (North Macedonia), BA (Bosnia-Herzegovina), AL (Albania), MD (Moldova), UA (Ukraine), BY (Belarus)*

## Permit Types

### 1. Annual Multilateral CEMT Permit

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Validity** | 1 January – 31 December (calendar year) |
| **Scope** | Unlimited journeys throughout all ITF member countries during the year |
| **Cost** | Approximately €45–60 per permit (varies by country of issue) |
| **Quota** | Each ITF member country receives an annual quota from the ITF Secretariat; highly limited |
| **Allocation** | National transport authority or association distributes to operators based on criteria (need, environmental class of vehicle, prior use) |
| **Best for** | High-frequency international operators; carriers with diverse routes across multiple non-EU countries |

### 2. Short-Term CEMT Permit (30-Day)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Validity** | 30 consecutive days; date must be specified |
| **Scope** | Same multilateral access as annual; for one month |
| **Cost** | Approximately €4–8 per permit |
| **Quota** | Separate monthly quota pool; easier to obtain than annual |
| **Best for** | Occasional international operators; seasonal operations; testing new routes |

### 3. Bilateral Permits

Separate bilateral agreements between specific pairs of countries supplement the CEMT system. These are country-to-country quotas negotiated annually through diplomatic channels:

- Example: SE-TR bilateral transport permit for direct Swedish-Turkish transport
- DE-UA, PL-UA, NL-TR permits cover high-volume corridors
- Bilateral permits may be **more permissive** (no environmental requirements) or may cover routes excluded from CEMT

**Operators should apply for bilateral permits first** where the route is well-defined; use CEMT for multi-country flexibility.

## Environmental Class Requirements

CEMT permits are **tiered by vehicle Euro emission class**. Lower emission standards result in restricted permit eligibility:

| Euro standard | CEMT eligibility | Weighting factor |
|---|---|---|
| Euro III | Some legacy permits; most countries no longer accept | 1× (lowest) |
| Euro IV | Eligible for basic CEMT permits | 1× |
| Euro V | **Minimum for most CEMT annual permits** as of 2022 onwards | 1× |
| Euro VI | Eligible for "Green" CEMT permits; enhanced access | **2×** (counts as 2 permits against quota) |
| "Super Green" (Euro VI + additional criteria) | Best access; some countries prioritise | 2× or 3× weighting |

**Key implication:** Operators with Euro VI vehicles effectively receive twice the transport capacity from the same quota allocation, incentivising fleet renewal.

Vehicle Euro standard must be certified on the CEMT permit itself; enforcement officers check vehicle registration/engine plate against declared standard.

## Annual Quota Allocation

The ITF Secretariat allocates permits to member countries each year based on:
- Historical transport volumes
- Country economic size and trade flows
- Political negotiations at ITF Council

### How Quotas Work

1. **ITF distributes** a set number of annual and monthly permits to each member country
2. **National authority/association distributes** permits to operators within that country
3. **Operators apply** to national body (see below) — applications typically open October–December for following year
4. **Exhaustion:** If a country's quota runs out mid-year, operators must resort to bilateral permits or alternative routes
5. **Unused permits:** Can sometimes be transferred back; varies by national rules

### National Distribution Authorities

| Country | Authority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SE (Sweden) | Transportstyrelsen (Swedish Transport Agency) | Via Transportföretagen for applications |
| DE (Germany) | Bundesamt für Logistik und Mobilität (BALM) / BGL | BGL distributes to members |
| FR (France) | DGITM (Direction générale des infrastructures) / FNTR | FNTR guides operators |
| PL (Poland) | GITD (Główny Inspektorat Transportu Drogowego) / ZMPD | High quota — major transit country |
| NL (Netherlands) | RDW / NLT | |
| GB (UK) | DVSA | Post-Brexit ECMT permits — very limited quota; apply early |
| TR (Turkey) | Ministry of Transport / TOBB | Turkey has large bilateral permit network separately |
| UA (Ukraine) | Ministry of Infrastructure / ASMAP-Ukraine | |

**UK post-Brexit note:** UK operators face extremely limited ECMT permit quotas for EU transport. Most UK-EU haulage now relies on bilateral agreements with individual EU states; UK operators should consult DVSA for current permit availability.

## The Permit Book — Practical Use

Each CEMT/ITF annual permit consists of a **permit book** with:

### Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Cover page** | Vehicle registration, operator details, issuing country, permit number, Euro class, validity dates |
| **Journey pages (journal)** | One row per journey; completed in advance or on day of departure |
| **Instructions pages** | Multi-language summary of rules |

### Journey Journal — What to Record

For **each loaded journey** the driver completes one journal row:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Date | Date of loading/departure |
| Country of loading | Country where goods taken over |
| Country of unloading | Destination country |
| Goods description | Brief description of cargo |
| Vehicle registration | Tractor + trailer |
| Driver signature | Required |

**Empty return journeys** are permitted without using a journal entry (CEMT rules generally allow empty running); verify national rules.

### Return of Permit Book

At the end of the validity year:
- Completed permit book **must be returned** to the issuing authority within the specified deadline (typically 31 January of following year)
- Non-return = loss of future quota allocation
- Authority checks journal to verify permit was used appropriately (not for cabotage, not transferred to another operator)

---

## Key Rules and Restrictions

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **No cabotage** | CEMT permits **do not allow cabotage** (domestic transport operations within a single foreign country); each journey must be international (loading and unloading in different countries) |
| **One permit per vehicle** | Each permit is valid for one specific vehicle (tractor); trailer may be changed |
| **Non-transferable** | Permits cannot be sold, lent, or transferred to other operators; doing so is grounds for exclusion |
| **Goods restrictions** | Generally no goods restriction; some bilateral agreements exclude certain goods (e.g., military, hazardous waste) |
| **Vehicle standard** | Vehicle must meet the Euro class stated on the permit throughout the year of validity |
| **Loaded journeys only** | In principle; empty return legs are generally tolerated but journal should note "empty" |

## CEMT vs. Community Licence vs. TIR

| Instrument | Purpose | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **EU Community Licence** | Access to intra-EU road freight market | All commercial transport within EU/EEA |
| **CEMT/ITF Permit** | Multilateral access beyond EU borders | Transport to/from non-EU ITF members |
| **Bilateral permit** | Country-pair access where quota available | Specific high-frequency non-EU routes |
| **TIR Carnet** | Customs transit guarantee (not market access) | Transit through non-EU countries; used alongside CEMT/bilateral |

A single cross-border operation to Turkey might require: EU Community Licence + CEMT or bilateral permit + TIR Carnet + Green Card.

## Common Issues

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Quota exhaustion** | Annual permits typically run out by Q3 in high-demand corridors (e.g., PL-TR); apply early and maintain buffer stock of short-term permits |
| **Mid-year Euro class requirement change** | Some countries have raised minimum Euro standard mid-year; operators with older fleets may find their permits invalid in certain states |
| **UK post-Brexit quota scarcity** | UK's ITF quota is very small; most UK-EU operators struggle; consider operating via EU-based subsidiary |
| **Wrong permit type used for route** | Using an annual permit for a route requiring a bilateral permit (or vice versa) can result in permit being confiscated at border |
| **Journal not completed** | Authorities may treat incomplete journal as misuse; permit may be revoked |
| **Vehicle change without re-registration** | Permit is vehicle-specific; if tractor is replaced, permit must be updated by issuing authority |

---

## Penalties

| Violation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Operating without valid permit (where required) | Vehicle detained at border; goods held; fine under national law; may require local guarantee payment |
| Using permit for cabotage operations | Permit confiscated; operator excluded from future quota allocation; fine |
| Falsifying journal entries | Criminal charge for document fraud; permanent ban from TIR/CEMT systems |
| Non-return of permit book | Exclusion from following year's quota allocation |
| Vehicle below stated Euro class | Permit treated as invalid; same consequences as no permit |
| UK post-Brexit: operating without permit | Immediate prohibition from EU territory; DVSA notified |
