## Overview

Sustainability has moved from voluntary to mandatory for many road transport operators. The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) requires large companies to report transport-related emissions from 2025, creating demand for per-shipment CO2 data from carriers. Simultaneously, operators face rising fuel costs, LEZ requirements, and customer pressure to adopt alternative fuels. This file covers eco-driving techniques, alternative fuel options, sustainability reporting obligations, and green certification schemes for transport.

## Eco-Driving — Fuel Saving Techniques

European Commission research confirms **10–15% fuel savings** are achievable through driving technique changes alone, with no capital cost:

| Technique | Fuel saving potential | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Anticipation (reading the road) | 3–5% | Reduce unnecessary braking; use engine braking |
| Gear optimisation (high gear, low RPM) | 2–4% | Most efficient: 1,200–1,500 RPM at cruise |
| Speed reduction (drive at 80 vs 90 km/h) | 5–10% | Aerodynamic drag increases with speed squared |
| Eliminate idling | 1–3% | Engine idle ≈ 0.8 litres/hour; 10-min shutdown if >5 min stationary |
| Tyre inflation (+PSI) | 0.5–1% per PSI | Under-inflated tyres increase rolling resistance |
| Route optimisation | 5–15% | Avoiding congestion, steep gradients, detours |
| Vehicle speed limiter at 85 km/h | 5–8% vs 90 km/h limit | Optimal aerodynamic efficiency point for semi-trailers |

**Total achievable saving (combining techniques):** 15–25% vs. unaware driver

### Swedish FarKost Eco-Driving Training

**FarKost** is a Swedish eco-driving training programme for professional HGV drivers, developed in cooperation with Trafikverket and transport organisations:
- Theory + practical training sessions
- Covers: anticipation, gear technique, tyre pressure, route planning
- Not mandatory but widely used in SE; often required by major shippers in supplier codes of conduct
- Transportföretagen and Trafikverket jointly promote the programme

## Alternative Fuels

### HVO (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| CO2 reduction (well-to-wheel) | Up to **90%** vs fossil diesel |
| Compatibility | Drop-in replacement for diesel; no engine modification needed |
| Energy density | ~93% of diesel (slightly lower fuel economy) |
| Available in SE | Widely available; Preem, Circle K; common at truck stops |
| Available in DE/NL | Growing; available at major truck stops |
| Cost premium | 10–30% above diesel (varies by market) |
| EN 15940 standard | Pure HVO certified under EN 15940 ("paraffinic diesel") |
| Blending | Can blend up to B100 (pure HVO) or mixed with diesel |
| Recommended for | Immediate CO2 reduction without fleet investment |

### LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| CO2 reduction vs diesel | 10–20% (fossil LNG); up to 70% (bio-LNG) |
| Range | 800–1,000 km (suitable for long-haul) |
| Infrastructure | ~500 LNG truck fuelling stations in EU (mainly DE, NL, SE, ES, IT, FR) |
| Engine type | Dedicated LNG heavy truck engines (Volvo, Scania, IVECO) |
| Temperature | Cryogenic storage at −162°C; requires specialised tank (insulated) |
| Cost | Often cheaper than diesel per energy unit where available |
| Best for | Long-distance routes with planned LNG stop availability |

### BEV (Battery Electric Vehicle)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| CO2 reduction | 0% tailpipe; grid-dependent (SE grid ~98% renewable — very low lifecycle) |
| Current range | 200–500 km (improving rapidly); Volvo FH Electric: ~300 km |
| Charging | DC fast charging (150–350 kW); overnight depot charging (AC 22–44 kW) |
| Charging time | 1.5–4 hours DC; 6–10 hours AC |
| Infrastructure | Growing — EU requires HGV charging every 120 km on TEN-T core by 2030 |
| Payload | Battery weight (typically 1,500–3,000 kg) reduces payload vs. diesel |
| +2t weight allowance | EU Directive 2015/719 allows +2t GVW for zero-emission vehicles |
| Best for | Urban/regional distribution (≤300 km daily); depot-to-depot operations |

### FCEV (Fuel Cell Electric — Hydrogen)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| CO2 reduction | 0% tailpipe; green hydrogen (electrolysis) = very low lifecycle |
| Range | 500–1,000 km (competitive with diesel) |
| Refuelling time | ~15–20 minutes |
| Infrastructure | Very limited (DE, NL, SE have some H2 truck stations) |
| Cost | Currently 3–5× higher than diesel per km |
| Technology | Daimler GenH2, Nikola, Hyundai XCIENT |
| Best for | Future long-haul (infrastructure investment in progress) |

## CSRD — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive

**EU Directive 2022/2464 (CSRD)** — mandatory sustainability reporting, phased in by company size:

| Phase | Companies | Reporting from |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Large public-interest entities (>500 employees) | Financial year **2024** (reports published 2025) |
| Phase 2 | Large companies (>250 employees OR >€40M turnover OR >€20M assets) | Financial year **2025** (reports published 2026) |
| Phase 3 | Listed SMEs (optional until 2028) | Financial year **2026** |

**Transport implications of CSRD:**
- **Scope 3 emissions** include transport and distribution (Category 4: upstream; Category 9: downstream)
- Large shippers must report CO2 emissions from their supply chain — they will ask carriers for **per-shipment CO2 data**
- Carriers should be able to provide: kg CO2 per tonne-km or per shipment
- **ISO 14083 (2023):** New international standard for quantifying and reporting GHG emissions from transport — becoming the standard methodology

**For transport operators:** Even if not subject to CSRD directly, customers subject to CSRD will require CO2 data from you. Implement:
- Fuel consumption tracking per vehicle/route
- Calculate CO2 per shipment (fuel consumed × emission factor)
- Provide to customers in standard format

## Penalties

No direct penalties for failing to meet eco-driving targets (voluntary). However:

| Violation | Consequence | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| CSRD reporting failure or greenwashing | Regulatory fine; reputational damage | CSRD Directive 2022/2464; national implementation |
| Operating non-compliant vehicle in LEZ | LEZ fines (see environmental-zones.md) | Local authority regulations |
| Euro VII non-compliance (from 2027) | Cannot register/sell new non-compliant vehicles | EU Regulation 2024/1257 |
