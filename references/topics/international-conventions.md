## Overview

Road transport is governed by a layered system of international conventions, EU regulations, and national law. No single instrument covers everything; instead, a web of agreements — each administered by different international bodies, each with different signatory states — determines which rules apply to any given journey or operation.

Understanding this layered system is essential for compliance: a vehicle crossing from Sweden to Turkey may simultaneously be subject to EU 561/2006 (on the EU portion), AETR (on the non-EU portion), CMR (for the freight contract), ADR (if carrying dangerous goods), and TIR (for customs transit). Each layer must be satisfied independently.

This file is the **navigational index** of the knowledge base. Use it to identify which convention governs a given topic, then follow the links to the relevant detail files.

## How Conventions Layer

For any given international road transport journey, multiple conventions apply simultaneously. The applicable rules depend on several key questions:

### Decision Tree: Which Rules Apply?

```
START: Is the vehicle performing road transport? (>3.5t GVW or bus)
│
├─ NO → National light-vehicle rules only; EU 561/2006 / AETR / CMR do not apply
│
└─ YES
    │
    ├─ Is the journey DOMESTIC only?
    │   ├─ YES → National driving/rest rules (often mirroring EU 561/2006)
    │   │         National carrier liability law
    │   │         ADR applies if dangerous goods carried domestically
    │   │
    │   └─ NO → International journey
    │
    ├─ Does the journey take place ENTIRELY within EU/EEA territory?
    │   ├─ YES → EU Regulation 561/2006 applies (driving/rest)
    │   │         AETR does NOT apply (EU 561/2006 takes precedence)
    │   │         CMR applies if international freight contract
    │   │         ADR applies if dangerous goods
    │   │         EU cabotage rules (1072/2009) if domestic operations in another EU state
    │   │
    │   └─ NO → Journey passes through or to a non-EU/EEA AETR state
    │
    └─ Does the route include a non-EU/EEA AETR contracting state?
        ├─ YES → AETR applies for the ENTIRE journey
        │         (EU 561/2006 does not apply alongside AETR; AETR governs throughout)
        │         CMR applies if both origin and destination are CMR signatories
        │         ADR or IMDG/RID interfaces apply depending on modal legs
        │         TIR may apply for customs transit through third countries
        │         CEMT/ITF permit required if non-EU/EEA bilateral quota applies
        │
        └─ NO (destination is non-signatory) → Check bilateral transport agreements
                                                National rules of transit countries apply
                                                CMR may still apply if both states are parties
```

### Key Principle: AETR vs EU 561/2006

This is the most common source of confusion:

- **EU 561/2006** governs driving/rest time when the **entire journey** remains within EU/EEA territory.
- **AETR** governs driving/rest time when **any part of the journey** passes through or to a non-EU/EEA AETR contracting state (e.g., Turkey, Ukraine, Belarus, Russia, Kazakhstan).
- When AETR applies, it applies to the **whole journey** — not just the non-EU portion.
- The rules are similar but not identical; AETR has some stricter requirements in specific areas.

### Key Principle: Convention vs EU Law

EU regulations are directly applicable in all EU member states and override conflicting national law. EU regulations are also incorporated into EEA law (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) via the EEA Agreement. International conventions (AETR, CMR, ADR, etc.) may be incorporated by reference into EU law or national law; where they conflict, EU law generally prevails within EU territory unless the convention is specifically preserved.

## Penalties

Penalties for non-compliance with conventions are imposed by national enforcement authorities under national law — international conventions themselves rarely specify penalty amounts. Key enforcement mechanisms:

| Convention | Enforcement route | Penalty range |
|---|---|---|
| AETR | National transport police / inspection bodies | Per country; see references/aetr/sanctions.md |
| ADR | National dangerous goods authorities | Per country; can include vehicle immobilisation |
| CMR | Civil courts (carrier liability claims) | Up to 8.33 SDR per kg of damaged/lost goods |
| TIR | Customs authorities | Guarantee chain activated; carnet holder liability |
| EU 561/2006 | National enforcement (police, transport inspectors) | Per country; see references/countries/{country}/penalties.md |
| EU 1072/2009 | National transport authorities | Per country; see references/topics/cabotage-enforcement-by-country.md |
